# ABG Interpretation for Nurses: ROME, TIC TAC TOE & NCLEX Practice

**Published:** 2026-08-11 | **Reading Time:** 10 min | **NCLEX Category:** Physiological Integrity / Reduction of Risk Potential

---

## Quick Summary

Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) interpretation is a high-yield NCLEX skill. You need to determine three things:

1. **Is the pH acidic or alkalotic?**
2. **Is the primary cause respiratory or metabolic?**
3. **Is the body compensating?**

| Value | Normal Range | Acidosis | Alkalosis |
|-------|-------------|----------|-----------|
| **pH** | 7.35 – 7.45 | < 7.35 | > 7.45 |
| **PaCO₂** | 35 – 45 mmHg | > 45 (retained CO₂) | < 35 (blown off CO₂) |
| **HCO₃⁻** | 22 – 26 mEq/L | < 22 (lost bicarb) | > 26 (retained bicarb) |

**Key Principle:** PaCO₂ is the respiratory component. HCO₃⁻ is the metabolic component. They move in opposite directions to compensate.

---

## The Three-Step Method (Fast, Works Every Time)

### Step 1: Look at the pH

| pH | Interpretation |
|----|---------------|
| < 7.35 | Acidosis |
| 7.35 – 7.45 | Normal or compensated |
| > 7.45 | Alkalosis |

### Step 2: Determine the Primary Cause

Look at PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻. **Whichever matches the pH direction is the primary cause.**

| If pH shows... | And PaCO₂ is... | And HCO₃⁻ is... | Primary Problem |
|---------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| Acidosis | ↑ High | Normal | Respiratory acidosis |
| Acidosis | Normal | ↓ Low | Metabolic acidosis |
| Alkalosis | ↓ Low | Normal | Respiratory alkalosis |
| Alkalosis | Normal | ↑ High | Metabolic alkalosis |

### Step 3: Check for Compensation

| Pattern | Interpretation |
|---------|---------------|
| One value abnormal, other normal | Uncompensated |
| Both abnormal, pH still abnormal | Partially compensated |
| Both abnormal, pH normal | Fully compensated |

## Mnemonic 1: ROME

> **R**espiratory **O**pposite, **M**etabolic **E**qual

| Problem | pH Direction | PaCO₂ Direction | Relationship |
|---------|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| Respiratory Acidosis | ↓ | ↑ | **Opposite** |
| Respiratory Alkalosis | ↑ | ↓ | **Opposite** |
| Metabolic Acidosis | ↓ | ↓ | **Equal** |
| Metabolic Alkalosis | ↑ | ↑ | **Equal** |

**How to use ROME:**
1. Look at pH → know if it's acidosis or alkalosis
2. Look at PaCO₂
3. Ask: "Is PaCO₂ moving opposite or equal to pH?"
   - Opposite = Respiratory cause
   - Equal = Metabolic cause

## Mnemonic 2: TIC TAC TOE

Draw a tic-tac-toe grid. Place pH, PaCO₂, and HCO₃⁻ in their acid/alkaline columns:

```
        ACID          NORMAL          ALKALINE
      ┌────────┬─────────────┬──────────────┐
      │  pH ↓  │   pH 7.35-7.45   │   pH ↑   │
      │ PaCO₂ ↑│                │ PaCO₂ ↓  │
      │ HCO₃⁻ ↓│                │ HCO₃⁻ ↑  │
      └────────┴─────────────┴──────────────┘
```

**How to use TIC TAC TOE:**
1. Place pH in the appropriate column (acid or alkaline)
2. Place PaCO₂ in its column
3. Place HCO₃⁻ in its column
4. The one that **matches pH's column** is the primary cause
5. If all three are in the same row → mixed disorder (advanced)

## The Four Primary ABG Disorders

### 1. Respiratory Acidosis

**Core Problem:** Hypoventilation → CO₂ retention → carbonic acid buildup

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| pH | < 7.35 |
| PaCO₂ | > 45 mmHg |
| HCO₃⁻ | Normal (uncompensated) or > 26 (compensated) |

**Common Causes:**
- COPD, asthma, pneumonia
- Opioid overdose / sedation
- Chest wall trauma / flail chest
- Neuromuscular disorders (ALS, myasthenia gravis)
- Sleep apnea

**Nursing Interventions:**
- Improve ventilation: positioning, deep breathing, incentive spirometry
- Treat underlying cause (bronchodilators, naloxone for opioid overdose)
- Oxygen cautiously (COPD patients may rely on hypoxic drive)
- Prepare for BiPAP or intubation if severe

**NCLEX Trap:** Never give high-flow O₂ to a COPD patient without caution. These patients rely on low O₂ levels to stimulate breathing. High O₂ can suppress their respiratory drive.

---

### 2. Respiratory Alkalosis

**Core Problem:** Hyperventilation → CO₂ blown off → alkalosis

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| pH | > 7.45 |
| PaCO₂ | < 35 mmHg |
| HCO₃⁻ | Normal (uncompensated) or < 22 (compensated) |

**Common Causes:**
- Anxiety / panic attacks (most common)
- Pain
- Fever
- Early sepsis
- High altitude
- Mechanical ventilation (over-ventilated)

**Nursing Interventions:**
- Treat underlying cause (pain management, anti-anxiety)
- Breathing techniques: rebreathing into paper bag (controversial, rarely used now)
- If mechanically ventilated, decrease rate or tidal volume
- Correct electrolyte imbalances (hypokalemia, hypocalcemia common)

---

### 3. Metabolic Acidosis

**Core Problem:** Loss of bicarbonate OR accumulation of acid

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| pH | < 7.35 |
| HCO₃⁻ | < 22 mEq/L |
| PaCO₂ | Normal (uncompensated) or < 35 (compensated) |

**Common Causes:**
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
- Lactic acidosis (shock, sepsis)
- Renal failure (can't excrete acid)
- Severe diarrhea (loss of bicarbonate)
- Salicylate overdose (aspirin)
- Methanol or ethylene glycol poisoning

**Nursing Interventions:**
- Treat underlying cause (insulin for DKA, fluids for shock)
- Monitor K⁺ closely — acidosis drives K⁺ out of cells → hyperkalemia
- Sodium bicarbonate only if pH < 7.1 and hemodynamically unstable
- Calculate anion gap to identify cause:
  - **Anion Gap = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)**
  - Normal: 8–12 mEq/L
  - High anion gap: DKA, lactic acidosis, renal failure, toxins
  - Normal anion gap: diarrhea, renal tubular acidosis

**NCLEX Pearl:** In metabolic acidosis, always check potassium. Acidosis causes extracellular shifting of K⁺ → **hyperkalemia**. When you correct the acidosis, K⁺ shifts back into cells → watch for **hypokalemia**.

---

### 4. Metabolic Alkalosis

**Core Problem:** Loss of acid OR gain of bicarbonate

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| pH | > 7.45 |
| HCO₃⁻ | > 26 mEq/L |
| PaCO₂ | Normal (uncompensated) or > 45 (compensated) |

**Common Causes:**
- Prolonged vomiting or NG suction (loss of HCl)
- Excessive diuretic use (loss of K⁺ and H⁺)
- Excessive antacid use
- Cushing's syndrome / steroid use
- Severe dehydration (contraction alkalosis)

**Nursing Interventions:**
- Replace volume and potassium
- Treat underlying cause (antiemetics, adjust diuretics)
- If NG suctioning, irrigate with normal saline, not water
- Monitor for hypokalemia and hypocalcemia

## Compensation Patterns

| Disorder | Uncompensated | Partially Compensated | Fully Compensated |
|----------|--------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Respiratory Acidosis** | pH ↓, PaCO₂ ↑, HCO₃⁻ normal | pH ↓, PaCO₂ ↑, HCO₃⁻ ↑ | pH normal, PaCO₂ ↑, HCO₃⁻ ↑ |
| **Respiratory Alkalosis** | pH ↑, PaCO₂ ↓, HCO₃⁻ normal | pH ↑, PaCO₂ ↓, HCO₃⁻ ↓ | pH normal, PaCO₂ ↓, HCO₃⁻ ↓ |
| **Metabolic Acidosis** | pH ↓, HCO₃⁻ ↓, PaCO₂ normal | pH ↓, HCO₃⁻ ↓, PaCO₂ ↓ | pH normal, HCO₃⁻ ↓, PaCO₂ ↓ |
| **Metabolic Alkalosis** | pH ↑, HCO₃⁻ ↑, PaCO₂ normal | pH ↑, HCO₃⁻ ↑, PaCO₂ ↑ | pH normal, HCO₃⁻ ↑, PaCO₂ ↑ |

**Key Point:** The body tries to normalize pH. Lungs compensate for metabolic problems by changing PaCO₂. Kidneys compensate for respiratory problems by changing HCO₃⁻.

## Oxygenation Assessment

Don't forget to evaluate oxygenation:

| Parameter | Normal | Hypoxemia |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| PaO₂ | 80–100 mmHg | < 80 mmHg |
| SaO₂ | > 95% | < 90% |

**Causes of low PaO₂:**
- V/Q mismatch (pneumonia, pulmonary edema)
- Shunt (atelectasis, ARDS)
- Hypoventilation
- Low FiO₂ (high altitude)

**Nursing Interventions for Hypoxemia:**
1. Increase FiO₂ (nasal cannula → simple mask → NRB → intubation)
2. Positioning: high-Fowler's, prone if ARDS
3. Treat underlying cause
4. Monitor for CO₂ retention in COPD patients

## NCLEX Practice Questions

### Question 1
A patient's ABG results: pH 7.30, PaCO₂ 55 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 24 mEq/L. The nurse interprets this as:

A. Metabolic acidosis, uncompensated  
B. Respiratory acidosis, uncompensated  
C. Metabolic acidosis, partially compensated  
D. Respiratory acidosis, partially compensated

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Respiratory acidosis, uncompensated**

- pH 7.30 = acidosis
- PaCO₂ 55 = high (retained CO₂) → respiratory cause
- HCO₃⁻ 24 = normal → no metabolic compensation yet
- ROME: pH is ↓, PaCO₂ is ↑ → **opposite** → respiratory

</details>

### Question 2
A patient with DKA has the following ABG: pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 28 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 14 mEq/L. The nurse recognizes this as:

A. Respiratory acidosis with metabolic compensation  
B. Metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation  
C. Respiratory alkalosis with metabolic compensation  
D. Metabolic alkalosis with respiratory compensation

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation**

- pH 7.25 = acidosis
- HCO₃⁻ 14 = low → metabolic cause
- PaCO₂ 28 = low → lungs are compensating by blowing off CO₂
- ROME: pH is ↓, HCO₃⁻ is ↓ → **equal** → metabolic
- PaCO₂ is also abnormal → partially compensated

</details>

### Question 3
A patient has been hyperventilating due to anxiety. ABG shows: pH 7.50, PaCO₂ 28 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 24 mEq/L. The priority nursing intervention is:

A. Administer sodium bicarbonate  
B. Have the patient breathe into a paper bag  
C. Address the underlying anxiety  
D. Prepare for intubation

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**C. Address the underlying anxiety**

- pH 7.50 = alkalosis
- PaCO₂ 28 = low → respiratory alkalosis from hyperventilation
- This is uncompensated (HCO₃⁻ normal)
- Priority: treat the cause (anxiety, pain)
- Paper bag breathing is controversial and rarely used
- Bicarbonate would worsen alkalosis
- Intubation is not indicated

</details>

### Question 4 (NGN-Style)
A patient with COPD is admitted with pneumonia. ABG: pH 7.32, PaCO₂ 62 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 30 mEq/L, PaO₂ 58 mmHg. The patient is on 2L NC.

**Which nursing action is most appropriate?**

A. Increase O₂ to 6L NC  
B. Maintain current O₂ and prepare for BiPAP  
C. Administer sodium bicarbonate IV  
D. Encourage vigorous deep breathing exercises

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Maintain current O₂ and prepare for BiPAP**

- pH 7.32 + PaCO₂ 62 = respiratory acidosis
- HCO₃⁻ 30 = kidneys have compensated (chronic COPD)
- This is compensated respiratory acidosis
- PaO₂ 58 = hypoxemic
- **Don't increase O₂ aggressively** — COPD patients rely on hypoxic drive
- BiPAP will help with CO₂ retention and work of breathing
- Bicarbonate is not indicated (pH > 7.1)
- Deep breathing may tire the patient further

</details>
