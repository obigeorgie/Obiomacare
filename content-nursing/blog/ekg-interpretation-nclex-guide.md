# EKG Interpretation for Nurses: Rhythm Recognition & NCLEX Guide

**Published:** 2026-08-11 | **Reading Time:** 12 min | **NCLEX Category:** Physiological Integrity / Reduction of Risk Potential

---

## Quick Summary

EKG interpretation is a high-yield NCLEX skill. You don't need to be a cardiologist — you need to recognize **lethal rhythms**, **ischemia patterns**, and **electrolyte effects** on the EKG. Most NCLEX questions test whether you know what's an emergency and what to do first.

| Rhythm | Rate | Key Feature | Action |
|--------|------|-------------|--------|
| **Normal Sinus** | 60–100 | Regular, P before each QRS | Monitor |
| **Sinus Bradycardia** | < 60 | Regular, P before each QRS | Atropine if symptomatic |
| **Sinus Tachycardia** | > 100 | Regular, P before each QRS | Treat cause |
| **A-Fib** | Irregular | No P waves, irregular R-R | Anticoagulation, rate control |
| **A-Flutter** | Regular/irregular | Sawtooth flutter waves | Rate control, possible cardioversion |
| **V-Tach** | 100–250 | Wide QRS, no P waves | Pulse? → Defibrillate/CPR |
| **V-Fib** | Chaotic | No organized rhythm | Defibrillate immediately |
| **Asystole** | None | Flat line | CPR, epinephrine, find cause |
| **PEA** | None | Electrical activity, no pulse | CPR, H's and T's |

---

## The One Thing to Remember

> **In cardiac emergencies, treat the patient, not the monitor.** A patient talking to you with a rhythm of V-tach on the monitor is very different from an unresponsive patient with the same rhythm. Check pulses before acting.

## EKG Basics: What Each Wave Means

### The Cardiac Cycle on Paper

```
P wave → PR segment → QRS complex → ST segment → T wave → U wave (optional)
```

| Wave/Segment | Represents | Normal |
|--------------|-----------|--------|
| **P wave** | Atrial depolarization (contraction) | < 0.12 sec, < 2.5 mm height |
| **PR interval** | Conduction through AV node | 0.12–0.20 sec (3–5 small boxes) |
| **QRS complex** | Ventricular depolarization | < 0.12 sec (3 small boxes) |
| **ST segment** | Early ventricular repolarization | Isoelectric (flat) |
| **T wave** | Ventricular repolarization | Upright in most leads |
| **QT interval** | Total ventricular activity | < 0.44 sec (varies with heart rate) |

### Measuring Time and Voltage
- **Small box:** 1 mm = 0.04 seconds
- **Large box:** 5 mm = 0.20 seconds
- **Vertical:** 1 mm = 0.1 mV

### Quick Rate Calculation
**300 method:** Count the number of large boxes between R waves. Divide 300 by that number.
- 1 large box = 300 bpm
- 2 large boxes = 150 bpm
- 3 large boxes = 100 bpm
- 4 large boxes = 75 bpm
- 5 large boxes = 60 bpm
- 6 large boxes = 50 bpm

## Systematic Rhythm Interpretation (5 Steps)

Use this for every strip:

### Step 1: Rate
- Normal: 60–100 bpm
- Bradycardia: < 60 bpm
- Tachycardia: > 100 bpm

### Step 2: Rhythm
- Regular? (consistent R-R intervals)
- Irregularly irregular? (A-fib)
- Regularly irregular? (patterns like grouped beating)

### Step 3: P Waves
- Is there a P wave before every QRS?
- Are P waves upright in lead II? (sinus rhythm)
- Are P waves present at all? (A-fib has no P waves)

### Step 4: PR Interval
- Normal: 0.12–0.20 seconds
- Short PR: < 0.12 sec (pre-excitation, junctional)
- Long PR: > 0.20 sec (first-degree AV block)
- Variable PR: Wenckebach (second-degree type I)

### Step 5: QRS Complex
- Narrow: < 0.12 seconds (supraventricular origin)
- Wide: > 0.12 seconds (ventricular origin or aberrant conduction)

## Normal Sinus Rhythm (NSR)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | 60–100 bpm |
| Rhythm | Regular |
| P waves | Present before each QRS, upright in lead II |
| PR interval | 0.12–0.20 sec |
| QRS | Narrow (< 0.12 sec) |

**Nursing Action:** None needed. This is the goal rhythm.

---

## Sinus Bradycardia

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | < 60 bpm |
| Rhythm | Regular |
| P waves | Present before each QRS |
| PR interval | Normal |
| QRS | Narrow |

**Causes:**
- Athletic heart (normal in athletes)
- Medications: beta-blockers, digoxin, calcium channel blockers
- Increased intracranial pressure
- Hypothyroidism
- Hypothermia
- Sleep
- Vagal stimulation (Valsalva, suctioning)

**Nursing Care:**
- **Asymptomatic:** Monitor
- **Symptomatic** (hypotension, dizziness, syncope, chest pain, SOB):
  1. **Atropine 0.5 mg IV** (repeat every 3–5 min, max 3 mg)
  2. **Transcutaneous pacing** if atropine ineffective
  3. **Dopamine or epinephrine infusion**

**NCLEX Trap:** Don't treat asymptomatic bradycardia. Many athletes have resting HR of 40–50 with no issues.

---

## Sinus Tachycardia

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | > 100 bpm |
| Rhythm | Regular |
| P waves | Present before each QRS |
| PR interval | Normal |
| QRS | Narrow |

**Causes:**
- Pain, anxiety, fever
- Hypovolemia, anemia
- Heart failure
- Pulmonary embolism
- Medications: albuterol, epinephrine, caffeine
- Exercise (normal)

**Nursing Care:**
- Treat the underlying cause (pain relief, fluids, cooling)
- Monitor for ischemia if persistent (chest pain, ST changes)
- Beta-blockers if indicated

**NCLEX Point:** Sinus tachycardia is a **symptom**, not a diagnosis. Find and treat the cause.

---

## Atrial Fibrillation (A-Fib)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Variable (controlled vs. rapid ventricular response) |
| Rhythm | Irregularly irregular |
| P waves | **Absent** — replaced by fibrillatory waves |
| PR interval | Not measurable |
| QRS | Narrow |

**Clinical Concerns:**
- **Irregular rhythm** → ineffective atrial contraction → blood stasis → **clot formation**
- **Risk of stroke:** 5× higher than normal
- **Rapid ventricular response** → heart failure, ischemia

**Nursing Care:**
1. **Assess hemodynamic stability**
   - Stable: rate control (beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin)
   - Unstable: **synchronized cardioversion**
2. **Anticoagulation assessment**
   - CHA₂DS₂-VASc score for stroke risk
   - Warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban if indicated
3. **Monitor for signs of stroke** (sudden weakness, aphasia)

**NCLEX Priority:** A-fib with **RVR (rapid ventricular response)** and hypotension = **synchronized cardioversion** (not defibrillation — the patient has a pulse).

---

## Atrial Flutter

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Atrial rate 250–350 bpm; ventricular rate varies |
| Rhythm | Regular or irregular |
| P waves | **Sawtooth flutter waves** (F waves) |
| PR interval | Not measurable |
| QRS | Narrow |

**Nursing Care:**
- Similar to A-fib: rate control, anticoagulation
- Cardioversion if unstable
- Catheter ablation for recurrent cases

---

## Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Underlying rate + premature beats |
| Rhythm | Irregular |
| P waves | None before PVC |
| QRS | **Wide and bizarre** (> 0.12 sec) |

**Patterns:**
- **Unifocal:** All PVCs look the same (one irritable focus)
- **Multifocal:** PVCs look different (multiple irritable foci — more dangerous)
- **Bigeminy:** Every other beat is a PVC
- **Trigeminy:** Every third beat is a PVC
- **Couplet:** Two PVCs in a row
- **Triplets (Salvo):** Three PVCs in a row = short run of V-tach

**When to Worry:**
- > 6 PVCs per minute
- Multifocal PVCs
- Couplets or triplets
- R-on-T phenomenon (PVC falls on T wave of preceding beat)

**Nursing Care:**
- Check electrolytes (especially K⁺ and Mg²⁺)
- Check for hypoxia
- Assess medication levels (digoxin toxicity)
- Treat if symptomatic or dangerous patterns

---

## Ventricular Tachycardia (V-Tach)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | 100–250 bpm |
| Rhythm | Usually regular |
| P waves | Absent or dissociated |
| QRS | **Wide** (> 0.12 sec), bizarre |

**Two Types:**
1. **Monomorphic V-tach:** All QRS look the same
2. **Polymorphic V-tach:** QRS change shape (Torsades de Pointes)

**Nursing Care — The Decision Tree:**

```
Is there a pulse?
├── YES (Pulse present)
│   ├── Stable? → Amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min
│   └── Unstable? (hypotension, altered mental status, signs of shock)
│       → Synchronized cardioversion
└── NO (Pulseless)
    → Defibrillate immediately (unsynchronized shock)
    → CPR
    → Epinephrine 1 mg IV every 3–5 min
    → Amiodarone 300 mg IV push
```

**Critical NCLEX Point:**
> **V-tach with a pulse = cardioversion. V-tach without a pulse = defibrillation.**
> The energy delivery is different. Cardioversion is synchronized (avoiding T wave). Defibrillation is unsynchronized.

---

## Ventricular Fibrillation (V-Fib)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Chaotic, no organized rate |
| Rhythm | Chaotic, no pattern |
| P waves | Absent |
| QRS | No identifiable QRS — chaotic waves |

**This is a cardiac arrest rhythm. The heart quivers instead of pumping.**

**Nursing Care (Immediate):**
1. **Defibrillate immediately** (200 J biphasic)
2. **Begin CPR** if no pulse
3. **Epinephrine 1 mg IV** every 3–5 min
4. **Amiodarone 300 mg IV** after 3rd shock
5. **Continue CPR** between shocks

**Defibrillation vs. Cardioversion:**
| Feature | Defibrillation | Cardioversion |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| Rhythm | V-fib, pulseless V-tach | A-fib, A-flutter, V-tach with pulse |
| Synchronization | No (unsynchronized) | Yes (synchronized to R wave) |
| Pulse | Absent | Present |
| Energy | 200–360 J | 50–200 J |

---

## Asystole

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | None |
| Rhythm | Flat line |
| P waves | Absent |
| QRS | Absent |

**"Flat line" = no electrical activity.**

**Nursing Care:**
1. **Confirm it's real** — check leads, gain, different leads
2. **CPR immediately**
3. **Epinephrine 1 mg IV** every 3–5 min
4. **Find and treat cause (H's and T's)**
5. **DO NOT shock asystole** — shocking a flat line is not effective

---

## Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Electrical activity present |
| Rhythm | Any organized rhythm |
| P waves | May be present |
| QRS | May be present |
| **Key Feature** | **No pulse despite electrical activity** |

**This means the heart has electrical activity but isn't mechanically contracting.**

**Nursing Care:**
1. **CPR immediately**
2. **Epinephrine 1 mg IV** every 3–5 min
3. **Find and treat the cause (H's and T's)**

---

## The H's and T's (Causes of PEA and Asystole)

| H's | T's |
|-----|-----|
| **H**ypovolemia | **T**ension pneumothorax |
| **H**ypoxia | **T**amponade (cardiac) |
| **H**ydrogen ion (acidosis) | **T**oxins |
| **H**ypo-/Hyperkalemia | **T**hrombosis (pulmonary — PE) |
| **H**ypothermia | **T**hrombosis (coronary — MI) |
| | **T**rauma |

**Mnemonic: "Help Me, Tom"**

## Heart Blocks

### First-Degree AV Block

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Normal |
| Rhythm | Regular |
| P waves | Present before each QRS |
| PR interval | **> 0.20 seconds** (prolonged) |
| QRS | Narrow |

**Nursing Care:**
- Monitor
- Usually benign
- May progress to higher-degree blocks

### Second-Degree AV Block Type I (Wenckebach)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Variable |
| Rhythm | Irregular |
| P waves | Present |
| PR interval | **Progressively lengthens until QRS drops** |
| QRS | Narrow |

**Pattern:** "Longer, longer, longer, drop — then you've got a Wenckebach"

**Nursing Care:**
- Usually stable
- Monitor for progression to Type II
- Atropine if symptomatic

### Second-Degree AV Block Type II

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Variable |
| Rhythm | Regular or irregular |
| P waves | Present |
| PR interval | **Constant** (normal or prolonged) |
| QRS | May be wide |
| Key Feature | **QRS drops without warning** |

**Nursing Care:**
- **More dangerous than Type I**
- Can progress to third-degree block suddenly
- **Transcutaneous pacing** ready
- May need permanent pacemaker

### Third-Degree AV Block (Complete Heart Block)

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Rate | Atrial rate normal; ventricular rate 20–60 |
| Rhythm | Regular (both atria and ventricles beat regularly but independently) |
| P waves | Present but **no relationship to QRS** (AV dissociation) |
| PR interval | Variable |
| QRS | Usually wide |

**The atria and ventricles are completely disconnected. No impulses pass through the AV node.**

**Nursing Care:**
- **Symptomatic bradycardia** — treat per ACLS
- **Transcutaneous pacing** if unstable
- **Permanent pacemaker** usually needed
- Monitor for progression from lesser blocks

## EKG Changes in Electrolyte Imbalances

| Electrolyte | EKG Change | Clinical Significance |
|-------------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Hyperkalemia** | Peaked T waves → widened QRS → sine wave → V-fib/asystole | **Medical emergency** |
| **Hypokalemia** | Flat T waves, U waves, ST depression | Arrhythmia risk |
| **Hypercalcemia** | Short QT interval | Bradycardia, heart block |
| **Hypocalcemia** | Prolonged QT interval | Torsades de Pointes risk |
| **Hypermagnesemia** | Prolonged PR, widened QRS, heart block | Respiratory/cardiac arrest |
| **Hypomagnesemia** | Prolonged QT, Torsades de Pointes | Cardiac arrest |

**Critical NCLEX Point:**
> **Hyperkalemia is the electrolyte emergency you must recognize on EKG.** Peaked T waves are the first sign. If untreated, it progresses to sine wave pattern and then V-fib. Treatment: calcium gluconate (stabilizes myocardium), insulin + glucose (shifts K⁺ into cells), Kayexalate.

## EKG Changes in Acute Coronary Syndrome

### ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI)

| Feature | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| ST segment | **Elevation** > 1 mm in 2+ contiguous leads |
| T waves | May be peaked (hyperacute) |
| Q waves | May develop later |

**Nursing Priority:**
- **Activate cath lab immediately** — goal door-to-balloon < 90 minutes
- MONA: Morphine, Oxygen, Nitroglycerin, Aspirin
- 12-lead EKG within 10 minutes of arrival

### Non-ST-Elevation MI (NSTEMI)

| Feature | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| ST segment | Depression or T wave inversion |
| Troponins | Elevated |

**Nursing Priority:**
- Anticoagulation
- Possible cardiac catheterization
- Not emergent cath lab activation (unless unstable)

### Unstable Angina

| Feature | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| ST/T changes | May be present |
| Troponins | Normal |

**Differentiation:** Symptoms at rest or with minimal exertion, but no myocardial necrosis (troponins normal).

## 12-Lead EKG Basics for Nurses

### Lead Groups and What They View

| Leads | Area of Heart Viewed |
|-------|---------------------|
| **II, III, aVF** | Inferior wall |
| **V1–V4** | Anterior wall |
| **I, aVL, V5–V6** | Lateral wall |
| **V1–V2** | Septal area |

### Localizing the MI

| ST Elevation In... | Infarction Location | Affected Coronary Artery |
|-------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| II, III, aVF | Inferior | Right coronary artery (RCA) |
| V1–V4 | Anterior | Left anterior descending (LAD) |
| I, aVL, V5–V6 | Lateral | Left circumflex (LCx) |
| V1–V2 + inferior leads | Right ventricular | RCA |

**NCLEX Tip:** Inferior MIs often involve the right ventricle. If you see inferior ST elevation, check V4R (right-sided lead). If V4R shows ST elevation = right ventricular infarct. **Avoid nitrates** — they cause hypotension in RV infarcts (preload dependent).

## NCLEX Practice Questions

### Question 1
The nurse is monitoring a patient's cardiac rhythm and notes a regular rhythm at 160 bpm with narrow QRS complexes. There are no visible P waves. The patient is alert with BP 110/72. What is the nurse's priority action?

A. Defibrillate immediately  
B. Prepare for synchronized cardioversion  
C. Administer adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push  
D. Continue monitoring and notify provider

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**C. Administer adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push**

This is likely supraventricular tachycardia (SVT). The narrow QRS + regular rhythm + rate ~150–250 suggests SVT. Adenosine is first-line for stable SVT. It causes transient AV block, often converting the rhythm to sinus. Must be given RAPID IV push followed by saline flush (half-life is seconds).

</details>

### Question 2
A patient in the ICU suddenly becomes unresponsive. The monitor shows chaotic, irregular waves with no identifiable QRS complexes. The nurse confirms no pulse. What is the priority action?

A. Synchronized cardioversion  
B. Defibrillation  
C. Administer atropine  
D. Begin transcutaneous pacing

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Defibrillation**

This is ventricular fibrillation. The patient is pulseless. Defibrillate immediately with 200 J biphasic. Begin CPR if no immediate response. Do NOT synchronize (there's no pulse to synchronize with). Do NOT give atropine (for bradycardia, not V-fib).

</details>

### Question 3 (NGN-Style)
A patient with chest pain has the following 12-lead EKG findings: ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF. ST elevation is also noted in lead V4R. The patient's BP is 78/50, HR 110, RR 24.

**Which action should the nurse take first?**

A. Administer nitroglycerin sublingual  
B. Give aspirin 325 mg chewable  
C. Begin IV fluid bolus  
D. Prepare for immediate synchronized cardioversion

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**C. Begin IV fluid bolus**

- ST elevation in II, III, aVF = inferior MI
- ST elevation in V4R = right ventricular involvement
- Right ventricular infarct = **preload dependent**
- BP 78/50 = hypotensive
- **Nitroglycerin is contraindicated** — it reduces preload and can cause severe hypotension in RV infarct
- Priority: **IV fluids to increase preload** and improve BP
- Aspirin is appropriate but not the FIRST priority when hypotensive

</details>

### Question 4
The nurse notes the patient's rhythm strip shows a P wave before each QRS, but the PR interval is consistently 0.24 seconds. The heart rate is 68 bpm. The patient is asymptomatic. The nurse should:

A. Prepare for transcutaneous pacing  
B. Notify the provider and continue monitoring  
C. Administer atropine 0.5 mg IV  
D. Begin CPR

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Notify the provider and continue monitoring**

This is first-degree AV block (PR > 0.20 sec). The patient is asymptomatic with a normal heart rate. First-degree AV block is usually benign and requires monitoring. No immediate intervention needed. Atropine and pacing are for symptomatic bradycardia. CPR is for cardiac arrest.

</details>
