# IV Therapy & Dosage Calculations: The Complete Nursing Guide

**Published:** 2026-08-11 | **Reading Time:** 10 min | **NCLEX Category:** Physiological Integrity / Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

---

## Quick Summary

Dosage calculations are a must-know for the NCLEX and clinical practice. Most questions test dimensional analysis, IV drip rates, weight-based dosing, and safe medication administration. The key: **set up your equation, cancel units, and verify your answer makes sense.**

| Calculation Type | Formula | Example |
|-----------------|---------|---------|
| **Tablet/Capsule** | Desired ÷ Stock = Dose | 500 mg ordered ÷ 250 mg/tab = 2 tabs |
| **Liquid Medication** | Desired ÷ Stock × Volume = Dose | 300 mg ordered ÷ 150 mg/5 mL = 10 mL |
| **IV Drip Rate (gtt/min)** | (Volume × Drop factor) ÷ Time | (1000 mL × 15 gtt/mL) ÷ 480 min = 31 gtt/min |
| **IV Flow Rate (mL/hr)** | Volume ÷ Time | 1000 mL ÷ 8 hr = 125 mL/hr |
| **Weight-based Dose** | Dose/kg × Weight = Total dose | 5 mg/kg × 70 kg = 350 mg |
| **BSA-based Dose** | Dose/m² × BSA = Total dose | 150 mg/m² × 1.8 m² = 270 mg |

---

## The One Thing to Remember

> **Always check your answer against the safe range.** If you calculate 100 tablets or 50 mL of morphine, something is wrong. A reasonable adult dose of most oral medications is 1–4 tablets or 5–30 mL of liquid.

## Dimensional Analysis: The Universal Method

Dimensional analysis (DA) works for every calculation type. Set up the equation so units cancel, leaving only the unit you need.

### The Setup

```
[What you want] = [What you have] × [Conversion factors]
```

Arrange conversion factors as fractions so unwanted units cancel.

### Example 1: Tablet Calculation

**Order:** Amoxicillin 750 mg PO TID  
**Available:** Amoxicillin 250 mg tablets

```
750 mg     1 tab
------- × ------- = 3 tablets
  1      250 mg
```

### Example 2: Liquid Medication

**Order:** Furosemide 40 mg PO daily  
**Available:** Furosemide 10 mg/mL oral solution

```
40 mg      1 mL
------ × ------- = 4 mL
  1      10 mg
```

### Example 3: Unit Conversion First

**Order:** Levothyroxine 0.15 mg PO daily  
**Available:** Levothyroxine 75 mcg tablets

```
0.15 mg    1000 mcg    1 tab
-------- × --------- × ------- = 2 tablets
   1        1 mg      75 mcg
```

## IV Drip Rate Calculations

### Drop Factor

| Tubing Type | Drop Factor (gtt/mL) | When Used |
|------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Macrodrip | 10, 15, or 20 gtt/mL | Standard adult infusions |
| Microdrip | 60 gtt/mL | Pediatric, precise measurements |

Always check the tubing packaging for the drop factor.

### Formula: Drops per Minute

```
Total volume (mL) × Drop factor (gtt/mL) ÷ Total time (minutes) = gtt/min
```

### Example 1: Standard Adult Drip

**Order:** 1000 mL LR over 8 hours  
**Tubing:** 15 gtt/mL

```
1000 mL × 15 gtt/mL ÷ 480 min = 31.25 → 31 gtt/min
```

### Example 2: Pediatric Microdrip

**Order:** 250 mL D5W over 6 hours  
**Tubing:** 60 gtt/mL (microdrip)

```
250 mL × 60 gtt/mL ÷ 360 min = 41.67 → 42 gtt/min
```

### Using an IV Pump (mL/hr)

Most hospitals use electronic pumps. The calculation is simpler:

```
Total volume (mL) ÷ Total time (hours) = mL/hr
```

**Example:** 500 mL NS over 4 hours

```
500 mL ÷ 4 hr = 125 mL/hr
```

## Weight-Based Dosing

### Step 1: Convert Weight to kg

```
lbs ÷ 2.2 = kg
```

**Example:** 154 lbs

```
154 ÷ 2.2 = 70 kg
```

### Step 2: Calculate Dose

```
Dose per kg × Weight in kg = Total dose
```

**Example:** Dopamine 5 mcg/kg/min for a 70 kg patient

```
5 mcg/kg/min × 70 kg = 350 mcg/min
```

### Step 3: Convert to mL/hr (for IV infusions)

**Order:** Dopamine 350 mcg/min  
**Available:** Dopamine 400 mg in 250 mL D5W

```
350 mcg    1 mg      250 mL    60 min
-------- × ------- × ------- × ------ = 13.125 mL/hr
  min      1000 mcg   400 mg     1 hr
```

**Round to nearest whole number: 13 mL/hr**

## Pediatric Dosing

### mg/kg Dosing

**Order:** Amoxicillin 45 mg/kg/day divided BID for a 22 lb child

```
Step 1: Convert weight
22 lb ÷ 2.2 = 10 kg

Step 2: Calculate daily dose
45 mg/kg/day × 10 kg = 450 mg/day

Step 3: Divide by frequency (BID = 2 doses)
450 mg ÷ 2 = 225 mg per dose
```

### Safe Dose Range (SDR)

Always check if your calculated dose is within the safe range.

**Example:** Acetaminophen SDR is 10–15 mg/kg/dose q4–6h for children

For a 15 kg child:
- Low: 10 mg/kg × 15 kg = 150 mg
- High: 15 mg/kg × 15 kg = 225 mg

An order of 300 mg would be **unsafe** (exceeds SDR).

## Reconstitution

Some medications come as powders that must be mixed with a diluent.

**Example:** Cefazolin 1 g IM

**Directions:** Add 2.5 mL sterile water for injection to yield 330 mg/mL

```
1000 mg     1 mL
-------- × ------- = 3.03 mL → 3 mL
   1      330 mg
```

**NCLEX Tip:** The final concentration matters. Different volumes of diluent yield different concentrations. Always read the reconstitution label carefully.

## Heparin Dosing & Protocols

### Heparin Infusion Protocol (Example)

| aPTT (seconds) | Bolus | Hold Infusion | Rate Change | Next aPTT |
|---------------|-------|--------------|-------------|-----------|
| < 50 | 5000 units | No | ↑ by 2 units/kg/hr | 6 hours |
| 50–70 (therapeutic) | None | No | No change | 24 hours |
| 71–99 | None | No | ↓ by 1 unit/kg/hr | 6 hours |
| > 100 | None | 1 hour | ↓ by 3 units/kg/hr | 6 hours |

### Heparin Calculation

**Order:** Heparin 25,000 units in 500 mL D5W. Infuse at 1200 units/hr.

```
1200 units     500 mL
---------- × --------- = 24 mL/hr
   hr       25,000 units
```

### Protamine Sulfate (Heparin Antidote)

- 1 mg protamine neutralizes approximately 100 units of heparin
- Give slow IV (over 1–3 minutes) to prevent hypotension

## Insulin Calculations

### U-100 Insulin

Most insulin is U-100 (100 units/mL).

**Order:** 15 units regular insulin subQ  
**Available:** U-100 insulin (100 units/mL)

```
15 units     1 mL
-------- × ------- = 0.15 mL
   1      100 units
```

Use an insulin syringe marked in units (not mL) to avoid errors.

### IV Insulin Drip

**Order:** Regular insulin 100 units in 100 mL NS (1 unit/mL). Start at 2 units/hr.

```
2 units     100 mL
------- × --------- = 2 mL/hr
  hr      100 units
```

## Critical Care Calculations

### Mcg/kg/min to mL/hr

**Order:** Norepinephrine 0.05 mcg/kg/min for a 80 kg patient  
**Available:** Norepinephrine 4 mg in 250 mL D5W

```
0.05 mcg   80 kg    1 mg     250 mL    60 min
-------- × ------ × ------- × ------- × ------ = 15 mL/hr
 kg·min     1     1000 mcg    4 mg      1 hr
```

### Mg/kg/hr to mL/hr

**Order:** Morphine 0.1 mg/kg/hr for a 60 kg child  
**Available:** Morphine 50 mg in 50 mL (1 mg/mL)

```
0.1 mg    60 kg    1 mL
------- × ------ × ------- = 6 mL/hr
 kg·hr     1      1 mg
```

## Tips for Avoiding Calculation Errors

1. **Always write out your equation** — don't do mental math
2. **Circle the unit you need** in your final answer
3. **Verify with a second nurse** for high-risk medications (heparin, insulin, chemo)
4. **Check reasonable range:**
   - Tablets: usually 0.5–4 tabs
   - Oral liquid: usually 5–30 mL
   - IM injections: usually 0.5–3 mL (max adult IM: 3–5 mL per site)
   - IV push: varies by medication
5. **Use leading zeros** (0.5 mL), **never trailing zeros** (5.0 mL → write 5 mL)

## NCLEX Practice Questions

### Question 1
A patient is to receive 750 mg of cephalexin PO. The pharmacy sends cephalexin 250 mg capsules. How many capsules should the nurse administer?

A. 1 capsule  
B. 2 capsules  
C. 3 capsules  
D. 4 capsules

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**C. 3 capsules**

750 mg ÷ 250 mg/capsule = 3 capsules

</details>

### Question 2
An IV of 1000 mL NS is to infuse over 6 hours using tubing with a drop factor of 15 gtt/mL. What is the drip rate?

A. 21 gtt/min  
B. 28 gtt/min  
C. 42 gtt/min  
D. 56 gtt/min

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**C. 42 gtt/min**

(1000 mL × 15 gtt/mL) ÷ 360 minutes = 41.67 → 42 gtt/min

</details>

### Question 3 (NGN-Style)
A child weighs 33 lbs. The provider orders amoxicillin 40 mg/kg/day divided q8h. The available suspension is 250 mg/5 mL.

**How many mL should the nurse administer per dose?**

A. 2 mL  
B. 4 mL  
C. 6 mL  
D. 8 mL

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. 4 mL**

Step 1: 33 lb ÷ 2.2 = 15 kg  
Step 2: 40 mg/kg/day × 15 kg = 600 mg/day  
Step 3: q8h = 3 doses per day → 600 ÷ 3 = 200 mg/dose  
Step 4: (200 mg ÷ 250 mg) × 5 mL = 4 mL

</details>
