# OB Nursing: Labor Stages, Fetal Monitoring & NCLEX Priorities

**Published:** 2026-08-11 | **Reading Time:** 14 min | **NCLEX Category:** Health Promotion and Maintenance / Physiological Integrity

---

## Quick Summary

Maternity nursing is 9–15% of the NCLEX. You need to know the stages of labor, fetal heart rate patterns, APGAR scoring, and how to recognize complications early. Most questions test whether you know what's **normal** vs. **concerning** — and what to do first.

| Stage | Key Events | Duration (Nullipara) | Duration (Multipara) |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **1st (Latent)** | 0–3 cm, mild contractions | 6–8 hours | 4–6 hours |
| **1st (Active)** | 4–7 cm, stronger contractions | 3–6 hours | 2–3 hours |
| **1st (Transition)** | 8–10 cm, intense contractions | 20 min – 2 hours | 20–45 min |
| **2nd** | Pushing to delivery | 30 min – 3 hours | 5–30 min |
| **3rd** | Placenta delivery | 5–30 min | 5–15 min |
| **4th** | Recovery (first 2–4 hours) | 2–4 hours | 2–4 hours |

---

## The One Thing to Remember

> **In OB, the mother is your priority UNLESS the fetal heart rate is nonreassuring.** If FHR shows late decelerations, severe bradycardia, or absent variability — the fetus becomes the priority immediately.

## First Stage of Labor: Dilation & Effacement

### What's Happening
The cervix dilates (opens) to 10 cm and effaces (thins) from 0% to 100%. This stage is divided into three phases.

### Latent Phase (0–3 cm)

| Parameter | Normal Findings |
|-----------|----------------|
| Contractions | Mild, irregular, 5–30 min apart |
| Duration | 30–45 seconds |
| Intensity | Mild (palpable but not painful) |
| Patient Behavior | Talkative, excited, can walk/ambulate |
| Cervical Change | Slow (may take hours) |

**Nursing Care:**
- Encourage ambulation and position changes
- Hydration (clear fluids, ice chips)
- Breathing techniques and relaxation
- Support person involvement
- Don't rush — latent phase can take time

**NCLEX Tip:** A patient at 2 cm who is coping well doesn't need pain medication yet. Offer nonpharmacologic comfort measures first.

### Active Phase (4–7 cm)

| Parameter | Normal Findings |
|-----------|----------------|
| Contractions | Regular, 3–5 min apart |
| Duration | 45–60 seconds |
| Intensity | Moderate to strong |
| Patient Behavior | Focused inward, less talkative |
| Cervical Change | 1–1.2 cm/hour (nullipara), 1.5 cm/hour (multipara) |

**Nursing Care:**
- Continuous fetal monitoring if not already
- Pain management assessment (ready for epidural?)
- Monitor vital signs every 30–60 min
- Assess contractions: frequency, duration, intensity
- Encourage voiding every 1–2 hours (full bladder impedes descent)

**Prolonged Active Phase:**
- Nullipara: > 6 hours with no change
- Multipara: > 3 hours with no change
- Causes: CPD (cephalopelvic disproportion), malposition, inadequate contractions

### Transition Phase (8–10 cm)

| Parameter | Normal Findings |
|-----------|----------------|
| Contractions | Strong, 2–3 min apart |
| Duration | 60–90 seconds |
| Intensity | Very strong |
| Patient Behavior | Irritable, restless, feels urge to push |

**Signs of Transition:**
- Nausea and vomiting
- Shaking/chills
- Feeling of rectal pressure (urge to push)
- Contractions feel overwhelming

**Nursing Care:**
- Coach breathing — panting breaths if urge to push before 10 cm
- Supportive, calm presence (patient may become irritable)
- Prepare for second stage
- Monitor FHR continuously

**Critical NCLEX Point:**
> **Don't let the patient push before complete dilation (10 cm).** Pushing against an incompletely dilated cervix can cause cervical edema, lacerations, and prolonged labor. If the patient feels the urge to push at 8–9 cm, coach panting or "blow-out" breathing.

## Second Stage of Labor: Pushing to Delivery

### What's Happening
The cervix is fully dilated. The patient pushes with contractions to move the fetus through the birth canal.

### Cardinal Movements of Delivery
1. **Engagement** — fetal head enters pelvic inlet
2. **Descent** — moves down birth canal
3. **Flexion** — chin tucks to chest
4. **Internal rotation** — rotates to occiput anterior (OA)
5. **Extension** — head delivers
6. **External rotation** — head realigns with shoulders
7. **Expulsion** — shoulders and body deliver

### Nursing Care During Pushing

**Coach effective pushing:**
- Take a deep breath at contraction peak
- Hold breath and bear down (closed glottis pushing)
- Push for 6–8 seconds
- Exhale and breathe
- Repeat 2–3 times per contraction

**Monitor FHR:**
- Check between contractions
- Normal: 110–160 bpm
- Concerning: < 110 or > 160, late decelerations, decreased variability

**Positioning:**
- Lithotomy (traditional)
- Side-lying (reduces perineal trauma)
- Hands-and-knees (helps occiput posterior rotate)
- Squatting (widens pelvic outlet)

**Episiotomy:**
- Not routine — only if fetal distress requires immediate delivery
- Median (midline) vs. mediolateral
- If performed, local anesthetic is used

### Delivery Sequence
1. **Crowning** — fetal head visible, doesn't recede between pushes
2. **Perineal support** — apply warm compress, support perineum
3. **Head delivery** — guide head out, check for nuchal cord
4. **Suction mouth and nose** — bulb syringe (if meconium-stained, deeper suctioning may be needed)
5. **Deliver shoulders** — gentle downward traction for anterior shoulder, then upward for posterior
6. **Clamp and cut cord** — after pulsation stops or at 1–3 minutes (delayed cord clamping benefits)

## Third Stage of Labor: Placenta Delivery

### What's Happening
The placenta separates from the uterine wall and delivers. This stage is critical for preventing hemorrhage.

### Signs of Placental Separation
1. **Gush of blood** from vagina
2. **Lengthening of umbilical cord**
3. **Uterus becomes firm and globular** (rises in abdomen)
4. **Uterus shifts upward** — fundus moves from left to midline

### Placental Delivery Methods
| Method | Description | When Used |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Spontaneous** | Patient pushes it out naturally | Preferred method |
| **Controlled cord traction** | Gentle traction on cord while supporting uterus | Active management |
| **Manual removal** | Provider reaches in to remove | If placenta doesn't deliver within 30 min |

### Active Management of Third Stage (Recommended)
1. **Oxytocin (Pitocin)** — 10 units IM or IV after anterior shoulder delivery
2. **Controlled cord traction** — gentle, steady traction
3. **Uterine massage** — after placental delivery

**Why this matters:** Active management reduces postpartum hemorrhage risk by 60%.

### Inspect the Placenta
- Complete? (missing cotyledons suggest retained fragments)
- Cord vessels: should be 2 arteries + 1 vein
- Any abnormalities?

## Fourth Stage: Immediate Postpartum Recovery

### What's Happening
The first 2–4 hours after delivery. This is the most dangerous time for postpartum hemorrhage.

### BUBBLE-HE Assessment

| Letter | System | Assessment |
|--------|--------|------------|
| **B** | Breasts | Engorgement, nipple condition, feeding |
| **U** | Uterus | Fundal height, firmness, position |
| **B** | Bladder | Distention, voiding |
| **B** | Bowels | Function, hemorrhoids |
| **L** | Lochia | Amount, color, odor |
| **E** | Episiotomy/Perineum | Healing, edema, hematoma |
| **H** | Homans' sign | Calf pain (DVT check) — **Note: not routinely done anymore** |
| **E** | Emotional status | Bonding, mood, support system |

### Fundal Assessment
- **Location:** Should be at or below umbilicus (U), firm
- **If boggy (soft):** Massage fundus until firm → expels clots
- **If deviated to right:** Full bladder → have patient void
- **If rising above U:** Uterine atony or hemorrhage

### Lochia Patterns
| Type | Timing | Description | Warning Signs |
|------|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **Rubra** | Days 1–3 | Bright red, bloody, small clots OK | Large clots, foul odor |
| **Serosa** | Days 4–10 | Pinkish-brown, serous | Return to rubra |
| **Alba** | Days 10–6 weeks | White/yellow, creamy | Foul odor, persists > 6 weeks |

**Saturated pad in 15 min or less = excessive bleeding. Notify provider immediately.**

## Fetal Heart Rate Monitoring

### Baseline FHR
| Category | Rate | Interpretation |
|----------|------|----------------|
| Normal | 110–160 bpm | Reassuring |
| Bradycardia | < 110 bpm | Nonreassuring if persistent |
| Tachycardia | > 160 bpm | Nonreassuring if persistent |

### Variability
| Type | Description | Interpretation |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| Absent | No fluctuations | **Nonreassuring** — possible fetal hypoxia |
| Minimal | < 5 bpm fluctuation | Concern if persistent |
| Moderate | 6–25 bpm fluctuation | **Reassuring** — normal |
| Marked | > 25 bpm fluctuation | Usually benign |

**Moderate variability is the single best indicator of fetal well-being.**

### Deceleration Patterns (HIGH YIELD)

#### Early Decelerations ✅ Reassuring
- **Shape:** Mirror image of contraction (gradual onset, gradual return)
- **Timing:** Begins at start of contraction, returns to baseline by end
- **Cause:** Head compression → vagal response
- **Action:** Normal, continue monitoring

#### Late Decelerations ⚠️ Nonreassuring
- **Shape:** Gradual onset, returns to baseline AFTER contraction ends
- **Timing:** Starts at peak or after peak of contraction
- **Cause:** Uteroplacental insufficiency → fetal hypoxia
- **Action:**
  1. **Turn patient to left side** (improves placental perfusion)
  2. **Increase IV fluids** (if not contraindicated)
  3. **Stop oxytocin if infusing**
  4. **Administer O₂ by face mask** (8–10 L)
  5. **Notify provider** — may need emergent delivery

#### Variable Decelerations ⚠️ Usually Concerning
- **Shape:** Abrupt onset, abrupt return (V or U shaped), may be variable depth
- **Timing:** Can occur any time, unrelated to contraction timing
- **Cause:** Cord compression
- **Action:**
  1. **Change maternal position** (relieve cord compression)
  2. **Discontinue oxytocin**
  3. **Assess for cord prolapse** (especially if severe and persistent)
  4. **Notify provider**

#### Prolonged Deceleration 🚨 Emergency
- FHR drops > 15 bpm below baseline for > 2 minutes but < 10 minutes
- Causes: Cord prolapse, uterine rupture, placental abruption, maternal hypotension
- **Action:** Immediate delivery preparation if not resolved quickly

### FHR Pattern Interpretation Summary

| Pattern | Cause | Action |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Early decelerations | Head compression | None needed |
| Late decelerations | Placental insufficiency | Left side, O₂, fluids, stop pitocin, notify provider |
| Variable decelerations | Cord compression | Change position, assess cord, notify provider |
| Prolonged deceleration | Multiple (cord, abruption, rupture) | Immediate intervention, prepare for delivery |

## APGAR Scoring

Assessed at 1 minute and 5 minutes after delivery.

| Sign | 0 Points | 1 Point | 2 Points |
|------|----------|---------|----------|
| **A**ppearance (color) | Blue/pale | Body pink, extremities blue | Completely pink |
| **P**ulse (HR) | Absent | < 100 bpm | > 100 bpm |
| **G**rimace (reflex) | No response | Grimace/feeble cry | Cough, sneeze, vigorous cry |
| **A**ctivity (muscle tone) | Flaccid | Some flexion | Active movement, flexed |
| **R**espiration | Absent | Slow, irregular | Good, crying |

### Interpretation
| Score | Condition |
|-------|-----------|
| 7–10 | Normal, no intervention needed |
| 4–6 | Moderate depression — may need suctioning, O₂, stimulation |
| 0–3 | Severe depression — requires resuscitation (PPV, intubation) |

**NCLEX Tip:** The 1-minute score reflects how the baby tolerated labor/delivery. The 5-minute score reflects how the baby responds to resuscitation. If 5-minute score is < 7, continue scoring every 5 minutes up to 20 minutes.

## Common OB Complications

### Preeclampsia & Eclampsia

| Feature | Preeclampsia | Severe Preeclampsia | Eclampsia |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|-----------|
| BP | ≥ 140/90 (×2, 4+ hours apart) | ≥ 160/110 | Any with seizure |
| Proteinuria | ≥ 300 mg/24h or 1+ dipstick | ≥ 5 g/24h or 3+ dipstick | May or may not be present |
| Symptoms | Often asymptomatic | Headache, visual changes, RUQ pain, hyperreflexia | Seizure/convulsion |

**HELLP Syndrome:** Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets

**Nursing Priorities for Severe Preeclampsia:**
1. **Seizure precautions** — padded side rails, low stimulation
2. **Magnesium sulfate** — seizure prophylaxis (loading dose 4–6 g IV, then 1–2 g/hr)
3. **Monitor magnesium levels** — therapeutic 4–8 mg/dL
4. **Watch for magnesium toxicity:**
   - Loss of deep tendon reflexes (first sign)
   - Respiratory depression
   - Decreased urine output
   - Cardiac arrest
5. **Antidote for magnesium toxicity:** Calcium gluconate 1 g IV

**Eclampsia = Seizure:**
- Turn patient on side
- Maintain airway
- Suction if needed
- Administer magnesium sulfate
- Prepare for delivery (definitive treatment)

### Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

**Definition:**
- Vaginal birth: > 500 mL blood loss in first 24 hours
- C-section: > 1000 mL blood loss

**Causes (4 T's):**
| T | Cause | Percentage |
|---|-------|-----------|
| **Tone** | Uterine atony (boggy uterus) | 70% |
| **Trauma** | Lacerations, hematoma, inversion | 20% |
| **Tissue** | Retained placenta, clots | 10% |
| **Thrombin** | Coagulopathy (DIC) | 1% |

**Nursing Interventions (in order):**
1. **Uterine massage** — if atony
2. **Empty bladder** — catheterize if needed
3. **Oxytocin (Pitocin)** — first-line medication
4. **Methylergonovine (Methergine)** — if no hypertension
5. **Carboprost (Hemabate)** — if no asthma
6. **Misoprostol (Cytotec)** — rectal
7. **Prepare for OR** if bleeding doesn't stop

### Shoulder Dystocia

**Definition:** After delivery of head, anterior shoulder is caught behind symphysis pubis.

**Emergency Management (HELPERR):**
| Letter | Action |
|--------|--------|
| **H** | Call for Help |
| **E** | Evaluate for episiotomy |
| **L** | Legs (McRoberts maneuver) — hyperflex hips, abduct thighs |
| **P** | Suprapubic Pressure — push above pubic bone |
| **E** | Enter maneuvers (internal rotation) |
| **R** | Remove posterior arm |
| **R** | Roll patient to hands-and-knees |

## NCLEX Practice Questions

### Question 1
A patient in active labor is fully dilated and feels a strong urge to push. The nurse notes the fetal heart rate baseline is 140 bpm with moderate variability. During the last three contractions, the FHR gradually decreased during the contraction and returned to baseline after the contraction ended. The nurse should:

A. Turn the patient to her left side immediately  
B. Document early decelerations and continue monitoring  
C. Administer O₂ by face mask  
D. Notify the provider of late decelerations

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Document early decelerations and continue monitoring**

The description matches early decelerations: gradual decrease with contraction, returns to baseline by the end of contraction. This is caused by head compression and is normal/reassuring. No intervention needed.

</details>

### Question 2
A patient at 32 weeks gestation has BP 158/110, proteinuria 3+, severe headache, and hyperreflexia. The nurse is preparing to administer magnesium sulfate. Which finding would require the nurse to stop the infusion?

A. Urine output 40 mL/hr  
B. Respiratory rate 18/min  
C. Absent deep tendon reflexes  
D. Fetal heart rate 130 bpm

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**C. Absent deep tendon reflexes**

Loss of DTRs is the first sign of magnesium toxicity. The antidote is calcium gluconate. Other signs of toxicity: respiratory depression (< 12/min), decreased urine output (< 30 mL/hr). A urine output of 40 mL/hr is acceptable.

</details>

### Question 3 (NGN-Style)
A patient delivered vaginally 30 minutes ago. The nurse assesses the fundus and finds it boggy (soft), deviated to the right, and located 2 cm above the umbilicus. Lochia is heavy with large clots. The patient's vital signs are: BP 92/58, HR 118, RR 22.

**What is the nurse's priority action?**

A. Administer methergine IM  
B. Massage the fundus and have the patient void  
C. Prepare the patient for emergency surgery  
D. Increase IV fluid rate and administer oxytocin

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Massage the fundus and have the patient void**

The boggy uterus + deviated to the right + above umbilicus = uterine atony with full bladder. The bladder prevents the uterus from contracting effectively.
- First: massage fundus to stimulate contraction
- Second: have patient void (or catheterize) to empty bladder
- If atony persists after bladder emptying: then administer oxytocin
- Methergine is contraindicated if patient has hypertension (unknown here)
- Surgery is not indicated as first-line for atony

</details>

### Question 4
A newborn's 1-minute APGAR is 5. The baby has a heart rate of 90 bpm, weak cry, some flexion of extremities, acrocyanosis, and irregular respiratory effort. What is the nurse's priority action?

A. Initiate positive pressure ventilation immediately  
B. Dry and stimulate the infant, provide O₂ as needed  
C. Begin chest compressions  
D. Call the neonatal resuscitation team for intubation

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Dry and stimulate the infant, provide O₂ as needed**

APGAR of 5 = moderate depression. The heart rate is > 100? No — it's 90, which is < 100 (1 point). However, the baby has a weak cry, some flexion, and irregular respirations — these warrant stimulation and possibly blow-by O₂. Positive pressure ventilation is for HR < 100 after 30 seconds of stimulation. Chest compressions are for HR < 60 after PPV. Follow NRP algorithm: dry, stimulate, position, suction if needed, then assess need for PPV.

</details>
