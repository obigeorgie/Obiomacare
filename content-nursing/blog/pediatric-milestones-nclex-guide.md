# Pediatric Milestones: NCLEX Developmental Guide by Age

**Published:** 2026-08-11 | **Reading Time:** 11 min | **NCLEX Category:** Health Promotion and Maintenance

---

## Quick Summary

Pediatric developmental milestones are 9–12% of the NCLEX. Questions test whether you know what's **normal** for each age and can **recognize red flags** that indicate developmental delay. The key is knowing the major milestones in motor, language, and social/emotional domains.

| Age | Gross Motor | Fine Motor | Language | Social |
|-----|------------|-----------|----------|--------|
| **1 month** | Head lag, primitive reflexes | Grasp reflex | Cries | Startles to sound |
| **2 months** | Lifts head prone | Hands unfist | Coos | Social smile |
| **4 months** | Rolls front to back | Reaches for objects | Laughs, squeals | Recognizes familiar faces |
| **6 months** | Sits with support | Transfers objects | Babbles (ma, da) | Stranger anxiety begins |
| **9 months** | Creeps/crawls, pulls to stand | Pincer grasp | Says "mama/dada" | Waves bye-bye |
| **12 months** | Walks holding on, stands alone | Pincer mature | 1–2 words + jargon | Imitates activities |
| **15 months** | Walks alone, stoops to pick up | Scribbles | 4–6 words | Points to objects |
| **18 months** | Runs, walks up stairs (hand held) | Builds tower 2–4 blocks | 10–20 words | Temper tantrums |
| **2 years** | Runs well, kicks ball, jumps | Builds tower 6 blocks | 2-word phrases | Parallel play |
| **3 years** | Pedals tricycle, stands on one foot | Copies circle | 3-word sentences | Cooperative play |
| **4 years** | Hops on one foot, climbs well | Copies cross, square | Tells stories | Imaginary friends |
| **5 years** | Skips, jumps rope | Copies triangle, letters | 5+ word sentences | Plays with peers |

---

## The One Thing to Remember

> **If a child isn't doing what 90% of their peers can do, it's a red flag.** Know the key milestones that must be present at each age — and know which missing milestones require referral.

## Primitive Reflexes (Birth–6 Months)

Primitive reflexes are present at birth and disappear as the nervous system matures. Their persistence beyond expected age indicates neurological dysfunction.

| Reflex | Stimulus | Response | Disappears By |
|--------|----------|----------|---------------|
| **Moro (startle)** | Sudden movement or loud noise | Arms abduct, extend, then adduct; legs extend | 3–4 months |
| **Palmar grasp** | Object placed in palm | Fingers flex and grasp | 3–4 months |
| **Plantar grasp** | Object pressed against sole of foot | Toes curl down | 8–10 months |
| **Rooting** | Stroke cheek near mouth | Head turns toward stimulus, mouth opens | 3–4 months |
| **Sucking** | Object placed in mouth | Rhythmic sucking | 4 months (voluntary sucking remains) |
| **Tonic neck (fencing)** | Turn head to one side | Ipsilateral arm extends, contralateral flexes | 4–6 months |
| **Babinski** | Stroke sole of foot lateral to medial | Toes fan upward | 12–24 months (then becomes plantar flexion) |

**NCLEX Trap:** If Moro or rooting persists past 4–6 months, investigate for neurological issues.

## Infant Milestones (0–12 Months)

### Newborn (0–1 Month)

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Head lag when pulled to sit, flexed posture, primitive reflexes present |
| **Sensory** | Visual acuity 20/400, fixes on faces, responds to sound |
| **Communication** | Cries to communicate needs |
| **Social** | Quiet when hearing familiar voice |

**Red Flags:**
- No response to loud sounds
- No visual fixation
- Extreme floppiness or stiffness
- Feeding difficulties

### 2 Months

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Lifts head 45° when prone, bears some weight on legs |
| **Fine Motor** | Hands unfist, briefly holds rattle |
| **Communication** | Coos, makes vowel sounds |
| **Social** | **Social smile** (KEY MILESTONE) |

### 4 Months

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Rolls front to back, head steady when sitting supported |
| **Fine Motor** | Reaches for and grasps objects, brings hands to midline |
| **Communication** | Laughs, squeals, turns to sound |
| **Social** | Recognizes familiar faces, enjoys looking at mirror |

**NCLEX High-Yield:** Rolling **front to back** at 4 months. Back to front comes later (6 months).

### 6 Months

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Sits with support, rolls back to front, bears full weight on legs |
| **Fine Motor** | Transfers objects hand to hand, raking grasp |
| **Communication** | Babbles (consonant sounds: "ba," "da," "ma") |
| **Social** | **Stranger anxiety begins**, recognizes own name |

**Key Milestone:** Babbling with consonants at 6 months. No babbling by 6 months = speech evaluation.

### 9 Months

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Creeps/crawls, pulls to stand, cruises along furniture |
| **Fine Motor** | **Pincer grasp emerges** (thumb + finger) |
| **Communication** | Says "mama" and "dada" nonspecifically, understands "no" |
| **Social** | Waves bye-bye, plays peek-a-boo, separation anxiety peaks |

### 12 Months (1 Year)

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Walks holding on (cruising), may stand alone, first steps |
| **Fine Motor** | Mature pincer, puts objects in container |
| **Communication** | **1–2 words** with meaning ("mama," "dada" specific), follows simple commands |
| **Social** | Imitates activities, shows affection, explores environment |

**Critical Milestones at 12 Months:**
- Walking holding on (or independently)
- At least 1 word with meaning
- Pincer grasp
- Responds to name

**Red Flags at 12 Months:**
- Not crawling or bearing weight on legs
- No words
- No pointing or waving
- No response to name
- Loss of previously acquired skills

## Toddler Milestones (1–3 Years)

### 15 Months

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Walks alone, stoops to pick up object, climbs stairs (crawling) |
| **Fine Motor** | Scribbles, builds tower 2 blocks |
| **Communication** | 4–6 words, points to wanted object |
| **Social** | Shows affection, indicates wet pants |

### 18 Months

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Runs, walks up stairs with hand held, throws ball |
| **Fine Motor** | Builds tower 3–4 blocks, turns pages of book |
| **Communication** | 10–20 words, uses jargon (nonsense with intonation) |
| **Social** | **Temper tantrums** begin, copies others, shows independence |

**Key Milestone:** 10+ words by 18 months. Less than 10 words = speech/language evaluation.

### 2 Years (24 Months)

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Runs well, kicks ball, jumps in place, walks up/down stairs (2 feet per step) |
| **Fine Motor** | Builds tower 6 blocks, unscrews lid, turns pages one at a time |
| **Communication** | **2-word phrases** ("more milk"), 50+ words, 50% intelligible |
| **Social** | **Parallel play** (plays alongside, not with, other children), imitation play |

**Critical Milestones at 2 Years:**
- 50+ words
- 2-word combinations
- Runs and walks up stairs
- Feeds self with spoon

**Red Flags at 2 Years:**
- No 2-word phrases
- Doesn't follow simple instructions
- No pretend play
- Unsteady gait, frequent falls

### 3 Years

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Pedals tricycle, stands on one foot briefly, walks stairs alternating feet |
| **Fine Motor** | Copies circle, draws person with head, dresses self (some help) |
| **Communication** | 3-word sentences, asks "why," 75% intelligible |
| **Social** | **Cooperative play** begins, takes turns, knows age and gender |

## Preschool Milestones (3–5 Years)

### 4 Years

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Hops on one foot, climbs well, throws ball overhand |
| **Fine Motor** | Copies cross and square, draws person with 2–4 parts |
| **Communication** | Tells stories, speaks in 4–5 word sentences, 100% intelligible |
| **Social** | Imaginary friends, plays board games, understands rules |

### 5 Years

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Motor** | Skips, jumps rope, walks backward heel-to-toe |
| **Fine Motor** | Copies triangle and letters, writes name, ties shoelaces |
| **Communication** | 5+ word sentences, uses future tense, tells elaborate stories |
| **Social** | Plays with peers, understands time concepts, wants to please |

**School Readiness at 5 Years:**
- Recognizes colors, shapes, some letters
- Counts to 10
- Understands opposites (big/little, up/down)
- Can sit still for short periods
- Separates from parents without distress

---

## School-Age Milestones (6–12 Years)

| Age | Physical | Cognitive | Social |
|-----|----------|-----------|--------|
| **6–7 years** | Loses first teeth, growth ~5 lbs/year | Reads, writes, understands time | Peer importance increases, follows rules |
| **8–9 years** | Improved coordination, permanent teeth erupt | Abstract thinking begins, interested in how things work | Team sports, clubs, best friends |
| **10–12 years** | Puberty begins (earlier in girls), growth spurt | Logical reasoning, problem-solving | Peer group very important, identity formation |

**Prepubescent Growth:**
- Average weight gain: 5–7 lbs/year
- Average height gain: 2 inches/year
- First permanent teeth: 6 years (molars)
- Girls begin puberty: 8–13 years
- Boys begin puberty: 9–14 years

## Adolescent Milestones (12–18 Years)

### Puberty Sequence (Tanner Stages)

| Tanner Stage | Girls | Boys |
|-------------|-------|------|
| **Stage 1** | Prepubertal | Prepubertal |
| **Stage 2** | Breast buds (thelarche), sparse pubic hair | Testicular enlargement, sparse pubic hair |
| **Stage 3** | Breast enlargement, darker/coarser pubic hair | Penis enlargement, continued pubic hair growth |
| **Stage 4** | Areola and nipple form secondary mound, adult-type hair | Increased penis size, scrotal darkening, adult hair |
| **Stage 5** | Adult breast contour, adult pubic hair distribution | Adult genitalia, adult hair distribution |

**Key Points:**
- **Girls:** Peak growth spurt occurs early in puberty (before menarche). Menarche typically occurs in Tanner Stage 3–4.
- **Boys:** Peak growth spurt occurs later in puberty (Stage 4).
- **Menarche average age:** 12.5 years (range 10–16)
- **Early puberty:** Before 8 (girls), 9 (boys)
- **Delayed puberty:** No breast development by 13 (girls), no testicular enlargement by 14 (boys)

**NCLEX Trap:** Early menarche does NOT mean a girl is emotionally mature. She needs age-appropriate support.

## Pediatric Vital Signs by Age

| Age | Heart Rate (awake) | Respiratory Rate | Blood Pressure (systolic) |
|-----|-------------------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| **Newborn** | 100–160 | 30–60 | 60–90 |
| **1–12 months** | 80–140 | 25–40 | 72–104 |
| **1–2 years** | 80–130 | 20–30 | 74–106 |
| **3–5 years** | 80–120 | 20–25 | 78–110 |
| **6–12 years** | 70–110 | 18–25 | 82–118 |
| **> 12 years** | 60–100 | 12–20 | 90–120 |

**NCLEX High-Yield:** Tachycardia and tachypnea are early signs of respiratory distress in children. Hypotension is a **late** sign — children maintain BP through vasoconstriction until they're in severe shock.

## Growth Charts and Percentiles

### Measuring Growth
- **Infants:** Length (not height), weight, head circumference
- **Children > 2 years:** Height, weight, BMI
- **Head circumference:** Measured until age 2 (brain growth 80% complete by then)

### Interpreting Percentiles
| Percentile | Interpretation |
|-----------|---------------|
| > 95th | Overweight/obese (weight), macrocephaly (head) |
| 90–95th | At risk |
| 10–90th | Normal |
| 5–10th | Monitor closely |
| < 5th | Failure to thrive (weight), microcephaly (head) |

**Crossing Percentiles:**
- Dropping 2+ percentile lines = concern
- Rising 2+ percentile lines = concern (obesity)

## Immunization Schedule (Birth to 6 Years)

| Age | Vaccines |
|-----|----------|
| **Birth** | Hepatitis B #1 |
| **2 months** | DTaP #1, IPV #1, Hib #1, PCV #1, Rotavirus #1 |
| **4 months** | DTaP #2, IPV #2, Hib #2, PCV #2, Rotavirus #2 |
| **6 months** | DTaP #3, IPV #3, Hib #3, PCV #3, Hep B #2, Rotavirus #3 |
| **12 months** | MMR #1, Varicella #1, Hib #4, PCV #4 |
| **15–18 months** | DTaP #4 |
| **4–6 years** | DTaP #5, IPV #4, MMR #2, Varicella #2 |

**Additional:**
- **Influenza:** Yearly starting at 6 months
- **Hepatitis A:** 2 doses starting at 12 months
- **COVID-19:** Per current guidelines

**NCLEX Tip:** Live vaccines (MMR, varicella, intranasal flu) are **contraindicated in immunocompromised patients** and **pregnant women**.

## Red Flags for Developmental Delay

### By 2 Months
- [ ] No response to loud sounds
- [ ] Doesn't watch moving objects
- [ ] Doesn't bring hands to mouth
- [ ] Can't hold head up when pushing up on arms

### By 4 Months
- [ ] Doesn't watch faces or smile
- [ ] Doesn't coo or make sounds
- [ ] Doesn't push down with legs when feet on hard surface
- [ ] Has trouble moving one or both eyes

### By 6 Months
- [ ] Doesn't laugh or make squealing sounds
- [ ] Doesn't roll over in either direction
- [ ] Very stiff or very floppy muscles
- [ ] Doesn't try to reach for objects

### By 9 Months
- [ ] Doesn't bear weight on legs with support
- [ ] Doesn't babble ("ma," "da," "ba")
- [ ] Doesn't play games (peek-a-boo)
- [ ] Doesn't look where you point

### By 12 Months
- [ ] Doesn't crawl
- [ ] Doesn't say single words ("mama," "dada")
- [ ] Doesn't point, wave, or gesture
- [ ] Doesn't search for hidden objects
- [ ] Loses skills previously had

### By 18 Months
- [ ] Doesn't point to show things to others
- [ ] Doesn't know what familiar things are for
- [ ] Doesn't have at least 6 words
- [ ] Doesn't gain new skills
- [ ] Loses skills previously had

### By 2 Years
- [ ] Doesn't use 2-word phrases
- [ ] Doesn't imitate actions or words
- [ ] Doesn't follow simple instructions
- [ ] Doesn't walk steadily
- [ ] Loses skills previously had

## NCLEX Practice Questions

### Question 1
A nurse is assessing a 6-month-old infant during a well-child visit. Which finding requires follow-up?

A. Transfers objects from hand to hand  
B. Sits with support  
C. Does not babble consonant sounds  
D. Bears full weight on legs when held standing

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**C. Does not babble consonant sounds**

By 6 months, infants should babble with consonant sounds ("ba," "da," "ma"). Not babbling by 6 months requires referral for speech/language evaluation. The other options are all normal 6-month milestones.

</details>

### Question 2
A parent asks the nurse when their 2-year-old should be able to pedal a tricycle. The nurse correctly responds:

A. "Most children can pedal a tricycle by age 2."  
B. "Tricycle pedaling usually develops around age 3."  
C. "Children typically pedal a tricycle by age 4."  
D. "Your child should already be able to do this."

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. "Tricycle pedaling usually develops around age 3."**

Pedaling a tricycle is a 3-year milestone. At 2 years, children run well, kick balls, and jump in place but don't yet have the coordinated reciprocal leg movement for pedaling.

</details>

### Question 3 (NGN-Style)
A nurse is assessing a 12-month-old during a well-child visit. The parent reports the child:
- Walks holding onto furniture
- Says "mama" and "dada" specifically
- Points to wanted objects
- Plays peek-a-boo
- The child was previously waving bye-bye but has stopped doing this in the past month

**What action should the nurse take?**

A. Reassure the parent that this is normal toddler behavior  
B. Refer the child for developmental evaluation  
C. Schedule a follow-up visit in 3 months  
D. Assess the child's hearing

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Refer the child for developmental evaluation**

The loss of a previously acquired skill (waving bye-bye) at 12 months is a **red flag** for developmental regression. This requires immediate evaluation for possible autism spectrum disorder, neurological issues, or other developmental concerns. Reassurance is inappropriate. Hearing assessment is important but the regression is the priority concern.

</details>

### Question 4
A 4-year-old is brought to the clinic for a preschool physical. The nurse would expect the child to demonstrate which skill?

A. Ties shoelaces  
B. Copies a triangle  
C. Rides a tricycle  
D. Builds a tower of 6 blocks

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>

**B. Copies a triangle**

At 4 years, children copy a cross and square and begin to copy a triangle. Tying shoelaces is a 5-year milestone. Riding a tricycle is 3 years. Building a tower of 6 blocks is 2 years.

</details>
