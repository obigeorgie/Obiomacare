Antibiotics & Infectious Diseases

Antibiotic classes, mechanisms, side effects, resistance patterns, and infectious disease nursing care for the NCLEX.

🔬 Antibiotic Classes & Mechanisms

Penicillins (e.g., penicillin G, amoxicillin, piperacillin)

Mechanism: Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to PBPs (penicillin-binding proteins)

Spectrum: Gram-positive cocci, some gram-negative

Extended spectrum (piperacillin): Pseudomonas, Enterobacteriaceae

Side Effects:
  • Hypersensitivity reactions (rash, anaphylaxis)
  • Diarrhea, C. difficile colitis
  • Seizures (high doses)
Nursing Priority: Ask about penicillin allergy before administration. Cross-reactivity with cephalosporins ~10%. Have epinephrine available for IM/IV penicillin.

Cephalosporins (e.g., cephalexin, ceftriaxone, cefepime)

Generations:

  • 1st (cephalexin): Gram-positive cocci, some E. coli, Klebsiella, Proteus
  • 2nd (cefaclor): + H. influenzae, Moraxella
  • 3rd (ceftriaxone): + Enterobacteriaceae, Neisseria
  • 4th (cefepime): Broad gram-negative + Pseudomonas
  • 5th (ceftaroline): MRSA coverage
Side Effects: GI upset, C. difficile, hypersensitivity (cross-reactivity with penicillins)

Macrolides (e.g., azithromycin, clarithromycin, erythromycin)

Mechanism: Inhibit bacterial protein synthesis (50S ribosomal subunit)

Uses: Atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella), pertussis, strep throat (PCN-allergic patients)

Side Effects:
  • GI upset (erythromycin > azithromycin)
  • QT prolongation → monitor ECG
  • Hepatotoxicity
  • Ototoxicity (high doses, renal impairment)
Mnemonic: "MAC attacks the 50S" — Macrolides bind 50S ribosomal subunit

Fluoroquinolones (e.g., ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin)

Mechanism: Inhibit DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV

Uses: UTIs, pseudomonal infections, anthrax, atypical pneumonia, complicated intra-abdominal

Side Effects:
  • Tendon rupture (especially >60 years, corticosteroids)
  • Peripheral neuropathy
  • QT prolongation
  • CNS effects (insomnia, headache, seizures)
  • Photosensitivity
  • Aortic aneurysm risk
NCLEX Trap: Avoid in pregnancy and children <18 years (cartilage damage). Do NOT give with antacids, iron, calcium (reduced absorption).

Aminoglycosides (e.g., gentamicin, tobramycin, amikacin)

Mechanism: Inhibit bacterial protein synthesis (30S ribosomal subunit)

Uses: Gram-negative sepsis, endocarditis ( synergy with beta-lactams), cystic fibrosis

Side Effects:
  • Nephrotoxicity — monitor creatinine, urine output
  • Ototoxicity (irreversible) — monitor hearing, vestibular function
  • Neuromuscular blockade (rare, potentiated with anesthesia)
Nursing Priority: Monitor peak and trough levels. Ensure adequate hydration. Check renal function before and during therapy. Ototoxicity is irreversible — stop drug if hearing changes.

Metronidazole

Uses: Anaerobic infections, C. difficile, bacterial vaginosis, trichomoniasis, giardiasis

Side Effects:
  • Metallic taste
  • Disulfiram reaction with alcohol (severe vomiting, flushing, headache)
  • Peripheral neuropathy (long-term use)
  • Dark urine (harmless)
NCLEX Trap: Patient MUST avoid alcohol during treatment and for 48-72 hours after last dose. Teach patient about disulfiram reaction.

Vancomycin

Uses: MRSA, C. difficile (PO only), severe C. difficile colitis

Side Effects:
  • Red Man Syndrome (histamine release) — slow infusion prevents
  • Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity
  • Thrombophlebitis
Nursing Priority: Infuse over ≥1 hour (≥10 mg/min). Monitor trough levels (goal 10-20 mcg/mL for most infections). Pre-medicate with antihistamine if history of Red Man Syndrome. Never give IM (tissue necrosis).

Carbapenems (e.g., meropenem, imipenem)

Broadest spectrum beta-lactams — gram-positive, gram-negative, anaerobes

Uses: ESBL producers, polymicrobial intra-abdominal, hospital-acquired pneumonia, febrile neutropenia

Side Effects: Seizures (imipenem > meropenem), GI upset, C. difficile
NCLEX Trap: Imipenem is metabolized by renal dehydropeptidase — must give with cilastatin. Meropenem does NOT need cilastatin.

🦠 Specific Infectious Diseases

Clostridioides difficile (C. diff) Infection

Risk factors: Broad-spectrum antibiotics (especially clindamycin, fluoroquinolones, cephalosporins), PPIs, advanced age, hospitalization

Manifestations: Watery diarrhea (>3 episodes/day), abdominal cramping, fever, leukocytosis, pseudomembranous colitis

Treatment:

  • First episode: Oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin (preferred)
  • Severe: PO vancomycin 125 mg QID
  • Fulminant: PO vancomycin + IV metronidazole
  • Recurrent: Fecal microbiota transplantation
Nursing Priority: Contact precautions (gown and gloves). Hand hygiene with soap and water (alcohol-based sanitizers do NOT kill C. diff spores). Dedicated equipment. Discontinue offending antibiotic if possible.

Sepsis & Septic Shock

Definition (Sepsis-3): Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection (SOFA score ≥2)

Manifestations: Fever or hypothermia, tachycardia, tachypnea, altered mental status, hypotension, oliguria, elevated lactate
Hour-1 Bundle (Surviving Sepsis Campaign):
  1. Measure lactate level (remeasure if initial >2 mmol/L)
  2. Obtain blood cultures BEFORE antibiotics (do NOT delay >45 min)
  3. Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour
  4. Rapid administration of 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L
  5. Apply vasopressors if hypotensive during/after fluid resuscitation (target MAP ≥65 mmHg)
NCLEX Trap: Blood cultures MUST be drawn BEFORE starting antibiotics, but do NOT delay antibiotics >1 hour for cultures. Give fluids rapidly (30 mL/kg) for hypotension or elevated lactate.

Tuberculosis (TB)

Transmission: Airborne — Mycobacterium tuberculosis

Manifestations: Cough >3 weeks, hemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss, fever, fatigue

Diagnosis: Sputum AFB smear and culture, chest X-ray (upper lobe infiltrates, cavitation), nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT)

Treatment (active TB): RIPE regimen for 6 months

  • Rifampin — orange body fluids, hepatotoxicity, many drug interactions
  • Isoniazid (INH) — peripheral neuropathy (give pyridoxine B6), hepatotoxicity
  • Pyrazinamide — hepatotoxicity, hyperuricemia
  • Ethambutol — optic neuritis (red-green color blindness)
Mnemonic: "RIPE for 6 months" — Rifampin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol. All four drugs for 2 months, then RI for 4 months.
Nursing Priority: Airborne precautions (N95 respirator, negative pressure room). Directly observed therapy (DOT) to ensure compliance. Monitor LFTs monthly. Teach about hepatotoxicity s/s (jaundice, dark urine, RUQ pain).

HIV/AIDS

Transmission: Blood, semen, vaginal fluids, breast milk, perinatal

NOT transmitted by: Casual contact, sharing utensils, hugging, mosquitos

Acute infection (2-4 weeks post-exposure): Flu-like symptoms, fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, high viral load
Clinical latency: Asymptomatic, CD4+ >200, viral load detectable but lower
AIDS (CD4+ <200 or opportunistic infection): PCP pneumonia, toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal meningitis, Kaposi sarcoma, CMV retinitis, MAI, esophageal candidiasis

Antiretroviral Therapy (ART):

  • NRTIs: Tenofovir, emtricitabine — mitochondrial toxicity (lactic acidosis, hepatic steatosis)
  • NNRTIs: Efavirenz — CNS effects (vivid dreams), hepatotoxicity, rash
  • PIs: Darunavir, atazanavir — hyperlipidemia, hyperglycemia, lipodystrophy
  • INSTIs: Dolutegravir, bictegravir — well-tolerated, preferred first-line
Nursing Priority: Adherence is CRITICAL (≥95% to prevent resistance). Monitor CD4+ count and viral load. Screen for opportunistic infections. Prophylaxis: TMP-SMX for PCP (CD4+ <200), azithromycin for MAI (CD4+ <50).
NCLEX Trap: Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) — paradoxical worsening of OI symptoms after starting ART (inflammatory response to pathogens). Do NOT stop ART.

📋 Antibiotic Stewardship

Core Principles

  • Obtain cultures BEFORE starting antibiotics when possible
  • Use narrowest spectrum antibiotic effective for the infection
  • De-escalate based on culture results (24-72 hours)
  • Discontinue when infection is treated (don't extend "just in case")
  • Document indication, expected duration, plan for reassessment
Key Nursing Actions: Verify antibiotic indication and appropriateness. Check for allergies. Monitor for adverse effects. Ensure proper administration timing (especially time-dependent vs concentration-dependent antibiotics). Educate patients on completing the full course.
