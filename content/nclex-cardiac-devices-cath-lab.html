NCLEX Cardiac Devices & Cath Lab

Pacemakers, ICDs, cardiac catheterization, stents, and post-procedure care for NCLEX success.

1. Pacemakers

📋 Pacemaker Basics

TypeChambers PacedIndications
AAIAtriumSick sinus syndrome with intact AV conduction
VVIVentricleChronic AF with slow ventricular response, AV block
DDDDual (A + V)AV block, sick sinus syndrome (most common)
Biventricular (CRT)RA + RV + LVHeart failure with wide QRS, EF ≤35%
💡 NBG Pacemaker Code (first 3 letters):
1st letter: Chamber paced (A=atrium, V=ventricle, D=dual)
2nd letter: Chamber sensed (A, V, D)
3rd letter: Response to sensing (T=triggered, I=inhibited, D=dual)

📋 Pacemaker Assessment

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Pacer spike: Small vertical deflection on ECG before P wave (atrial) or QRS (ventricular)
• Capture: Pacer spike followed by depolarization (P wave or QRS)
• Failure to capture: Spike without depolarization → check settings, battery, lead position
• Failure to pace: No spike when expected → battery depletion, oversensing
• Magnet response: Magnet over generator → asynchronous pacing (fixed rate)
• Check pulse: Pacer set rate vs actual pulse (if different = problem)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: A patient with a pacemaker who develops hiccups may have diaphragmatic stimulation (pacer lead stimulating phrenic nerve). Also, NEVER place BP cuff on same arm as pacemaker (especially temporary pacemaker) — can dislodge leads. For permanent pacemakers, avoid arm abduction >90° for 4–6 weeks.

2. ICDs (Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator)

📋 ICD Overview

Indications: Survivor of cardiac arrest, sustained VT, EF ≤35% (NYHA II-III), hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with risk factors, inherited arrhythmia syndromes
✅ ICD Nursing Priorities:
• Device check: Regular interrogation (every 3–6 months, remote monitoring)
• Shock delivered: Assess patient (LOC, rhythm), comfort, document event, notify provider
• Multiple shocks: VT storm — call provider, prepare for antiarrhythmics, sedation
• Inactivation: Magnet placed over ICD disables SHOCK therapy (pacing continues)
• Electromagnetic interference: MRI-conditional devices only in approved scanners
• Patient education: Avoid strong magnets, arc welding, chain saws; cell phone 6 inches from device
🚨 NCLEX Trap: During CPR on a patient with an ICD, continue compressions even if ICD fires. The ICD may deliver shocks during compressions — this is safe for rescuers with standard PPE. Remove AED pads at least 1 inch from device. After any shock (ICD or AED), resume CPR immediately (2 min before rhythm check).

3. Cardiac Catheterization

📋 Pre-Procedure Care

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Verify informed consent
• NPO 6–8 hours (except medications with sips of water)
• Labs: BUN/Cr, CBC, coags, type & screen
• Allergy assessment (iodinated contrast — shellfish allergy is NOT a true contraindication, but ask about prior contrast reaction)
• IV access (large bore)
• Mark peripheral pulses (for post-procedure comparison)
• Pre-procedure medications: antiplatelets (aspirin, clopidogrel), sedation
• Void before procedure
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Metformin is held 48 hours before and after cardiac cath with contrast (risk lactic acidosis in renal impairment). Always check creatinine before procedure. Hydration before and after contrast protects kidneys.

📋 Post-Procedure Care (Femoral Access)

✅ Immediate Post-Cath Priorities:
• Bed rest: Flat 4–6 hours (femoral); shorter for radial access
• Leg straight: No bending at hip
• Pressure: Manual or closure device; sandbag if needed
• Vital signs: q15min × 4, q30min × 4, then q1h
• Pulse checks: Distal to insertion site (foot pulses) compare to baseline
• Site inspection: Bleeding, hematoma, bruising
• Monitor for complications: See below

📋 Post-Cath Complications

🚨 NCLEX Trap: The most dangerous post-cath complication is RETROPERITONEAL BLEEDING (from high femoral puncture above inguinal ligament). Signs are subtle: back pain, hypotension, dropping Hct without obvious groin bleeding. This is a surgical emergency. Always suspect this if a post-cath patient becomes hypotensive without obvious external bleeding.

4. Coronary Stents

📋 Post-Stent Care

✅ Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT):
• Bare metal stent (BMS): Aspirin + clopidogrel minimum 1 month (ideally 12 months)
• Drug-eluting stent (DES): Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel, ticagrelor, prasugrel) minimum 12 months
• Never discontinue DAPT early without cardiology approval — risk stent thrombosis (MI/death)
• If surgery needed: continue aspirin; discuss P2Y12 with cardiologist
🚨 NCLEX Trap: A patient with a recent stent who needs non-cardiac surgery — the priority is consulting cardiology about DAPT management. Stopping antiplatelets within 3–6 months of DES placement for surgery carries high stent thrombosis risk. Bridging with short-acting agents may be needed.

5. Patient Education

📋 Discharge Teaching

✅ Post-Cath:
• No heavy lifting (>10 lb) or straining for 1 week
• Keep insertion site clean and dry
• Watch for bleeding, swelling, redness, fever, cold/painful limb
• Take antiplatelet medications as prescribed (CRITICAL)
• Hydrate well (contrast elimination)
• Follow-up appointment
✅ Pacemaker/ICD:
• Carry device ID card
• Report dizziness, syncope, palpitations, hiccups
• Cell phone on opposite side, 6 inches from device
• Avoid MRI unless device is MRI-conditional
• Airport security: walk through quickly; hand wand can be used briefly away from device
• Return to normal activity gradually; avoid contact sports
