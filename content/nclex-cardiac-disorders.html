NCLEX Cardiac Disorders: Complete Study Guide

Heart failure, MI, arrhythmias, hypertension, and valve disease — pathophysiology, nursing priorities, medications, and NCLEX traps.

1. Heart Failure

📋 Left-Sided Heart Failure (Most Common)

Symptoms — "LUNGS"
Lung crackles | Upright breathing (orthopnea) | Nocturnal dyspnea | Gasping (tachypnea) | S3 gallop

Patho: Left ventricle can't pump effectively → blood backs up into lungs → pulmonary congestion.

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• High Fowler's position (reduce preload)
• O₂ therapy (2-6L NC)
• Daily weights (same scale, same time)
• Strict I&O
• Monitor for pulmonary edema (pink frothy sputum = emergency!)
• Medications: Loop diuretics, ACE-I/ARB, beta-blockers, digoxin

📋 Right-Sided Heart Failure

Symptoms — "SWELLING"
Swelling (peripheral edema) | Weight gain | Elevated JVD | Liver congestion (hepatomegaly) | Large abdomen (ascites) | Intolerance to exercise | Nocturia | GI symptoms

Patho: Right ventricle can't pump effectively → blood backs up into systemic circulation → peripheral edema, JVD, hepatomegaly.

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Elevate legs (but NOT if left-sided failure present!)
• Sodium restriction (2g/day)
• Fluid restriction (if severe)
• Monitor liver function, abdominal girth
• Daily weights
⚠️ NCLEX TRAP: A patient with BIVENTRICULAR failure has symptoms of BOTH. The NCLEX may show peripheral edema (right) AND crackles (left). Don't assume one or the other — assess all symptoms.

2. Myocardial Infarction (MI)

📋 STEMI vs NSTEMI

STEMINSTEMI
ECGST elevationST depression or T-wave inversion
TroponinElevatedElevated
TreatmentEmergency PCI within 90 minMedical management, possible PCI within 24-72h
MortalityHigherLower but still serious
🧠 MNEMONIC: "MONA" — Initial MI Treatment
Morphine (pain relief, reduce catecholamines)
Oxygen (if SpO₂ <90%)
Nitroglycerin (vasodilation, reduce afterload)
Aspirin (antiplatelet, 325 mg chewable)
Note: MONA is for symptom relief. Definitive treatment is reperfusion (PCI or thrombolytics).
✅ Nursing Priorities for MI:
• Continuous ECG monitoring
• IV access, labs (troponin, CBC, CMP, PT/INR, aPTT)
• Pain assessment and management
• MONA protocol
• Monitor for complications: arrhythmias, cardiogenic shock, papillary muscle rupture, VSD
• Bed rest initially, then gradual activity
⚠️ NCLEX TRAP: Aspirin and Nitroglycerin are both given for MI, but give aspirin FIRST (chewed for faster absorption). Nitroglycerin is contraindicated if SBP <90 mmHg or if patient has taken sildenafil (Viagra) within 24 hours.

4. Hypertension

📋 Hypertensive Crisis

Hypertensive UrgencyHypertensive Emergency
BP>180/120>180/120
Target Organ DamageNoneYes (encephalopathy, MI, renal failure, aortic dissection)
Reduction GoalGradual over 24-48 hoursReduce MAP by 10-20% in first hour, 5-15% over next 23 hours
RouteOral medsIV meds (labetalol, nicardipine, nitroprusside)
⚠️ NCLEX TRAP: NEVER lower BP too rapidly in hypertensive emergency. Rapid reduction can cause cerebral ischemia, MI, or renal failure. The goal is controlled, gradual reduction.

🧩 NGN Case Study: Acute Coronary Syndrome

Client Scenario

Ms. Williams, 58-year-old female, arrives at the ED complaining of crushing chest pain that started 2 hours ago while gardening. She describes it as "pressure" radiating to her left jaw and arm. She is diaphoretic and nauseated. History includes hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and smoking (1 pack/day × 30 years).

Vital Signs 12-Lead ECG Labs

Vital Signs: BP 168/96, HR 102, RR 20, Temp 37.0°C, SpO₂ 94% on room air

ECG: ST-segment elevation in leads V1-V4, reciprocal ST depression in leads II, III, aVF. QRS narrow.

Labs: Troponin I 0.8 ng/mL (rising), CK-MB 12 U/L, BNP 180 pg/mL, K⁺ 4.2, Mg²⁺ 1.8

Question 1 — Recognize Cues: What is the priority nursing action within the first 10 minutes?

  • A. Obtain a detailed pain history
  • B. Administer aspirin 325mg (if not given by EMS) and establish IV access
  • C. Prepare the patient for immediate cardiac catheterization
  • D. Give sublingual nitroglycerin 0.4mg every 5 minutes

Correct: B. Administer aspirin 325mg (if not given by EMS) and establish IV access

For suspected STEMI, the door-to-aspirin time should be <10 minutes. Aspirin reduces mortality by preventing further platelet aggregation. IV access is needed for heparin, nitrates, and potential thrombolytics. While pain history and cath lab prep are important, aspirin is the single most time-sensitive intervention. Nitroglycerin is given AFTER aspirin, and only if SBP >90 and no RV infarct (which would cause hypotension with nitrates).

Question 2 — Analyze Cues: The ECG shows anterior STEMI (V1-V4). Which complication should the nurse monitor for most closely?

  • A. Right ventricular infarction
  • B. Left ventricular free wall rupture
  • C. Papillary muscle rupture causing acute mitral regurgitation
  • D. Cardiogenic shock from extensive anterior wall damage

Correct: D. Cardiogenic shock from extensive anterior wall damage

Anterior wall MI (LAD territory) affects a large area of the left ventricle and has the highest risk of cardiogenic shock (Killip Class IV). The anterior wall comprises the largest portion of LV mass. Monitor for hypotension, tachycardia, cool extremities, decreased urine output, and altered mental status. RV infarct is associated with inferior MI ( RCA territory). Free wall rupture and papillary muscle rupture are possible but less common than cardiogenic shock in anterior STEMI.

Question 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses: After aspirin, the provider orders morphine 2-4mg IV, metoprolol 25mg PO, and nitroglycerin drip at 10 mcg/min. The nurse should question which order?

  • A. Morphine — should be avoided in MI due to masking symptoms
  • B. Metoprolol — contraindicated with HR 102 and BP 168/96
  • C. Nitroglycerin — patient has crushing chest pain, appropriate
  • D. No orders need to be questioned; all are appropriate

Correct: D. No orders need to be questioned; all are appropriate

All three medications are standard ACS/STEMI management: Morphine for pain relief (reduces catecholamine surge and myocardial O₂ demand), metoprolol for rate control and afterload reduction (HR 102 and BP 168/96 are appropriate for beta-blockade — hold only if HR <60, SBP <100, or signs of shock), nitroglycerin for coronary vasodilation and afterload reduction. This is the classic MONA-B therapy (Morphine, Oxygen, Nitrates, Aspirin, Beta-blocker).

Question 4 — Generate Solutions: The patient suddenly becomes unresponsive. The monitor shows ventricular fibrillation. The crash cart is brought in. What is the correct medication sequence?

  • A. Defibrillate → epinephrine 1mg → amiodarone 300mg
  • B. Epinephrine 1mg → defibrillate → amiodarone 300mg
  • C. Defibrillate → CPR 2 min → epinephrine → defibrillate → amiodarone
  • D. Atropine 1mg → defibrillate → epinephrine 1mg

Correct: C. Defibrillate → CPR 2 min → epinephrine → defibrillate → amiodarone

ACLS pulseless VF/VT algorithm: 1) Defibrillate immediately (200J biphasic), 2) Begin CPR, 3) After 2 minutes, give epinephrine 1mg IV/IO, 4) Continue CPR 2 min, 5) Defibrillate again, 6) Give amiodarone 300mg IV/IO. CPR compressions should be minimally interrupted. Atropine is NOT used in pulseless arrest — it's only for symptomatic bradycardia.

Question 5 — Take Action: The patient is successfully cardioverted and stabilized. The cardiologist recommends primary PCI within 90 minutes. What is the nurse's priority action while preparing for cath lab?

  • A. Give a loading dose of clopidogrel 600mg
  • B. Insert a Foley catheter
  • C. Give IV heparin bolus per protocol
  • D. Obtain informed consent for the procedure

Correct: C. Give IV heparin bolus per protocol

For STEMI going to primary PCI, anticoagulation is critical to prevent further thrombus formation. Heparin is given per protocol (typically 60-100 units/kg bolus) to achieve therapeutic ACT in the cath lab. Clopidogrel loading may be given but anticoagulation takes priority. Foley insertion is not emergent and can be done in cath lab. Informed consent is obtained by the provider, not the nurse — though the nurse verifies understanding.

Question 6 — Evaluate Outcomes: Post-PCI, the patient has a drug-eluting stent placed in the LAD. She is stable on the cardiac step-down unit. Which discharge instruction is highest priority?

  • A. "You can resume smoking in moderation after 2 weeks"
  • B. "Take aspirin and clopidogrel together daily for at least 12 months"
  • C. "Avoid all physical activity for 6 months"
  • D. "You can stop the aspirin once chest pain resolves"

Correct: B. "Take aspirin and clopidogrel together daily for at least 12 months"

Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) — aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel, ticagrelor, or prasugrel) — is MANDATORY for 12 months after drug-eluting stent placement to prevent stent thrombosis, which has a 40-60% mortality rate. Premature discontinuation is a leading cause of post-PCI mortality. Option A is wrong — smoking must stop completely. Option C is wrong — cardiac rehab begins within 1-2 weeks. Option D is wrong — aspirin is lifelong.
