CASE: Post-Op Patient on Med-Surg Unit

Patient: Mrs. Johnson, 68-year-old female, post-op day 2 after total knee replacement

Report from night shift:

  • Vitals stable overnight
  • PCA morphine used 4 times
  • Dressing clean, dry, intact
  • Refusing to ambulate — "I'm too tired"

Current assessment (0800):

VitalValueNormal Range
Temperature38.2°C (100.8°F)36.5-37.5°C
Heart Rate104 bpm60-100
Blood Pressure98/62 mmHg90-140/60-90
Respiratory Rate22/min12-20
O2 Saturation94% on room air95-100%
Pain (0-10)6/100-3 acceptable

Additional data:

  • incision: red, warm, small amount of serosanguineous drainage
  • Lung sounds: diminished bilaterally, no crackles
  • Patient anxious, wants to know "why I feel so awful"
  • Last ambulated: yesterday afternoon (16 hours ago)
  • Urinary output: 180 mL last 8 hours (catheter removed 6 hours ago)
  • WBC: 14,200 (elevated)

Question: Which action should the nurse take FIRST?

Step-by-Step CJMM Analysis

Step 1: Recognize Cues

What data is RELEVANT vs. what is DISTRACTING?

Nurse Thinking:
"Let me sort through everything. What's abnormal? What's expected post-op?"
ABNORMAL Cues🟢 Expected/Normal
Temp 38.2°C (low-grade fever)Post-op day 2, some pain expected
HR 104 (tachycardia)Red, warm incision early post-op
BP 98/62 (low-normal)Some serosanguineous drainage normal
RR 22 (tachypnea)Anxiety about surgery
O2 sat 94% (slightly low)
Pain 6/10 (uncontrolled)
WBC 14,200 (elevated)
Urine output 180 mL/8hr (LOW)
Not ambulated for 16 hours

Step 2: Analyze Cues

What PATTERNS do the abnormal cues form?

Nurse Thinking:
"Fever + tachycardia + tachypnea + low urine output + elevated WBC... This looks like systemic response to something. Infection? Sepsis? Let's think about the timing."

Pattern Recognition:

  • Temp ↑ + HR ↑ + RR ↑ + WBC ↑ = Systemic inflammatory response
  • Low urine output (180 mL/8hr = 22.5 mL/hr) = Possible early sepsis (kidneys compensating)
  • BP 98/62 = Hypotension (baselines normally higher in 68yo)
  • O2 sat 94% = Early hypoxia
  • Incision red, warm = Local infection possible source
  • Not ambulated = Stasis, atelectasis risk

Putting it together: Patient shows signs of early sepsis from possible surgical site infection. The tachycardia, tachypnea, fever, and low urine output are compensatory mechanisms. This is not "just post-op discomfort."

Step 3: Prioritize Hypotheses

What's the MOST LIKELY and MOST URGENT problem?

Nurse Thinking:
"What could this be? Rank by urgency and likelihood."
HypothesisLikelihoodUrgency
Sepsis from surgical site infectionHigh CRITICAL
Atelectasis from immobilityModerate🟡 Important
DehydrationLow🟢 Monitor
Normal post-op recoveryVery Low Not applicable

Priority: SEPSIS — This patient meets SIRS criteria (Temp >38, HR >90, RR >20, WBC >12,000). With a possible infectious source (surgical site), sepsis is the priority.

Step 4: Generate Solutions

What actions should the nurse take?

Nurse Thinking:
"If this is sepsis, what does the evidence say to do FIRST?"

Sepsis Bundle (Evidence-Based):

  1. Obtain blood cultures (before antibiotics)
  2. Start IV antibiotics (within 1 hour of recognition)
  3. Fluid resuscitation (30 mL/kg crystalloid)
  4. Monitor lactate
  5. Notify provider (sepsis alert)
  6. Continue monitoring (vitals q15min)

Step 5: Take Action

Which action is the nurse's FIRST priority?

Nurse Thinking:
"The NCLEX wants to know what I do FIRST. Not what I do eventually — what I do RIGHT NOW."

Answer options:

A. Encourage patient to ambulate to prevent atelectasis
B. Administer PRN pain medication
C. Change the surgical dressing
D. Notify the provider and initiate sepsis protocol
Why D is correct:
This patient has SIRS + possible infectious source = sepsis until proven otherwise. The sepsis bundle requires provider notification and immediate intervention. Ambulation, pain medication, and dressing changes can wait. Sepsis cannot.

Step 6: Evaluate Outcomes

How do we know our actions worked?

Nurse Thinking:
"What should I expect to see if my interventions are effective?"
Expected OutcomeTimeline
Temperature returns to normal range24-48 hours after antibiotics
Heart rate <100 bpmWithin 2-4 hours of fluid resuscitation
Blood pressure stabilizes (>90 systolic)Within 1-2 hours
Urine output >0.5 mL/kg/hrWithin 2-3 hours

If outcomes are NOT met: Escalate care. Consider ICU transfer, vasopressors, central line placement.

Key Takeaways

What This Case Tests

  • Pattern recognition: Can you see SIRS criteria when presented together?
  • Prioritization: Can you identify the life-threatening problem vs. the discomfort?
  • Evidence-based practice: Do you know the sepsis bundle?
  • Clinical judgment: Not "what do you know?" but "how do you THINK?"

The NGN NCLEX doesn't ask "What are the SIRS criteria?" It gives you a patient and asks "What do you do?"

That's why the CJMM framework matters. It trains you to think like a nurse, not memorize like a student.
