COPD & Asthma Deep Dive

Pathophysiology, exacerbations, medications, oxygen therapy, and nursing management for COPD and asthma on the NCLEX.

🌬️ COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)

Types & Pathophysiology

Emphysema: Destruction of alveolar walls → loss of elastic recoil → air trapping. Pink puffer (thin, barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing, non-productive cough).

Chronic Bronchitis: Hypersecretion of mucus, inflamed airways. Blue bloater (overweight, cyanotic, productive cough, edema, signs of cor pulmonale).

Mnemonic: "Blue Bloater, Pink Puffer" — Blue bloater = bronchitis (cyanotic, edema). Pink puffer = emphysema (not cyanotic, thin, pursed-lip breathing).

COPD Exacerbation

Triggers: Infection (most common), air pollution, weather changes, non-adherence to therapy
Manifestations: Increased dyspnea, increased cough/sputum, sputum purulence, wheezing, tachypnea, use of accessory muscles, confusion (hypercapnia)
Management:
  1. Bronchodilators: SABA (albuterol) + SAMA (ipratropium) nebulizers
  2. Corticosteroids: Systemic (prednisone 40 mg x 5 days)
  3. Antibiotics: If 2+ Cardinal symptoms (increased dyspnea, sputum volume, purulence) OR ventilator support needed
  4. Oxygen: Target SpO₂ 88-92% (NOT 94-100% — risk of CO₂ retention and respiratory acidosis)
  5. Non-invasive ventilation (BiPAP): For acute respiratory acidosis (pH ≤7.35 + PaCO₂ >45)
  6. Mechanical ventilation: If NIV fails or contraindicated
NCLEX Trap: In COPD, give LOW-FLOW oxygen (2 L NC) to target 88-92%. High-flow O₂ suppresses hypoxic drive (these patients rely on hypoxemia to stimulate breathing). Monitor ABGs. Watch for signs of CO₂ narcosis (somnolence, confusion, headache).

COPD Medications

Class Examples Notes
SABA Albuterol, levalbuterol Rescue inhaler. Tremor, tachycardia.
SAMA Ipratropium Dry mouth, urinary retention. First-line for COPD.
LABA Salmeterol, formoterol NOT monotherapy — must combine with ICS
LAMA Tiotropium, umeclidinium First-line maintenance for COPD
ICS Fluticasone, budesonide For frequent exacerbations. Rinse mouth after use.
PDE-4 inhibitor Roflumilast Severe COPD with chronic bronchitis. Weight loss, diarrhea.

🫁 Asthma

Pathophysiology

Chronic inflammatory disorder of airways characterized by:

  • Bronchospasm (smooth muscle contraction)
  • Mucosal edema
  • Increased mucus production
  • Airway hyperresponsiveness

Reversible (unlike COPD) with treatment

Asthma Classification

Severity Daytime Symptoms Nighttime Symptoms Lung Function
Intermittent ≤2 days/week ≤2x/month FEV₁ ≥80%
Persistent (Mild) >2 days/week 3-4x/month FEV₁ ≥80%
Persistent (Moderate) Daily >1x/week FEV₁ 60-80%
Persistent (Severe) Throughout the day Frequent FEV₁ <60%

Acute Asthma Exacerbation

Severe exacerbation signs:
  • Unable to speak in full sentences
  • Accessory muscle use, tripod position
  • Respiratory rate >30
  • Heart rate >120
  • PEF <50% predicted
  • Pulse oximetry <90%
  • Silent chest (ominous — severe airway obstruction)
  • Altered consciousness
Management:
  1. Oxygen: Target SpO₂ 94-98% (asthma patients do NOT have chronic hypercapnia)
  2. SABA: Albuterol nebulizer or MDI with spacer (every 20 min x 3, then reassess)
  3. Ipratropium bromide: Add to SABA (reduces hospitalization)
  4. Systemic steroids: Prednisone/prednisolone within 1 hour (oral = IV for most patients)
  5. Magnesium sulfate: 2 g IV over 20 min (if severe, not responding to initial therapy)
  6. BiPAP/Intubation: If impending respiratory failure
NCLEX Trap: In asthma, give HIGH-FLOW oxygen (target 94-98%) — these patients are NOT CO₂ retainers. Do NOT give sedatives (mask respiratory failure). Peak flow <50% = severe. Intubate if altered mental status, exhaustion, or refractory hypoxemia.

Status Asthmaticus

Definition: Severe asthma that persists despite standard treatment (SABA + corticosteroids)

Management:
  • Continuous nebulized albuterol
  • High-dose systemic steroids
  • Magnesium sulfate
  • Heliox (reduces work of breathing)
  • Mechanical ventilation if needed (permissive hypercapnia strategy)
  • Ketamine (bronchodilator + sedation for intubation)

🩺 Long-Term Management

Asthma Action Plan (Green/Yellow/Red Zones)

  • Green (Doing Well): PEF 80-100% predicted. Continue daily controller.
  • Yellow (Caution): PEF 50-80%. Increase SABA, may increase ICS, contact provider.
  • Red (Medical Alert): PEF <50%. Take rescue inhaler immediately, call provider or 911.
Controller vs Reliever: Controllers (ICS, LABA+ICS combo) are taken DAILY to prevent inflammation. Relievers (SABA) are used as needed for acute symptoms. Patient education: Do NOT overuse SABA (>2x/week indicates poor control).
