Dialysis & Renal Replacement

Hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, CRRT, kidney transplant, and nursing management for the NCLEX.

🩸 Hemodialysis (HD)

Principles

Diffusion + Ultrafiltration: Blood passes through dialyzer semipermeable membrane. Waste products (urea, creatinine, K⁺) diffuse from blood to dialysate. Fluid removed by pressure gradient (ultrafiltration).

Vascular Access:

  • AV Fistula (preferred): Artery + vein anastomosis. Maturation 4-12 weeks. Lowest infection risk, longest patency.
  • AV Graft: Synthetic tube. Maturation 2-4 weeks. Higher infection/thrombosis risk than fistula.
  • Central Venous Catheter: Immediate use. Highest infection risk. Used for acute dialysis or when permanent access not ready.
AV Fistula/Graft Care:
  • Assess thrill (palpable vibration) and bruit (auscultated) — indicates patency
  • Do NOT measure BP, draw blood, or start IV in fistula arm
  • Assess for signs of infection, stenosis, thrombosis
  • Elevate arm post-cannulation to reduce bleeding
  • Apply pressure to needle sites 10-15 min after treatment

Complications

Intradialytic Hypotension: Most common complication. Caused by rapid fluid removal, autonomic dysfunction, cardiac issues. Management: Trendelenburg position, reduce ultrafiltration rate, give normal saline bolus, consider albumin. Prevention: Sodium modeling, cool dialysate, assess dry weight accurately.
Muscle Cramps: Caused by rapid fluid/electrolyte shifts. Management: Reduce ultrafiltration, give hypertonic saline or mannitol, massage, stretch.
Disequilibrium Syndrome: Acute cerebral edema from rapid solute removal. Manifestations: Headache, nausea, vomiting, confusion, seizures, coma (during or after dialysis). Prevention: Slow initial dialysis (reduced time, reduced blood flow). Treatment: Mannitol, anticonvulsants, supportive care.
Hemorrhage: From access site or systemic (heparin). Management: Apply direct pressure, reverse heparin with protamine if needed.

Pre-Dialysis Assessment

  • Vital signs (assess for hypotension, fever)
  • Weight (compare to dry weight)
  • Access assessment (thrill, bruit, signs of infection)
  • Review labs (K⁺, Na⁺, BUN, Cr, Hgb, albumin)
  • Assess edema, lung sounds, JVD
NCLEX Trap: Hold antihypertensives before dialysis (risk of hypotension). Check K⁺ before dialysis — if >6.5 or ECG changes, may need urgent dialysis. Heparin is commonly used during HD — assess for bleeding.

💧 Peritoneal Dialysis (PD)

Principles

Diffusion + Osmosis: Dialysate infused into peritoneal cavity. Waste products diffuse from blood (peritoneal capillaries) to dialysate. Fluid removed by osmotic gradient (dextrose in dialysate).

Types:

  • CAPD (Continuous Ambulatory): Manual exchanges 4-5x/day, dwell time 4-6 hours
  • APD (Automated): Cycler machine performs exchanges overnight, last dwell during day

Complications

Peritonitis (most serious):
Manifestations: Cloudy dialysate effluent, abdominal pain, fever, nausea, rebound tenderness
Management:
  • Send effluent for cell count, Gram stain, culture
  • WBC >100 with >50% neutrophils = peritonitis
  • Empiric antibiotics in dialysate (cover gram-positive + gram-negative)
  • Adjust based on culture results
Prevention: Strict aseptic technique, prophylactic antibiotics before invasive procedures (colonoscopy, dental), treat exit site infections promptly.
Exit Site/Tunnel Infection: Erythema, drainage, tenderness at catheter site. Treat with topical + oral antibiotics. May require catheter removal if tunnel infection.
Catheter Malfunction: Outflow failure. Causes: constipation (most common), catheter kinking, omentum wrapping, fibrin clot. Treat constipation first. Heparin flush for fibrin.
Hernia: Increased intra-abdominal pressure. Common at previous surgical sites, umbilicus, inguinal area. May require surgical repair.

PD Nursing Care

  • Assess exit site daily for redness, drainage, tenderness
  • Monitor effluent (should be clear, pale yellow)
  • Weigh daily, monitor BP, assess edema
  • Ensure proper dwell time and drain time
  • Monitor for signs of fluid overload or dehydration
  • Patient education: Aseptic technique, recognizing peritonitis, catheter care
NCLEX Trap: Warm dialysate to body temperature before infusion (prevents abdominal pain, vasodilation). Do NOT use hypertonic (4.25% dextrose) routinely (damages peritoneum). Monitor blood sugar in diabetic patients (dextrose absorption).

⚡ Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT)

Indications

Hemodynamically unstable patients who cannot tolerate hemodialysis (ICU setting)

  • Acute kidney injury with hemodynamic instability
  • Severe electrolyte disturbances
  • Fluid overload with pulmonary edema
  • Metabolic acidosis
  • Drug overdose (dialyzable toxins)
  • Rhabdomyolysis
Advantages over HD: Slower fluid and solute removal → better hemodynamic tolerance. Continuous → avoids peaks and troughs. Can provide nutritional support without fluid restriction.

🫘 Kidney Transplant

Post-Transplant Care

Immediate Post-Op:
  • Monitor urine output (should increase immediately) — diuresis expected
  • Monitor creatinine (should decrease)
  • Assess for bleeding (drain output, hematuria)
  • Monitor BP (hypertension common)
  • Fluids: Match output (may need aggressive hydration)
Immunosuppression:
  • Tacrolimus or cyclosporine: Calcineurin inhibitors. Nephrotoxic, neurotoxic (tremor), hypertension, hyperglycemia. Monitor trough levels.
  • Mycophenolate: Antiproliferative. Bone marrow suppression (CBC monitoring), GI upset.
  • Prednisone: Corticosteroid. Hyperglycemia, weight gain, osteoporosis, infection risk, peptic ulcer.
  • Sirolimus/everolimus: mTOR inhibitors. Hyperlipidemia, delayed wound healing, mouth ulcers.
Rejection:
  • Acute: Fever, oliguria, elevated creatinine, graft tenderness, hypertension. Treat with high-dose steroids or anti-thymocyte globulin.
  • Chronic: Gradual decline in function. No specific treatment.
NCLEX Trap: Transplant patients are immunosuppressed — AVOID live vaccines. Report fever >100.4°F immediately (may be only sign of infection). Monitor for CMV (fever, leukopenia, elevated LFTs) and BK virus (nephropathy). Do NOT give NSAIDs (nephrotoxic + interfere with immunosuppressants).
