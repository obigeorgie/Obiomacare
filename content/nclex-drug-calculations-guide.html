NCLEX Drug Calculations: Complete Guide

Master dosage calculations with dimensional analysis. Covers oral meds, IV drips, weight-based dosing, reconstitution, and pediatric calculations.

The Universal Formula: Dimensional Analysis

Dimensional analysis (DA) is the most reliable method for drug calculations. Set up your equation so units cancel out, leaving only the desired unit.

Desired Dose × Available Volume / Available Dose = Volume to Administer

Or using dimensional analysis:
mL to give = (Desired / Have) × (Quantity / 1)

1. Oral Medication Calculations

Example 1: Tablet Calculation

Order: Metoprolol 50 mg PO daily
Available: Metoprolol 25 mg tablets

Step 1: Set up the equation
? tablets = (50 mg / 25 mg) × 1 tablet = 2 tablets
Give 2 tablets

Example 2: Liquid Medication

Order: Amoxicillin 375 mg PO TID
Available: Amoxicillin 250 mg/5 mL

Step 1: ? mL = (375 mg / 250 mg) × 5 mL
Step 2: = 1.5 × 5 mL = 7.5 mL
Give 7.5 mL per dose

2. IV Drip Rate Calculations

mL/hr = Total Volume (mL) / Total Time (hours)

gtt/min = (Volume × Drop Factor) / Time (minutes)
Common drop factors: Macrodrip = 10, 15, or 20 gtt/mL | Microdrip = 60 gtt/mL

Example 3: IV Flow Rate (mL/hr)

Order: Infuse 1000 mL NS over 8 hours

Step 1: mL/hr = 1000 mL / 8 hours = 125 mL/hr
Set pump to 125 mL/hr

Example 4: IV Drip Rate (gtt/min)

Order: Infuse 500 mL LR over 4 hours using macrodrip tubing (15 gtt/mL)

Step 1: Convert hours to minutes: 4 hours × 60 = 240 minutes
Step 2: gtt/min = (500 mL × 15 gtt/mL) / 240 min
Step 3: = 7500 / 240 = 31.25 → 31 gtt/min
Set drip rate to 31 gtt/min

3. Weight-Based Dosing

First: Convert lbs to kg → kg = lbs / 2.2
Then: Dose = mg/kg × Patient's weight in kg

Example 5: Weight-Based Dose

Order: Cefazolin 25 mg/kg IV pre-op
Patient: 154 lbs

Step 1: Convert weight: 154 lbs / 2.2 = 70 kg
Step 2: Calculate dose: 25 mg/kg × 70 kg = 1750 mg
Administer 1750 mg of Cefazolin

Example 6: mcg/kg/min (Critical Care)

Order: Dopamine 5 mcg/kg/min
Patient: 80 kg
Available: Dopamine 400 mg in 250 mL D5W

Step 1: Find concentration: 400 mg / 250 mL = 1.6 mg/mL = 1600 mcg/mL
Step 2: Desired dose per minute: 5 mcg/kg × 80 kg = 400 mcg/min
Step 3: mL/hr = (400 mcg/min × 60 min) / 1600 mcg/mL
Step 4: = 24000 / 1600 = 15 mL/hr
Set pump to 15 mL/hr

4. Reconstitution

Example 7: Reconstitution

Order: Penicillin G 400,000 units IM
Available: Penicillin G 1,000,000 units powder. Reconstitute with 4.6 mL diluent for final concentration of 200,000 units/mL

Step 1: ? mL = 400,000 units / 200,000 units/mL = 2 mL
Reconstitute, then withdraw 2 mL

5. Pediatric Calculations

KEY RULE: Always double-check pediatric calculations. Most pediatric medication errors are 10× dosing errors. When in doubt, have another RN verify.

Example 8: Pediatric Dosing (BSA)

Order: Chemotherapy 150 mg/m²
Child: Height 100 cm, Weight 20 kg

Step 1: Calculate BSA using Mosteller formula:
BSA (m²) = √[(Height cm × Weight kg) / 3600]
BSA = √[(100 × 20) / 3600] = √(2000/3600) = √0.556 = 0.75 m²
Step 2: Dose = 150 mg/m² × 0.75 m² = 112.5 mg
Administer 112.5 mg

6. Heparin Protocol Calculations

Example 9: Heparin Drip Titration

Protocol: Increase heparin by 2 units/kg/hr for aPTT 50-59 seconds
Patient: 70 kg, currently receiving 1000 units/hr
aPTT result: 55 seconds (goal 60-80)

Step 1: Increase by: 2 units/kg/hr × 70 kg = 140 units/hr
Step 2: New rate: 1000 + 140 = 1140 units/hr
Step 3: If concentration is 25,000 units in 500 mL = 50 units/mL
Step 4: mL/hr = 1140 / 50 = 22.8 mL/hr
Increase to 1140 units/hr (22.8 mL/hr)

Dimensional Analysis Setup

The beauty of dimensional analysis is that you can set up the entire problem in one line:

? mL = (Desired Dose / Have Dose) × (Quantity / 1) × (Other conversions needed)

Example: Give 750 mg of drug. Available: 1.5 g per 10 mL
? mL = (750 mg / 1) × (1 g / 1000 mg) × (10 mL / 1.5 g) = 5 mL

NGN Case Study: Pediatric Medication Calculation

Client Scenario

Sophie, 4-year-old female, weighs 18 kg and is admitted for a respiratory infection. She requires multiple medications. The nurse must calculate safe doses and verify orders.

Weight Allergies Vital Signs

Weight: 18 kg

Allergies: None known

Vital Signs: BP 92/58, HR 110, RR 28, Temp 38.2°C, SpO₂ 93%

Question 1 — Recognize Cues: The provider orders acetaminophen 240mg PO q6h PRN fever. The safe pediatric dose is 10-15 mg/kg/dose. Is this order safe?

  • A. Yes — 240mg is within the safe range for 18 kg
  • B. No — maximum safe dose is 180mg
  • C. No — minimum safe dose is 270mg
  • D. Cannot determine without knowing the concentration

Correct: A. Yes — 240mg is within the safe range for 18 kg

Calculation: 18 kg × 10 mg/kg = 180 mg (minimum); 18 kg × 15 mg/kg = 270 mg (maximum). Safe range = 180-270 mg per dose. 240 mg is within range. Maximum daily dose = 75 mg/kg/day = 1,350 mg/day for 18 kg. With q6h dosing, maximum 4 doses/day = 960 mg (also safe). The concentration is needed to calculate volume, but the dose itself is safe.

Question 2 — Analyze Cues: Amoxicillin is ordered: 400mg PO BID. The available suspension is 250mg/5mL. How many mL should the nurse administer per dose?

  • A. 5 mL
  • B. 8 mL
  • C. 10 mL
  • D. 16 mL

Correct: B. 8 mL

Using the ratio method: (Desired / Have) × Volume = (400 mg / 250 mg) × 5 mL = 1.6 × 5 = 8 mL. Dimensional analysis: 400 mg × (5 mL / 250 mg) = 8 mL. This is a standard pediatric antibiotic dose. The nurse should verify that 400mg BID is appropriate for the infection being treated (standard for otitis media is 80-90 mg/kg/day divided BID = 720-810 mg/day; 800 mg/day fits).

Question 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses: The provider orders IV fluids: D5½NS at 60 mL/hr. The nurse realizes the standard pediatric maintenance rate is 1,500 mL/day for this weight. Is the ordered rate appropriate?

  • A. Yes — 60 mL/hr × 24 hr = 1,440 mL, which is close to 1,500 mL
  • B. No — the rate should be 62.5 mL/hr for exact maintenance
  • C. No — D5½NS is hypotonic and inappropriate for maintenance
  • D. Cannot determine without knowing the child's hydration status

Correct: A. Yes — 60 mL/hr × 24 hr = 1,440 mL, which is close to 1,500 mL

Using the Holliday-Segar method: For first 10 kg = 100 mL/kg/day = 1,000 mL; for next 10 kg = 50 mL/kg/day = 400 mL (for 8 kg) = 400 mL. Total = 1,400 mL/day. Hourly rate = 1,400 / 24 ≈ 58.3 mL/hr. The ordered 60 mL/hr is reasonable. D5½NS (C) is actually standard maintenance fluid for children (provides glucose and some sodium). Exact precision to 62.5 mL/hr (B) is unnecessary in practice.

Question 4 — Generate Solutions: The provider orders morphine 1.8mg IV q4h PRN pain. The safe pediatric dose is 0.1 mg/kg/dose. The available concentration is 2mg/mL. How many mL should the nurse draw up?

  • A. 0.5 mL
  • B. 0.9 mL
  • C. 1.0 mL
  • D. 1.8 mL

Correct: B. 0.9 mL

First, verify safety: 18 kg × 0.1 mg/kg = 1.8 mg maximum safe dose per dose. The ordered 1.8 mg is at the upper limit of safe. Volume calculation: 1.8 mg ÷ 2 mg/mL = 0.9 mL. This is a very small volume — the nurse should use a 1 mL tuberculin syringe for accuracy. Double-checking with another nurse is recommended for high-alert medications like morphine in pediatric patients.

Question 5 — Take Action: Sophie requires ceftriaxone 900mg IV daily. The vial is 1g (1,000mg) reconstituted in 10mL sterile water (100mg/mL). How many mL should the nurse withdraw?

  • A. 9 mL
  • B. 9.5 mL
  • C. 10 mL
  • D. 0.9 mL

Correct: A. 9 mL

Concentration = 1,000 mg / 10 mL = 100 mg/mL. Volume = 900 mg ÷ 100 mg/mL = 9 mL. This is a reasonable volume for IV administration. Ceftriaxone should be infused over 30 minutes. The nurse should verify the daily dose is appropriate: 50 mg/kg/day × 18 kg = 900 mg/day (standard dosing). For meningitis, the dose would be 100 mg/kg/day divided q12h.

Question 6 — Evaluate Outcomes: The nurse administers all medications correctly. Two hours later, Sophie is sleepy but arousable. Her RR is 22. Which action is most appropriate?

  • A. Continue to monitor — these are expected findings after morphine
  • B. Administer naloxone immediately
  • C. Stop all PRN medications
  • D. Call the provider for a morphine dose reduction

Correct: A. Continue to monitor — these are expected findings after morphine

Sedation and mild respiratory depression are expected side effects of morphine. RR of 22 is still within normal range for a 4-year-old. The child is arousable — this is not overdose. Naloxone (B) is reserved for significant respiratory depression (RR <8 in adults, age-dependent in children) or unresponsiveness. Stopping all PRNs (C) is excessive. Dose reduction (D) may be considered if sedation is excessive but is not the immediate action.
