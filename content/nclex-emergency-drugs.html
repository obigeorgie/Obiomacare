NCLEX Emergency Drugs: Complete Study Guide

ACLS medications, crash cart drugs, antidotes, and reversal agents — indications, dosing, and nursing priorities.

1. ACLS Cardiac Arrest Algorithm

📋 ACLS Medications in Cardiac Arrest

DrugIndicationDoseKey Notes
EpinephrineCardiac arrest (all rhythms), anaphylaxis, severe bradycardia1 mg IV/IO q3-5 min (arrest); 0.5-1 mg IM/IV (anaphylaxis)Vasopressor; increases coronary perfusion; first drug in arrest
AmiodaroneV-fib/pulseless V-tach (after CPR, shock, epi)300 mg IV/IO bolus, then 150 mgAntiarrhythmic; can cause hypotension, bradycardia
LidocaineV-fib/pulseless V-tach (alternative to amiodarone)1-1.5 mg/kg IV, then 0.5-0.75 mg/kgAlternative antiarrhythmic; watch for neurotoxicity
AtropineSymptomatic bradycardia0.5 mg IV q3-5 min (max 3 mg)NOT for asystole or PEA (no longer recommended)
AdenosineStable SVT (narrow-complex tachycardia)6 mg rapid IV push, then 12 mgHalf-life <10 seconds; brief asystole expected; flush immediately
💡 ACLS Arrest Drug Sequence: "Every Alligator Likes Eggs"
Epinephrine | Amiodarone | Lidocaine | (Atropine for bradycardia only)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: In cardiac arrest, CPR and defibrillation come BEFORE drugs. Epinephrine is given q3-5 minutes, not continuously. Amiodarone is given AFTER epinephrine and a shock attempt. Atropine is NOT used in asystole or PEA anymore (removed from ACLS guidelines).

3. Anaphylaxis Treatment

📋 Anaphylaxis Protocol

✅ Immediate Actions (in order):
1. Epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg IM (lateral thigh) — FIRST and MOST IMPORTANT
2. Call for help, position supine with legs elevated
3. O₂ (high-flow)
4. IV access, fluid resuscitation
5. H1 antihistamine (diphenhydramine) — secondary
6. H2 blocker (ranitidine/famotidine) — secondary
7. Corticosteroids (methylprednisolone) — prevents biphasic reaction
8. Nebulized beta-agonist if bronchospasm
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Epinephrine is the ONLY drug that treats anaphylaxis. Antihistamines and steroids are adjuncts — they do NOT treat the life-threatening airway and cardiovascular collapse. Give epinephrine IM (not SQ) in the lateral thigh for fastest absorption. Repeat q5-15 min if needed.

🧩 NGN Case Study: Code Blue Pharmacology

Client Scenario

Mr. Jackson, 72-year-old male, is on the medical floor post-MI day 3. He is on telemetry when the alarm sounds: HR 38, BP 62/40. The nurse finds him unresponsive, gasping, with no palpable pulse. A code blue is called.

Rhythm Strip Vital Signs Current Meds

Rhythm: Third-degree AV block with ventricular escape rhythm at 35 bpm

Vital Signs: BP 58/36 (non-invasive), HR 35, SpO₂ 78% on room air

Current Meds: Metoprolol 50mg PO BID, atorvastatin 40mg PO daily, aspirin 81mg PO daily, heparin infusion

Question 1 — Recognize Cues: What is the first medication the nurse should prepare during this code?

  • A. Atropine 1mg IV
  • B. Epinephrine 1mg IV
  • C. Amiodarone 300mg IV
  • D. Adenosine 6mg rapid IV push

Correct: A. Atropine 1mg IV

For symptomatic bradycardia (HR <50 with hypotension and altered mental status), ACLS protocol is: atropine 1mg IV every 3-5 minutes (max 3mg) FIRST. Atropine blocks vagal stimulation and increases SA/AV node firing. Epinephrine is used if atropine is ineffective. Amiodarone is for ventricular arrhythmias. Adenosine is for SVT — contraindicated in bradycardia as it would worsen the block.

Question 2 — Analyze Cues: Atropine is given but the patient remains bradycardic with a pulse. What is the next intervention?

  • A. Prepare for transcutaneous pacing
  • B. Give epinephrine 1mg IV push
  • C. Start dopamine infusion at 5 mcg/kg/min
  • D. Give a second dose of atropine 0.5mg

Correct: A. Prepare for transcutaneous pacing

ACLS bradycardia algorithm: If atropine is ineffective, prepare for transcutaneous pacing immediately. TCP provides immediate electrical stimulation while waiting for other interventions to work. Epinephrine or dopamine infusion can be started as a bridge, but TCP is the definitive treatment for unstable bradycardia unresponsive to atropine. Second atropine dose is 1mg (not 0.5mg) but pacing takes priority.

Question 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses: The patient converts to ventricular fibrillation on the monitor. What is the first action?

  • A. Defibrillate at 200J biphasic
  • B. Give epinephrine 1mg IV
  • C. Begin CPR immediately
  • D. Establish IV access

Correct: A. Defibrillate at 200J biphasic

For pulseless VT/VF, the ACLS algorithm is: defibrillate FIRST (shockable rhythm), then CPR. Early defibrillation is the single most important intervention for survival in VF. Epinephrine is given after the first shock and 2 minutes of CPR. CPR is important but defibrillation takes precedence for shockable rhythms. IV access should already be established or obtained during CPR.

Question 4 — Generate Solutions: After the second shock, the patient remains in VF. The provider orders amiodarone. Which statement about amiodarone administration during code is correct?

  • A. Give 300mg IV push followed by 150mg in 3-5 minutes if persistent VF
  • B. Amiodarone should be diluted in 100mL D5W and infused over 30 minutes
  • C. Amiodarone is contraindicated in patients on metoprolol
  • D. Give 1mg IV push every 3-5 minutes (same as epinephrine dosing)

Correct: A. Give 300mg IV push followed by 150mg in 3-5 minutes if persistent VF

ACLS amiodarone dosing for VF/pulseless VT: First dose 300mg IV/IO push. Second dose 150mg if VF persists. It can be given undiluted or diluted in 20-30mL D5W (rapid push during CPR). While amiodarone and beta-blockers both slow HR, they are not contraindicated together — but monitor for bradycardia. Epinephrine is 1mg every 3-5 minutes, not amiodarone.

Question 5 — Take Action: ROSC is achieved. The patient is intubated and BP is 88/52. The provider orders norepinephrine. Which parameter should the nurse monitor most closely?

  • A. Urine output
  • B. Mean arterial pressure (MAP)
  • C. Central venous pressure (CVP)
  • D. Pulmonary artery pressure

Correct: B. Mean arterial pressure (MAP)

Norepinephrine is a vasopressor used to maintain organ perfusion. The target MAP for post-cardiac arrest is ≥65 mmHg (some protocols aim for 80-100 in the first hours). MAP = (2×DBP + SBP) ÷ 3. For BP 88/52: MAP = (2×52 + 88) ÷ 3 = 64 — borderline. The nurse titrates norepinephrine to achieve MAP goal. Urine output is important for renal perfusion but MAP is the direct titration target. CVP and PA pressures require invasive monitoring not always immediately available.

Question 6 — Evaluate Outcomes: The patient stabilizes on norepinephrine at 0.05 mcg/kg/min with MAP 72. Twelve hours later, the nurse notices the IV site is infiltrated. What is the priority action?

  • A. Stop the norepinephrine and restart in a new peripheral IV
  • B. Continue the infusion and apply warm compresses
  • C. Stop the infusion immediately and prepare for central line placement
  • D. Reduce the dose and monitor the site

Correct: C. Stop the infusion immediately and prepare for central line placement

Vasopressors (norepinephrine, epinephrine, dopamine, phenylephrine) cause severe tissue necrosis if they extravasate. The infusion must be stopped immediately. Norepinephrine should ideally run through a central line — if it was running peripherally and infiltrated, a central line is needed. Peripheral vasopressor administration is only a temporary bridge and requires a large proximal vein with frequent site checks. Warm compresses and continuing the infusion would cause tissue damage.
