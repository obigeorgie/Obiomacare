NCLEX Emergency & Trauma Nursing

Trauma assessment, triage, mass casualty incidents, shock, and hemorrhage control for NCLEX success.

1. Primary Survey (ABCDE)

📋 Trauma Primary Survey

✅ A — Airway with C-spine protection:
• Assess patency; clear obstruction
• Jaw thrust (NOT head tilt-chin lift if spinal injury suspected)
• Insert oral/nasopharyngeal airway if needed
• Intubate if GCS ≤8 or airway compromise
• Assume C-spine injury until cleared — maintain inline stabilization
✅ B — Breathing:
• Inspect, palpate, auscultate chest
• Look for: tracheal deviation, JVD, chest wall movement, crepitus, open wounds
• Treat immediately: tension pneumothorax (needle decompression), open pneumothorax (occlusive dressing), flail chest (positive pressure ventilation)
✅ C — Circulation:
• Control external bleeding (direct pressure, tourniquet if extremity)
• Assess pulses, skin color, capillary refill, BP
• Two large-bore IVs (14–16g); warmed fluids; blood products if ongoing hemorrhage
• Foley catheter (after urethral injury ruled out)
• Baseline Hgb/Hct (may be normal initially — hemodilution hasn't occurred yet)
✅ D — Disability:
• GCS, pupil size/reactivity, motor function
• Blood glucose (hypoglycemia can mimic neurological deficit)
✅ E — Exposure/Environment:
• Completely undress patient
• Prevent hypothermia (warm blankets, warmed fluids, room temp)
• Log-roll to inspect back

2. Shock

📋 Types of Shock

TypePathoSignsTreatment
HypovolemicFluid/blood loss↓ BP, ↑ HR, cool/clammy, ↓ UOP, anxiousFluid resuscitation, control bleeding, blood products
CardiogenicHeart failure/pump failure↓ BP, ↑ HR, JVD, crackles, cool/clammyInotropes, diuretics, IABP, treat cause
Distributive (Septic)Vasodilation, leaky capillaries↓ BP, ↑ HR, WARM/flushed, bounding pulses, feverAntibiotics within 1h, fluids, vasopressors, source control
Distributive (Anaphylactic)Massive vasodilation↓ BP, bronchospasm, angioedema, urticariaEpinephrine IM, fluids, O₂, antihistamines
Distributive (Neurogenic)Loss of sympathetic tone (spinal cord injury)↓ BP, bradycardia, WARM/dry skin, priapism, paralysisFluids, vasopressors (phenylephrine, norepinephrine), atropine for bradycardia
ObstructivePhysical blockage of circulation↓ BP, ↑ HR, JVD, pulsus paradoxus (tamponade)Relieve obstruction (pericardiocentesis, thrombolysis, needle decompression)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Neurogenic shock (from spinal cord injury above T6) causes BRADYCARDIA + hypotension + WARM DRY SKIN (loss of sympathetic tone). This is the ONLY shock where the skin is warm and dry. Do NOT give atropine unless symptomatic bradycardia (HR <50 with symptoms). Priority is fluids and vasopressors.

3. Triage

📋 Emergency Department Triage (ESI — Emergency Severity Index)

LevelDescriptionExamplesTime to Provider
ESI 1ResuscitationCardiac arrest, intubation, severe traumaImmediate
ESI 2EmergentChest pain, respiratory distress, altered mental status, severe pain<15 min
ESI 3UrgentAbdominal pain, asthma (moderate), moderate laceration<30 min
ESI 4Less urgentSimple laceration, UTI symptoms, minor complaints<60 min
ESI 5Non-urgentSuture removal, medication refill, minor rashes<120 min

📋 START Triage (Mass Casualty)

💡 Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment:
Step 1: Can patient walk? → MINOR (green tag) — move to treatment area
Step 2: Respirations? No → open airway. Still no? → EXPECTANT/DECEASED (black)
Step 3: RR >30? → IMMEDIATE (red tag)
Step 4: Perfusion — radial pulse absent or cap refill >2 sec? → IMMEDIATE (red tag)
Step 5: Mental status — unable to follow commands? → IMMEDIATE (red tag)
If none of above: → DELAYED (yellow tag)
Tag ColorPriorityMeaning
🔴 RedImmediateLife-threatening but salvageable; needs treatment within 1 hour
🟡 YellowDelayedSerious but stable; can wait for treatment
🟢 GreenMinorWalking wounded; minimal treatment needed
⚫ BlackExpectant/DeceasedUnsurvivable or already deceased
🚨 NCLEX Trap: In mass casualty triage, resources are limited. The goal is to do the MOST GOOD for the MOST PEOPLE. This means some patients who would normally be prioritized may be triaged as expectant (black tag) if they would consume resources that could save multiple others. This is ethically difficult but necessary in disaster triage.

4. Hemorrhage Control

📋 Stop the Bleed

✅ Hierarchy of Hemorrhage Control:
1. Direct pressure: First and best method; hold firm pressure for at least 5 minutes
2. Wound packing + pressure: For junctional wounds (groin, axilla, neck)
3. Tourniquet: For extremity hemorrhage not controlled by pressure; apply 2–3 inches above wound; mark time
4. Hemostatic dressings: Impregnated with clotting agents (kaolin, chitosan)
5. Pelvic binder: For suspected pelvic fracture with hemorrhage
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Tourniquets are safe for up to 2 hours without significant complications. Do NOT loosen a tourniquet "to let blood flow" — this causes reperfusion injury and can worsen bleeding. The tourniquet stays ON until the patient reaches definitive care. Write the time of application on the patient.

5. Specific Trauma

📋 Head Injury

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Maintain cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP = MAP − ICP; goal >60)
• Prevent secondary injury: hypoxia, hypotension, hyperthermia, hypoglycemia
• HOB 30° (promotes venous drainage, lowers ICP)
• Avoid neck flexion/rotation, Trendelenburg
• Mannitol or hypertonic saline for ↑ ICP
• Serial neuro checks (GCS, pupils, motor)
• Prevent agitation/coughing/straining (all increase ICP)

📋 Spinal Cord Injury

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Maintain inline stabilization until spine cleared
• Assess level of injury (highest level with intact sensation + motor)
• Monitor for neurogenic shock (see Shock section above)
• Monitor for spinal shock (areflexia, flaccid paralysis below injury — temporary, resolves in days-weeks)
• Autonomic dysreflexia (T6 and above): sudden severe HTN, headache, bradycardia, diaphoresis, flushing — EMERGENCY; sit patient up, loosen clothing, check for bladder/bowel distention, remove noxious stimuli
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Autonomic dysreflexia is a MEDICAL EMERGENCY in patients with spinal cord injury at T6 or above. The most common trigger is a DISTENDED BLADDER or bowel. Priority actions: sit patient up (lowers BP), check/relieve bladder/bowel, remove tight clothing, monitor BP q2-5 min, notify provider. Nifedipine or nitroglycerin for severe HTN. Do NOT let patient lie flat — this worsens hypertension.

🧩 NGN Case Study: Multi-Trauma Triage

Client Scenario

EMS brings in 4 patients from a motor vehicle collision on a rural highway. The ED has 3 trauma bays and is currently staffing 2 nurses, 1 physician, and 1 tech. The charge nurse must triage all patients within 2 minutes using the START triage system.

Patient A Patient B Patient C Patient D

Patient A (Driver): Ambulatory, confused but walking around scene, RR 22, radial pulse present, follows commands slowly.

Patient B (Front passenger): Not ambulatory, RR 8, no radial pulse, unresponsive to pain.

Patient C (Rear passenger, age 8): Not ambulatory, RR 32, weak radial pulse, responds to painful stimuli with flexion withdrawal.

Patient D (Motorcyclist): Ambulatory at scene, RR 18, radial pulse strong, completely alert and oriented, complaining of right wrist pain.

Question 1 — Recognize Cues: Using START triage, which patient receives the RED (Immediate) tag first?

  • A. Patient A
  • B. Patient B
  • C. Patient C
  • D. Patient D

Correct: C. Patient C

START triage order: RPM — Respirations, Perfusion, Mental status. Patient C: RR 32 (>30 = immediate), weak pulse (perfusion abnormal), altered mental status (withdrawal only). This is RED/Immediate. Patient B has no radial pulse but also no respirations — if after airway positioning they still don't breathe, they get BLACK/Deceased in mass casualty. Patient A is YELLOW (delayed — walking wounded but confused). Patient D is GREEN (minor — ambulatory, normal vitals).

Question 2 — Analyze Cues: After the initial triage, Patient B begins spontaneous respirations at 6 breaths/min after airway positioning. What is the correct triage category now?

  • A. RED/Immediate — apneic with respirations after positioning
  • B. YELLOW/Delayed — respirations present but abnormal
  • C. GREEN/Minor — breathing on own
  • D. BLACK/Expectant — injuries too severe

Correct: A. RED/Immediate

START triage rule: If apneic but starts breathing after airway positioning → RED/Immediate. Patient B initially had no respirations and no pulse. After airway positioning, they have respirations (albeit 6/min, which is abnormal). Any patient who requires airway intervention to breathe is RED because they need immediate airway management. The low respiratory rate also meets the <10 criteria for RED.

Question 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses: All 4 patients are brought to the ED. Patient C (8-year-old) becomes hypotensive (BP 68/40) and tachycardic (HR 160) during assessment. What is the priority nursing intervention?

  • A. Establish two large-bore IVs and begin fluid resuscitation
  • B. Obtain a CT scan of the abdomen to identify internal bleeding
  • C. Type and crossmatch for blood transfusion
  • D. Administer morphine for pain management

Correct: A. Establish two large-bore IVs and begin fluid resuscitation

A pediatric patient with hypotension and tachycardia after trauma is in hemorrhagic shock until proven otherwise. The priority is fluid resuscitation — "circulation" in ABCs. Large-bore IVs (or IO if IV access is difficult in children) with warmed crystalloid (20 mL/kg bolus) is the immediate intervention. CT, blood typing, and pain management are important but come after stabilization. Pediatric patients can maintain BP until they've lost 25-30% of blood volume — hypotension in a child is a late sign of shock.

Question 4 — Generate Solutions: Patient A's confusion worsens. He becomes combative and tries to remove his cervical collar. His pupils are equal and reactive. What is the most likely cause of his altered mental status?

  • A. Epidural hematoma with uncal herniation
  • B. Hypoxia from undiagnosed pneumothorax
  • C. Hypoglycemia or hypovolemia
  • D. Intoxication from alcohol or substances

Correct: C. Hypoglycemia or hypovolemia

Equal and reactive pupils make uncal herniation unlikely. In trauma, altered mental status with normal pupils should trigger assessment of hypoxia, hypoglycemia, hypovolemia/shock, and intoxication. Hypoglycemia and hypovolemia are immediately life-threatening and treatable. Check glucose (rule out hypoglycemia), check BP and perfusion (rule out shock), and assess O₂ saturation. Intoxication is a possibility but should be a diagnosis of exclusion after ruling out reversible causes.

Question 5 — Take Action: Patient D (motorcyclist) has a deformed right wrist and complains of numbness in his fingers. The hand is cool and pale. What is the priority action?

  • A. Apply ice and elevate the extremity
  • B. Attempt gentle reduction of the deformity
  • C. Splint in position found and mark distal pulse time
  • D. Prepare for immediate OR for vascular repair

Correct: C. Splint in position found and mark distal pulse time

A cool, pale hand with numbness after deformity suggests vascular compromise. Do NOT attempt reduction at the bedside unless you're trained — you could worsen vascular injury. Splint in the position found to immobilize and prevent further damage. Mark the time and document neurovascular status (pulse, capillary refill, sensation, motor). This is an orthopedic emergency requiring prompt specialist evaluation. Ice and elevation alone won't address the vascular issue.

Question 6 — Evaluate Outcomes: Two hours later, Patient C is stabilized and transferred to the pediatric ICU. The charge nurse is debriefing the team. Which action demonstrates effective mass casualty incident management?

  • A. The most experienced nurse cared for the most critical patient
  • B. All patients were treated in order of arrival
  • C. Triage was completed within 60 seconds using START
  • D. The least injured patient was discharged first

Correct: C. Triage was completed within 60 seconds using START

Effective MCI management requires rapid, systematic triage to do the most good for the most people. START triage should take <60 seconds per patient. Treating in order of arrival (B) is incorrect — triage determines priority. The most experienced nurse (A) is good practice but not the defining feature of MCI management. Discharging the least injured first (D) is not the goal — the goal is identifying who needs immediate care and who can wait.
