NCLEX Fluids & Electrolytes

Complete clinical judgment guide: osmolality, acid-base balance, IV therapy, and life-threatening electrolyte imbalances

NCLEX Clinical Judgment Focus: The NGN prioritizes fluids and electrolytes in scenarios involving DKA, renal failure, heart failure, and post-operative patients. Expect 3-5 questions on this topic.

1. Fluid Balance Fundamentals

Body Fluid Compartments

CompartmentPercentageKey Characteristics
Intracellular (ICF)40% TBWRich in K+, Mg2+, PO4-; low Na+
Extracellular (ECF)20% TBWRich in Na+, Cl-, HCO3-
Interstitial15% TBWBetween cells, lymph
Intravascular5% TBWPlasma volume, 3L average

Osmolality & Tonicity

TONICITY RULE: Water follows salt (Na+). Where sodium goes, water follows.
SolutionNa+ ContentEffect on CellsExamples
Isotonic250-375 mOsm/LNo change0.9% NaCl, LR, D5W (becomes hypotonic)
Hypotonic<250 mOsm/LCells swell0.45% NaCl, D5W
Hypertonic>375 mOsm/LCells shrinkD10, 3% NaCl, TPN
Clinical Alert: Never give hypotonic solutions to patients with increased intracranial pressure — causes cerebral edema. Use isotonic (0.9% NaCl) instead.

2. Critical Electrolytes

Potassium (K+) — 3.5-5.0 mEq/L

Hypokalemia (<3.5)Hyperkalemia (>5.0)
Flat T waves, U waves
ST depression
Muscle weakness, ileus
Digoxin toxicity risk		Peaked T waves → wide QRS → sine wave
Muscle weakness, paralysis
Metabolic acidosis
Cardiac arrest risk
K+ SHIFTERS: Acidosis pushes K+ OUT of cells (hyperkalemia). Alkalosis pulls K+ INTO cells (hypokalemia).
Hyperkalemia Emergency Protocol:
1. Calcium gluconate 1g IV — stabilizes cardiac membrane (works in minutes)
2. Insulin + D50 — shifts K+ into cells (30 min)
3. Albuterol nebulizer — shifts K+ into cells
4. Sodium bicarbonate — if acidotic
5. Kayexalate or dialysis — removes K+ from body (hours)

Sodium (Na+) — 135-145 mEq/L

Hyponatremia (<135)Hypernatremia (>145)
Hypotonic: Free water excess
Isotonic: Pseudohyponatremia (lipids)
Hypertonic: Hyperglycemia pulls water out

Symptoms: headache, nausea, seizures, coma

Correction: Max 8-12 mEq/L/day to prevent osmotic demyelination		Water deficit or sodium excess
Symptoms: thirst, fever, seizures, coma
Treatment: slow free water replacement
D5W or hypotonic saline
SIADH vs Diabetes Insipidus:
• SIADH: Dilute urine, concentrated serum, low Na+, fluid restriction
• DI: Concentrated urine (dilute), dilute serum, high Na+, ADH replacement

Calcium (Ca2+) — 8.5-10.5 mg/dL (total), 4.5-5.5 (ionized)

Hypocalcemia (<8.5)Hypercalcemia (>10.5)
Trousseau's sign (carpal spasm)
Chvostek's sign (facial twitch)
Prolonged QT interval
Tetany, seizures		"Stones, bones, groans, moans, thrones, psychic overtones"
Shortened QT interval
Cardiac arrest risk
Chvostek vs Trousseau:
• Chvostek = facial nerve tap → cheek twitch
• Trousseau = BP cuff inflated → carpal spasm

Magnesium (Mg2+) — 1.5-2.5 mg/dL

Hypomagnesemia often accompanies hypokalemia and hypocalcemia. Replete Mg2+ BEFORE K+ — without Mg2+, K+ won't stay in cells.

3. Acid-Base Balance

The ABG Interpretation Method

ROME:
• Respiratory Opposite — pH and PaCO2 move in opposite directions
• Metabolic Equal — pH and HCO3 move in the same direction
Disorder pH PaCO2 HCO3 Compensation
Respiratory Acidosis ↓ ↑ Normal/↑ Kidneys retain HCO3 (hours-days)
Respiratory Alkalosis ↑ ↓ Normal/↓ Kidneys excrete HCO3
Metabolic Acidosis ↓ Normal/↓ ↓ Hyperventilation (minutes)
Metabolic Alkalosis ↑ Normal/↑ ↑ Hypoventilation (limited)

Anion Gap

AG = Na+ - (Cl- + HCO3-) — Normal 8-12 mEq/L

MUDPILES (High Anion Gap):
Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Paraldehyde, Iron/INH, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates

5. NGN Clinical Judgment Case Study

Case: Post-Operative Fluid Shift

Scenario: A 68-year-old male is 8 hours post-op after a total colectomy for colon cancer. He received 3L of LR intraoperatively. Current assessment: BP 98/52, HR 112, RR 24, SpO2 94% on 2L NC. IV running at 125 mL/hr. Urine output last hour: 15 mL. Lungs clear bilaterally. Abdomen soft, distended. Skin cool, clammy. Labs: Na+ 138, K+ 3.2, Cl- 105, HCO3 22, BUN 28, Cr 1.4.

IV Potassium Rules:
• NEVER give IV push — causes cardiac arrest
• Concentration: max 40 mEq/L in peripheral lines
• Rate: max 10 mEq/hr peripheral, 20 mEq/hr central
• Must be on cardiac monitor
