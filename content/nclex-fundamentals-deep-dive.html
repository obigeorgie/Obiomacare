NCLEX Fundamentals Deep Dive

Infection control, safety, legal/ethical, documentation, vital signs, and perioperative care — the foundation of everything.

💡 NCLEX Tip: Fundamentals questions test your safety judgment. When in doubt, ask: "What keeps this patient safest?" That's usually the right answer.

🦠 Infection Control & Isolation Precautions

Standard Precautions (Used for ALL Patients)

  • Hand hygiene — #1 way to prevent infection transmission
  • Gloves when touching blood, body fluids, secretions, excretions, mucous membranes, non-intact skin
  • Gown, mask, eye protection if splash/spray likely
  • Safe needle practices — never recap, use sharps containers
NCLEX Trap: You do NOT need gloves to take a blood pressure, check a pulse, or help a patient walk. Standard precautions don't mean gloving for every touch — only when exposure to bodily fluids is possible.

Transmission-Based Precautions

TypeDiseasesKey Measures
ContactC. diff, MRSA, VRE, RSV, scabies, impetigoGloves + gown. Dedicated equipment. Wash hands with soap and water (alcohol doesn't kill C. diff spores).
DropletInfluenza, pertussis, meningitis, pneumonia, COVIDMask (surgical). Patient mask when transported. Room door can be open. Maintain 3 feet distance.
AirborneTB, measles, chickenpox (varicella), disseminated herpes zosterN95 respirator. Negative pressure room. Door closed. UV lights or HEPA filtration.
🧠 Mnemonic: "Contact = Touch. Droplet = 3 feet. Airborne = N95 + negative pressure."

Neutropenic Precautions (Protective Isolation)

For patients with ANC <500 (high risk for infection). The patient is protected from YOU, not the other way around.

  • Private room with positive pressure (or HEPA filtration)
  • No fresh flowers, plants, or standing water (Pseudomonas risk)
  • No ill visitors or children
  • Low-microbial diet — no raw fruits/vegetables, undercooked meat, unpasteurized products
  • Daily bathing, oral care, perineal care
NCLEX Trap: Nurses sometimes wear masks/gowns for neutropenic precautions, but the primary intervention is protecting the patient from environmental pathogens. Don't confuse this with standard or transmission-based precautions.

⚠️ Patient Safety

Fall Risk Assessment & Prevention

High-risk patients: Age >65, history of falls, impaired mobility, visual deficits, orthostatic hypotension, sedation, confusion/dementia, polypharmacy

Priority Interventions:
  1. Bed in lowest position with brakes locked
  2. Call light and personal items within reach
  3. Non-skid footwear
  4. Hourly rounding — address pain, potty, position, possessions
  5. Bed alarm or sitter for high-risk patients
  6. Keep floor clutter-free, adequate lighting
  7. Teach patient to call before getting up

Restraint Use (Last Resort)

CRITICAL: Restraints require a provider order within 1 hour of application. Must be least restrictive alternative. Continuous monitoring. Release and reapply every 2 hours (check circulation, offer food/fluid, toilet). NEVER tie to bed rails. Document rationale and alternative attempts.
  • Physical restraints: Soft wrist, vest, mitts, leg restraints
  • Chemical restraints: Medications used for behavior control (antipsychotics, benzodiazepines) — NOT if treating a medical condition
  • 4-point restraints: Emergency only. Provider face-to-face assessment within 1 hour

Fire Safety — RACE & PASS

RACE: Rescue patients in immediate danger → Alarm (pull station, call code) → Contain fire (close doors) → Extinguish or Evacuate
PASS (for extinguisher): Pull pin → Aim at base of fire → Squeeze handle → Sweep side to side
Priority: If fire is larger than a wastebasket → evacuate and close doors. Never use elevator during fire. Know your unit's fire plan and assembly point.

⚖️ Legal & Ethical Issues

Informed Consent

  • Must be voluntary, informed, and from a competent person
  • Nurse's role: witness signature, verify understanding, ensure no coercion
  • Nurse does NOT explain risks/benefits — that's the provider's role
  • Emergency exception: If patient unable to consent and delay would cause harm
NCLEX Trap: A patient can withdraw consent at ANY time, even after signing. A nurse who witnesses consent but suspects the patient doesn't understand should notify the provider — not try to re-explain procedures independently.

Advance Directives

TypePurposeNurse Action
Living WillWritten instructions for end-of-life care (e.g., no CPR, no ventilator)Follow wishes. If unclear, involve ethics team.
Durable Power of AttorneyDesignates someone to make healthcare decisionsCommunicate with POA. They speak for the patient when incompetent.
DNR/DNIDo Not Resuscitate / Do Not IntubateNo CPR, no intubation. Comfort measures only. Still treat pain, provide hygiene, offer food/fluid if desired.
POLST/MOLSTPhysician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment — portable medical orderFollow exactly. Valid across care settings.
Priority: A valid DNR means no chest compressions, no intubation, no defibrillation. It does NOT mean "do not treat." Continue all other care: pain management, wound care, positioning, oral care, emotional support.

HIPAA & Patient Privacy

  • Protected Health Information (PHI) includes: name, DOB, SSN, medical record number, diagnoses, treatments, photos
  • Share information only on need-to-know basis for patient care
  • Patient has right to: access records, request amendments, accounting of disclosures, restrict sharing
  • Breach notification required within 60 days
NCLEX Trap: Calling out a patient's full name in the hallway is a HIPAA violation. Using patient room numbers instead of names in public areas is best practice. Family members asking about patient status: verify the patient has authorized that person to receive information before sharing anything.

Professional Boundaries

  • No accepting gifts of significant value
  • No romantic/sexual relationships with patients (even after discharge)
  • No sharing personal problems or social media connections
  • Maintain therapeutic relationship — caring but professional
Report to supervisor: Any patient who makes sexual advances, asks for personal contact info, or gives expensive gifts. Document and notify charge nurse.

📝 Documentation

Legal Principles of Documentation

FACT: Factual (objective, measurable) → Accurate (exact quotes, times) → Concise → Timely (within 24 hours, ideally immediately after care)
  • Never: Use abbreviations not approved by facility, erase or white-out errors, document for someone else, include opinions or blame
  • Always: Use military time, sign with credentials, quote patient statements, record refusals with education provided
  • Correction: Single line through error, write "error," initial, date/time. Never obliterate original entry.
NCLEX Trap: "The patient was difficult and uncooperative" is subjective and inappropriate. Instead: "Patient stated 'I don't want this medication' and refused PO metformin. Education provided on importance of blood sugar control. Patient verbalized understanding but continued to refuse. Provider notified at 1400."

Incident Reports

  • Document only the facts in the medical record (what happened, patient response, actions taken)
  • Incident report is separate from medical record — used for quality improvement, not litigation
  • Never document "incident report filed" in the patient chart
  • File within 24 hours

🫀 Vital Signs — Beyond the Numbers

Blood Pressure — Orthostatic (Postural) Vital Signs

Check lying, sitting, standing (wait 1-2 min between positions).

Positive orthostatics: SBP drops ≥20 mmHg OR DBP drops ≥10 mmHg OR HR rises ≥20 bpm

Causes: Dehydration, blood loss, medications (diuretics, antihypertensives), prolonged bed rest, autonomic dysfunction

Priority: If positive, patient is at high fall risk. Assist with all transfers. Hold antihypertensives and notify provider.

Pulse Oximetry

  • Normal: SpO₂ ≥95%
  • Hypoxemia: SpO₂ <90% — emergency, apply O₂, assess airway, notify provider
  • COPD patients: Target 88-92% (higher SpO₂ suppresses respiratory drive)
Factors that falsely lower SpO₂: Cold extremities, nail polish (especially dark colors), poor perfusion, motion artifact, carbon monoxide poisoning (reads falsely normal)

Pain Assessment

Pain is the 5th vital sign. Always assess:

  • PQRST: Provocation/Palliation → Quality → Region/Radiation → Severity (0-10) → Timing
  • Reassess pain 30-60 min after intervention
  • Document effectiveness and side effects
Non-verbal pain cues: Grimacing, guarding, restlessness, elevated VS, withdrawal. Use FLACC (Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability) for non-verbal patients.

🏥 Perioperative Nursing

Pre-Operative Phase

Priority Checklist:
  1. Verify informed consent is signed and in chart
  2. Confirm NPO status (usually 6-8 hours for solids, 2 hours for clear liquids)
  3. Mark surgical site with patient participation (Joint Commission requirement)
  4. Remove jewelry, dentures, contact lenses, hearing aids
  5. Pre-op antibiotics within 60 min of incision (30 min for vancomycin/fluoroquinolones)
  6. DVT prophylaxis: SCDs, heparin/LMWH as ordered
  7. Verify allergies, current meds (hold anticoagulants per protocol)
  8. Baseline VS, skin assessment, neuro assessment
NCLEX Trap: If a patient ate breakfast and surgery is in 2 hours — notify surgeon immediately. Do NOT just document and proceed. Aspiration risk is lethal.

Post-Operative Phase — Priority Assessments (First 24 Hours)

ABCs + VS first, then:
  1. Airway: Patent? Gag reflex? O₂ saturation?
  2. Breathing: Rate, depth, symmetry, breath sounds, use of accessory muscles
  3. Circulation: BP, HR, capillary refill, bleeding at surgical site
  4. Neuro: LOC, movement of extremities, pain level
  5. Surgical site: Dressing intact? Drainage (type, amount)?
  6. Fluid balance: I&O, IV fluids, urine output (≥0.5 mL/kg/hr)

🎯 Clinical Judgment Case Studies

NCLEX-Style Safety

Case 1: Post-Op Day 1

A 68-year-old is POD#1 after laparoscopic cholecystectomy. He presses the call light and says, "I feel like I can't breathe." You find him diaphoretic with SpO₂ 89% on room air, RR 28, HR 118.

What is your FIRST action?

  1. Call the rapid response team
  2. Apply supplemental O₂ and raise the head of bed
  3. Check his incision for bleeding
  4. Notify the surgeon
Answer: 2 — Apply supplemental O₂ and raise the head of bed.

This follows the nursing process + ABCs. SpO₂ 89% = hypoxemia. First: apply O₂ (high-flow nasal cannula or non-rebreather), raise HOB to 45-90° (improves ventilation). THEN call rapid response if no improvement, notify surgeon, assess surgical site. But oxygen first — always.
NCLEX-Style Ethics

Case 2: DNR Confusion

A 78-year-old with a valid DNR bracelet is admitted for pneumonia. Her daughter insists, "Do everything for my mother. I don't care what that bracelet says." The patient is alert and oriented.

What is your best action?

  1. Follow the daughter's wishes — family knows best
  2. Follow the DNR — it's a valid medical order
  3. Ask the patient what she wants
  4. Call the ethics committee
Answer: 3 — Ask the patient what she wants.

The patient is alert and oriented — she has decision-making capacity. A DNR is her documented wish, but an alert patient can override or confirm any advance directive. Talk to HER. If she confirms DNR, follow it. If she wants full code, update the orders. The daughter cannot override a competent patient's wishes. Ethics committee is for when the patient cannot speak for themselves and family disagrees.
SATA 1: A nurse is caring for a patient on Contact Precautions for C. diff. Which actions are correct? (Select all that apply.)
  1. Wear gloves and gown when entering the room
  2. Clean hands with alcohol-based sanitizer after leaving the room
  3. Use dedicated equipment (stethoscope, BP cuff) for this patient
  4. Place the patient in a negative pressure room
  5. Wash hands with soap and water after removing gloves
  6. Keep the room door closed at all times
Correct: A, C, E

A: Yes — contact precautions require gloves + gown.
B: NO — alcohol doesn't kill C. diff spores. Must use soap and water.
C: Yes — dedicated equipment prevents transmission.
D: NO — negative pressure is for Airborne precautions (TB). C. diff is Contact.
E: Yes — soap and water is required for C. diff.
F: NO — door can be open for Contact precautions (unlike Airborne).
