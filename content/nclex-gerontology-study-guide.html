NCLEX Gerontology Study Guide

Normal aging changes, dementia, delirium, polypharmacy, elder abuse, and end-of-life care for NCLEX success.

1. Normal Physiological Aging Changes

📋 Body System Changes

SystemChangesNursing Implications
CardiovascularDecreased CO, stiff arteries, ↓ baroreceptor sensitivityOrthostatic hypotension risk, slower HR response to stress
Respiratory↓ VC, ↓ elastic recoil, ↓ PaO₂, weakened cough reflexMonitor SpO₂ at rest; aspiration risk; pneumonia is silent killer
GI↓ gastric acid, ↓ motility, ↓ absorption (B12, Ca, Fe)Constipation common; drug absorption altered; supplement B12
Renal↓ GFR, ↓ concentrating abilityDose reduction for renally cleared drugs; watch for dehydration
Neurological↓ neurons, slower nerve conduction, ↓ proprioceptionFall risk! Slower processing time; teach patiently
SensoryPresbycusis, presbyopia, ↓ taste/smellFace patient when speaking; adequate lighting; check hearing aids
IntegumentaryThin skin, ↓ subcutaneous fat, ↓ sweatingSkin tear risk; pressure injury risk; temp regulation impaired
ImmuneImmunosenescence, ↓ vaccine responseHigh-dose flu vaccine; watch for atypical infection presentations
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Never assume symptoms are "just normal aging." Even in older adults, new symptoms require thorough assessment. "Confusion" in elderly is NEVER normal — investigate for UTI, dehydration, medication side effects, delirium.

2. Dementia vs Delirium

📋 Critical Comparison

DementiaDelirium
OnsetGradual (months to years)Acute (hours to days)
CourseProgressive, irreversibleFluctuating, reversible
ConsciousnessClearAltered (hyperalert or lethargic)
AttentionNormal early, impaired lateSeverely impaired
MemoryRecent memory impaired firstImmediate memory impaired
SleepSundowning (worsens evening)Reversed sleep-wake cycle
CauseAlzheimer's, vascular, Lewy body, FTDInfection, meds, dehydration, pain, hypoxia, electrolyte imbalance
💡 Delirium Causes: "DELIRIUM"
Dehydration | Electrolyte imbalance | Lack of drugs (withdrawal) | Infection | Reduced sensory input | Intracranial | Urinary retention | Myocardial (MI, CHF)
✅ Nursing Priorities for Delirium:
• Treat the underlying cause (this is a MEDICAL EMERGENCY)
• Frequent reorientation (clock, calendar, family photos)
• Consistent caregivers when possible
• Reduce environmental stimuli (noise, lights at night)
• Ensure glasses and hearing aids are in place
• Monitor for injury risk (restraints are LAST resort)
• Pain assessment (atypical presentations in elderly)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Restraints should NEVER be the first intervention for confusion or agitation. Try reorientation, environmental modifications, family presence, pain management FIRST. Restraints require provider order, frequent monitoring, and documentation of alternatives attempted.

3. Types of Dementia

📋 Alzheimer's Disease (Most Common — 60-80%)

Patho: Amyloid plaques, neurofibrillary tangles → progressive neuronal death.

Stages:
• Early: Memory loss (recent), difficulty finding words, getting lost in familiar places
• Moderate: Confusion about time/place, personality changes, wandering, sleep disturbances, requires assistance with ADLs
• Severe: Inability to communicate, bedbound, swallowing difficulties, incontinence — high risk for aspiration and pressure injuries
✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Establish routine and consistency
• Simplify instructions; one-step commands
• Validate feelings (don't argue with delusions)
• Wandering precautions: ID bracelet, alarmed doors, safe walking areas
• Monitor nutrition and hydration
• Caregiver support and respite resources

📋 Vascular Dementia

Patho: Stepwise decline after CVAs or chronic ischemia. Often coexists with Alzheimer's (mixed dementia).

Characteristics: Sudden onset after stroke, patchy cognitive deficits, emotional lability, gait disturbances, urinary urgency.

📋 Lewy Body Dementia

Triad:
• Visual hallucinations (well-formed, detailed)
• Parkinsonism (rigidity, bradykinesia, shuffling gait)
• Fluctuating cognition (good days and bad days)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Lewy Body dementia patients are EXTREMELY sensitive to antipsychotics (risk of severe extrapyramidal symptoms and neuroleptic malignant syndrome). Use quetiapine if absolutely necessary, but non-pharmacological approaches preferred.

📋 Frontotemporal Dementia (Pick's Disease)

Characteristics: Personality changes and language difficulties EARLIER than memory loss. Disinhibition, apathy, compulsive behaviors. Occurs at younger age (45-65).

4. Polypharmacy

📋 Beers Criteria — Potentially Inappropriate Medications in Elderly

Drug ClassRiskAlternative
Anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, TCAs)Confusion, falls, constipation, urinary retentionSecond-generation antihistamines, SSRIs
BenzodiazepinesFalls, confusion, dependenceBuspirone, melatonin, sleep hygiene
NSAIDsGI bleed, renal injury, HF exacerbationAcetaminophen (first line for pain)
MeperidineNeurotoxic metabolite (seizures)Morphine, oxycodone
Sliding scale insulinHypoglycemia riskBasal insulin with mealtime coverage
Digoxin >0.125 mg/dayToxicity riskLower dose or alternative
✅ Polypharmacy Nursing Priorities:
• Medication reconciliation at EVERY transition of care
• Review for drug-drug and drug-disease interactions
• Assess adherence (pill organizers, simplified regimens)
• Watch for "prescribing cascade" — treating side effects with more drugs
• Monitor renal/hepatic function (affects drug clearance)
• Deprescribing: regularly evaluate if each medication is still needed

5. Elder Abuse

📋 Types and Warning Signs

TypeSigns
PhysicalBruises in various stages, burns, fractures, restraint marks
Emotional/PsychologicalWithdrawal, depression, fearfulness, sudden behavior changes
FinancialUnexplained withdrawals, missing valuables, sudden will changes
NeglectMalnutrition, dehydration, pressure injuries, poor hygiene
SexualBruising to genitals, STDs, torn clothing
AbandonmentLeft at hospital/ED, desertion by caregiver
✅ Nursing Action:
• Assess in private (away from suspected abuser)
• Document objectively with quotes and photos
• Report to Adult Protective Services (MANDATORY in all states)
• Report even if not 100% certain — investigation is not your role
• Know your state's mandatory reporting requirements
🚨 NCLEX Trap: You do NOT need "proof" to report suspected abuse. You are a MANDATORY reporter. The phrase "I should gather more evidence first" is WRONG. Report immediately; investigation is the role of APS.

6. End-of-Life Care

📋 Palliative Care vs Hospice

Palliative CareHospice
TimingAny stage of serious illness (can be curative)Prognosis ≤6 months (comfort care only)
GoalImprove quality of life alongside curative treatmentComfort and dignity at end of life
TreatmentMay include curative therapiesNo curative treatment; symptom management
LocationHospital, clinic, homeHome, hospice facility, nursing home
✅ End-of-Life Nursing Priorities:
• Pain management — NO ceiling for opioid dosing in terminal patients
• Mouth care (oral hygiene even when NPO)
• Positioning for comfort
• Emotional support for patient and family
• Honor advance directives and DNR/DNI orders
• Support family through anticipatory grief
• Death rattle (terminal secretions) — reposition, scopolamine patch (NOT suctioning)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Withholding pain medication at end of life because "it might hasten death" is WRONG. The doctrine of double effect applies: the intent is pain relief, and respiratory depression is an unintended side effect. Adequate pain control is an ethical obligation.
