NCLEX High-Alert Medications

ISMP high-alert medications that require extra safeguards. The NCLEX tests these constantly because errors cause the most harm.

⚠️ What Makes a Medication "High-Alert"?

According to the Institute for Safe Medication Practices (ISMP), high-alert medications are those that bear a heightened risk of causing significant patient harm when used in error. The NCLEX prioritizes safety — knowing these medications and their safeguards is critical.

1. Insulins

RISK Hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia, DKA, HHS
✅ SAFETY ACTIONS:
• Double-check dose with another RN (independent verification)
• Verify blood glucose before administration
• Know onset/peak/duration of each type
• Never mix rapid and long-acting in same syringe
• Have dextrose 50% available for hypoglycemia
• Check patient identity using two identifiers

2. Anticoagulants

RISK Bleeding, hemorrhage, HIT (heparin)
✅ SAFETY ACTIONS:
• Heparin: Monitor aPTT q6h until therapeutic. Use smart pumps. Check platelets for HIT.
• Warfarin: Monitor INR. Therapeutic = 2-3 (mechanical valve 2.5-3.5). Vitamin K = reversal.
• DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban): No routine monitoring. Check renal function. Reversal: andexanet alfa.
• All: Check for contraindications (active bleeding, recent surgery, low platelets)

3. Opioids

RISK Respiratory depression, sedation, hypotension, addiction
✅ SAFETY ACTIONS:
• Monitor respiratory rate and sedation level (Pasero Opioid-Induced Sedation Scale)
• Have naloxone (Narcan) readily available
• Use PCA smart pumps with lockout intervals
• Never leave first dose unattended
• Assess pain using valid scale (0-10 or Wong-Baker FACES)
• Document bowel regimen — constipation is expected

4. Neuromuscular Blocking Agents

RISK Paralysis without sedation, awareness, anaphylaxis
✅ SAFETY ACTIONS:
• NEVER give without confirmed sedation/anesthesia
• Verify ventilator is functioning before administration
• Store separately from other meds (different color bins)
• Ensure emergency airway equipment at bedside
• Examples: Succinylcholine, Rocuronium, Vecuronium

5. Chemotherapy

RISK Extravasation, myelosuppression, cardiotoxicity, teratogenicity
✅ SAFETY ACTIONS:
• Double-check calculations (BSA-based dosing)
• Verify vesicant vs irritant vs non-vesicant
• Monitor CBC before each cycle
• Use PPE during handling (gown, gloves, eye protection)
• Ensure vesicant administration through central line when possible

6. Electrolyte Solutions

RISK Cardiac arrest (K⁺, Mg²⁺), fluid overload, phlebitis
✅ SAFETY ACTIONS:
• Potassium: NEVER IV push. Max 10 mEq/hr via peripheral line, 20 mEq/hr central. Must be on cardiac monitor.
• Magnesium: Monitor reflexes, respiratory rate. Have calcium gluconate ready.
• Hypertonic saline (3% NaCl): Central line only. Monitor neurologic status closely.

7. Sedatives / Hypnotics

RISK Respiratory depression, falls, paradoxical reactions
✅ SAFETY ACTIONS:
• Monitor respiratory rate, SpO₂, level of consciousness
• Implement fall precautions
• Avoid combining multiple CNS depressants
• Have reversal agents available (flumazenil for benzos, naloxone for opioids)

ISMP Safety Strategies for High-Alert Meds

  1. Independent double check: Two RNs verify dose independently before administration
  2. Standardize concentrations: Use premixed solutions when available
  3. Smart pump technology: Use dose error reduction systems (DERS)
  4. Limit access: High-alert meds stored separately with visual alerts
  5. Patient education: Teach patients about their high-alert medications
