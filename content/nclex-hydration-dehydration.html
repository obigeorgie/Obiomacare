NCLEX Hydration & Dehydration: High-Yield Guide

Recognizing dehydration in adults and children, who is at risk, and how to prevent and evaluate it.

1. Adult Symptoms of Dehydration

  • Feeling very thirsty
  • Dry mouth
  • Urinating and sweating less than usual
  • Dark-colored urine
  • Dry skin
  • Feeling tired
  • Dizziness

2. Infant and Young Child Symptoms

Symptoms of dehydration in infants and young children include dry mouth and related findings. Infants and young children are more likely to have diarrhea or vomiting, putting them at higher risk.

3. Who Is at Higher Risk

  • Infants and young children, who are more likely to have diarrhea or vomiting
  • People with chronic illnesses that cause them to urinate or sweat more often

4. Prevention

The key to preventing dehydration is making sure the patient gets enough fluids: drink enough water every day.

5. Evaluation

The provider will ask about symptoms and may order tests such as:

  • Blood tests to check electrolyte levels, especially potassium and sodium
