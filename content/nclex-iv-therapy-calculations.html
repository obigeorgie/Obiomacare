NCLEX IV Therapy & Fluid Calculations: Complete Guide

Flow rates, drip factors, TPN, blood transfusions, and IV push medications. The calculations and clinical judgment you need for the NCLEX — with practice problems.

By Nnamdi Okorafor, RN · 15-year ER nurse · Updated August 2026 · Obioma

Why IV therapy matters on the NCLEX: You'll get at least 2-3 calculation questions and multiple clinical judgment scenarios involving IVs. The exam tests whether you can calculate correctly AND recognize when an IV complication is happening.

The Essential Formula

Flow Rate (gtt/min) = (Total Volume × Drip Factor) ÷ Time in Minutes

Or for mL/hr (pump rates):

mL/hr = Total Volume ÷ Time in Hours

Drip Factors by Tubing

Tubing TypeDrip Factor (gtt/mL)When Used
Macrodrop10, 15, or 20 gtt/mLStandard adult infusions
Microdrop (pediatric)60 gtt/mLPediatrics, precise medication dosing
Blood administration10 or 20 gtt/mLStandard blood tubing

Practice Problems

Problem 1: Basic Flow Rate

Order: 1000mL NS over 8 hours. Macrodrop tubing (15 gtt/mL).

What is the flow rate in gtt/min?

(1000 mL × 15 gtt/mL) ÷ 480 min = 31.25 → 31 gtt/min

Problem 2: mL/hr for Pump

Order: 500mL D5W over 4 hours.

What is the pump rate in mL/hr?

500 mL ÷ 4 hours = 125 mL/hr

Problem 3: IV Push Medication

Order: Lasix 40mg IV push over 2 minutes. Available: 10mg/mL.

How many mL will you give, and over how long?

40mg ÷ 10mg/mL = 4 mL over 2 minutes → 2 mL per minute

NCLEX Tip — Rounding: Round gtt/min to the nearest whole number. Round mL/hr to the nearest whole number. Round medication doses according to the syringe — if using a 1mL syringe with 0.01 markings, round to hundredths.

IV Complications: Recognition & Action

ComplicationSignsCauseNursing Action
InfiltrationCool, pale, swollen site; fluid leaking; slowed rateCatheter dislodged from veinStop infusion, remove catheter, elevate extremity, restart at new site
PhlebitisRed, warm, tender streak along vein; sluggish flowVein irritation from medication or catheterDiscontinue IV, apply warm compress, restart at new site
ExtravasationBlistering, tissue necrosis, severe pain, swellingVesicant medication (chemo, dopamine, phenytoin) leaking into tissueSTOP immediately, leave catheter in place, aspirate drug, notify provider, antidote per protocol
Fluid overloadDyspnea, crackles, JVD, weight gain, edema, hypertensionToo much fluid too fast; cardiac/renal impairmentSlow rate, call provider, sit patient upright, monitor I&O, may need diuretics
Air embolismSudden dyspnea, chest pain, hypotension, tachycardia, decreased LOCAir entering venous systemClamp catheter, place in left lateral Trendelenburg, O₂, call provider, monitor vitals
SepticemiaFever, chills, tachycardia, hypotensionContaminated IV line or solutionObtain cultures, discontinue line, notify provider, start antibiotics per orders
Speed shockDizziness, flushed face, headache, chest tightness, hypotension, syncopeMedication administered too rapidlyStop infusion, call provider, monitor vitals, supportive care

NCLEX Tip — Extravasation Priority: If a vesicant medication extravasates, do NOT remove the catheter immediately. Leave it in place to aspirate the drug from the tissue. Then remove it. This is a common NCLEX trap.

Blood Transfusions

Pre-Transfusion

  • Verify order and informed consent
  • Two RNs verify: patient identity, blood type, expiration date, unit number, integrity of bag
  • Vital signs within 30 minutes before starting
  • Ensure patent IV with normal saline only (never D5W or medications)
  • Use Y-tubing blood administration set with filter

During Transfusion

  • Stay with patient for first 15 minutes (most reactions occur within this time)
  • Start SLOW: 2 mL/min (120 mL/hr) for first 15 minutes
  • Vital signs at 15 minutes, then per protocol (usually every 30 min)
  • Complete transfusion within 4 hours per unit

Transfusion Reactions

ReactionOnsetSignsAction
Acute hemolyticWithin minutesFever, chills, back pain, hypotension, hemoglobinuria, DICSTOP transfusion immediately, maintain IV with NS, notify provider, send blood bag and tubing to lab
Febrile non-hemolytic1-6 hoursFever >1°C, chills, rigorsStop transfusion, antipyretic, monitor, resume if ruled out
Allergic (mild)During/afterPruritus, urticaria, flushingSlow or stop, antihistamine, monitor
AnaphylacticWithin minutesHypotension, bronchospasm, angioedemaSTOP, epinephrine, airway support, call code
TACO (fluid overload)1-6 hoursDyspnea, JVD, hypertension, pulmonary edemaSlow rate, diuretics, upright position, O₂
TRALI1-6 hoursAcute respiratory distress, fever, hypotensionStop transfusion, supportive care, may need ventilator

Any transfusion reaction: STOP the transfusion, keep the IV line open with normal saline, notify the provider, send the blood bag and tubing to the blood bank, and monitor vital signs. Document everything.

TPN (Total Parenteral Nutrition)

Key Principles

  • Dedicated line — no other medications or fluids through TPN line
  • Check blood glucose every 6 hours initially
  • Never stop TPN abruptly — wean over 24-48 hours to prevent hypoglycemia
  • Inspect bag for precipitates, cloudiness, or separation
  • Keep TPN refrigerated until 1 hour before use
  • Change tubing every 24 hours

TPN Complications

  • Hyperglycemia: Most common — insulin per protocol, monitor glucose
  • Hypoglycemia: If TPN stopped abruptly — give D50 or restart TPN
  • Electrolyte imbalances: Monitor K+, Mg²⁺, Phosphate daily
  • Infection/sepsis: Strict aseptic technique; fever = culture everything
  • Fluid overload: Monitor I&O, daily weights

Central Line Care

Dressing Changes

  • Transparent dressing: change every 7 days
  • Gauze dressing: change every 48 hours
  • Change immediately if damp, loose, or soiled
  • Use chlorhexidine scrub; allow to dry

Flushing Protocol

  • Before and after each use
  • After blood draws
  • Every 12 hours if not in use
  • Use 10mL syringe minimum (prevents line rupture from high pressure)
  • Saline lock: flush with 3-5mL NS after medication

Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection (CLABSI) Prevention

  • Hand hygiene before any line contact
  • Scrub the hub for 15 seconds before accessing
  • Use chlorhexidine skin prep
  • Remove lines as soon as no longer needed
  • Don't routinely replace central lines
