NCLEX Mechanical Ventilation

Complete clinical judgment guide: ventilator modes, ARDS management, PEEP, weaning, and emergency interventions

NCLEX Clinical Judgment Focus: Mechanical ventilation questions test your ability to recognize ventilator problems, interpret ABGs, and prioritize interventions. Expect 2-4 questions on this topic.

1. Ventilator Modes

ModeTriggerLimitCycleBest For
AC (Assist-Control)Patient or machineVolume or pressureVolume or timeInitial ventilation, full support
SIMV (Synchronized)Patient + mandatory breathsVolume or pressureVolume or timeWeaning, prevents respiratory muscle atrophy
PSV (Pressure Support)PatientPressureFlowWeaning, spontaneous breathing trials
CPAPPatientPressureFlowOSA, weaning, prevents atelectasis
BiPAPPatientPressure (IPAP/EPAP)FlowCOPD, CHF, avoid intubation
AC vs SIMV:
• AC = Every breath gets full support (patient-triggered or machine-triggered)
• SIMV = Set number of mandatory breaths; patient can breathe between them (but those spontaneous breaths get less support)

Lung Protective Strategy (ARDS):
• Low tidal volume: 6 mL/kg ideal body weight
• Plateau pressure < 30 cmH2O
• PEEP: start 5, titrate up
• Allow permissive hypercapnia (pH > 7.25 acceptable)

High Pressure Alarm Priority:
1. Look at patient (color, consciousness)
2. Check for disconnect first
3. Auscultate breath sounds
4. Suction if indicated
5. If pneumothorax suspected: STAT chest X-ray

4. ARDS Management

ARDS Criteria (Berlin Definition):
• Acute onset (< 1 week)
• Bilateral infiltrates on CXR
• Not cardiac in origin (PCWP < 18)
• PaO2/FiO2 ratio: Mild 200-300, Moderate 100-200, Severe < 100
InterventionRationaleNursing Considerations
Low Vt (6 mL/kg)Prevents volutraumaMay cause hypercapnia; monitor ABG
High PEEPRecruits alveoliMonitor hemodynamics (↓ preload)
Prone positioningImproves V/Q matchingTurn q16h; protect eyes/tube/lines
Neuromuscular blockadePrevents patient-ventilator dyssynchronySedation first; continuous EEG if possible
Conservative fluidsPrevents pulmonary edemaMonitor I&O, daily weights, albumin

5. Weaning Criteria

RSBI (Rapid Shallow Breathing Index):
RSBI = f/Vt (respiratory rate / tidal volume in liters)
• RSBI < 105 = likely to wean successfully
• RSBI > 105 = likely to fail weaning
ParameterGoal for Weaning
Spontaneous Vt> 5 mL/kg
Minute ventilation< 10 L/min
Negative inspiratory force< -20 cmH2O
Vital capacity> 10-15 mL/kg
PaO2/FiO2> 150-200
PEEP<= 8 cmH2O
ABGpH 7.35-7.45, PaCO2 < 45
Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT):
• 30-120 minutes on T-piece or low PSV (5-8 cmH2O)
• Monitor: RR, HR, BP, SpO2, work of breathing
• Stop if: RR > 35, SpO2 < 90%, HR > 140, arrhythmias, anxiety, diaphoresis

6. Endotracheal Tube Care

ETT Position Verification:
• Confirm bilateral breath sounds after insertion
• Chest X-ray: tip 2-4 cm above carina
• Mark tube at teeth/lip level
• Secure with tape/holder; prevent unplanned extubation

Suctioning Protocol
