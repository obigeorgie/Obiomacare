NCLEX Medication Safety: High-Yield Guide

The core medication-safety teaching the NCLEX expects you to deliver — taking medicines correctly and knowing what to ask.

1. Take Medicines Correctly

The most important medication-safety instruction is to take medicines correctly. Prescription and over-the-counter medicines are safe and effective when used correctly.

2. What to Ask About

Patients should ask about:

  • Side effects to watch for
  • Interactions with food, alcohol, or other medicines they may be taking

3. Prescription and Over-the-Counter Medicines

  • OTC medicines can interact with other medicines and should be used with the same care as prescriptions
  • Patients should tell the provider and pharmacist about everything they take — including OTC and herbal products
  • Never take a medicine differently than prescribed

4. Older Adults

Certain medications should be avoided or used with caution in older adults. Older adults are a key population for medication-review teaching — some medicines carry higher risks with age.

5. Nursing Priorities

  • Teach patients to keep a current medication list and share it with every provider
  • Ask about alcohol use, because alcohol can interact with medicines
  • Encourage questions to the pharmacist — asking about side effects is a safety behavior
