NCLEX Medication Suffixes: Complete Reference

Learn to identify drug classes by their suffixes. The NCLEX tests this constantly — know which drugs end in -olol, -pril, -sartan, -statin, and more.

Why Suffixes Matter on the NCLEX

The NCLEX doesn't expect you to memorize every drug. Instead, it tests whether you can recognize drug classes by their endings and apply the correct nursing interventions. If you know a drug ends in "-olol," you immediately know it's a beta-blocker and you need to check the apical pulse before administration.

Cardiovascular Drugs

-olol

Beta-Blockers (β-blockers)

Examples: Metoprolol, Atenolol, Propranolol, Carvedilol, Labetalol

Action: Block β-adrenergic receptors → decrease HR, BP, and contractility

🩺 Nursing Priority: Check apical pulse before giving. Hold if <60 bpm. Monitor for bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm. NEVER stop abruptly — taper over 1-2 weeks.
-pril

ACE Inhibitors (Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme)

Examples: Lisinopril, Enalapril, Captopril, Ramipril

Action: Block conversion of angiotensin I to II → vasodilation, ↓ BP, ↓ aldosterone

🩺 Nursing Priority: Monitor for dry cough, angioedema (emergency!), hyperkalemia. Check BP before giving. Contraindicated in pregnancy.
-sartan

ARBs (Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers)

Examples: Losartan, Valsartan, Irbesartan, Candesartan

Action: Block AT1 receptors → vasodilation, ↓ BP without the cough

🩺 Nursing Priority: Monitor BP, potassium, creatinine. Alternative to ACE-I when patient has cough. Also contraindicated in pregnancy.
-dipine

Calcium Channel Blockers (Dihydropyridines)

Examples: Amlodipine, Nifedipine, Nicardipine

Action: Block calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle → vasodilation

🩺 Nursing Priority: Monitor BP, watch for peripheral edema, reflex tachycardia. Grapefruit juice interaction!
-statin

HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors

Examples: Atorvastatin, Simvastatin, Rosuvastatin, Pravastatin

Action: Inhibit cholesterol synthesis in liver → ↓ LDL, ↑ HDL

🩺 Nursing Priority: Monitor LFTs (AST/ALT). Report muscle pain/weakness (rhabdomyolysis risk). Give in evening (cholesterol synthesis peaks at night).

Antibiotics

-cillin

Penicillins

Examples: Amoxicillin, Ampicillin, Piperacillin, Penicillin G

Action: Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis

🩺 Nursing Priority: ALWAYS ask about penicillin allergy. Cross-reactivity with cephalosporins (~10%). Monitor for anaphylaxis.
-mycin

Aminoglycosides / Macrolides

Examples: Gentamicin, Tobramycin, Erythromycin, Azithromycin, Vancomycin

Action: Inhibit protein synthesis (30S or 50S ribosomal subunit)

🩺 Nursing Priority: Aminoglycosides: Nephrotoxic and ototoxic — monitor BUN/Cr, hearing. Vancomycin: Monitor trough levels, infuse over 60+ min.
-floxacin

Fluoroquinolones

Examples: Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin

Action: Inhibit DNA gyrase → bactericidal

🩺 Nursing Priority: Tendon rupture risk (especially >60y, steroids). Avoid in pregnancy/children. Give 2h before or 6h after antacids.
-azole

Antifungals

Examples: Fluconazole, Ketoconazole, Itraconazole

Action: Inhibit fungal ergosterol synthesis

🩺 Nursing Priority: Monitor LFTs. Many drug interactions (CYP450). Ketoconazole: Take with acidic food/drink.

Central Nervous System

-zepam / -zolam

Benzodiazepines

Examples: Lorazepam, Diazepam, Alprazolam, Midazolam, Clonazepam

Action: Enhance GABA → sedation, anxiolysis, muscle relaxation, anticonvulsant

🩺 Nursing Priority: Risk of respiratory depression (especially with opioids). NEVER mix with alcohol. Flumazenil = reversal agent. Taper slowly — withdrawal can cause seizures.
-pram / -ine

SSRIs / SNRIs / TCAs

Examples: Sertraline, Fluoxetine, Escitalopram, Amitriptyline, Venlafaxine

Action: Increase serotonin (and/or norepinephrine) in synapse

🩺 Nursing Priority: Do NOT stop abruptly (discontinuation syndrome). Monitor for serotonin syndrome (hyperthermia, clonus, agitation). Watch for suicidal ideation in young adults.

🎯 Clinical Judgment Case Study

Pharmacology Drug Interactions

Case: Polypharmacy in Elderly

An 80-year-old is taking: metoprolol (-olol), lisinopril (-pril), furosemide, atorvastatin (-statin), warfarin, omeprazole (-prazole), and acetaminophen PRN. She presents with fatigue, dizziness, and a fall.

Which drug combination is MOST likely causing her symptoms?

  1. Metoprolol + lisinopril + furosemide (triple hypotensive effect)
  2. Atorvastatin + warfarin (increased bleeding risk)
  3. Omepprazole + acetaminophen (hepatotoxicity)
  4. Furosemide + warfarin (electrolyte imbalance)
Answer: 1 — Metoprolol + lisinopril + furosemide.

Triple hypotensive therapy = additive hypotension, especially in elderly with reduced baroreceptor response. Beta-blocker (metoprolol) + ACE inhibitor (lisinopril) + loop diuretic (furosemide) = high risk of orthostatic hypotension, dizziness, falls. Also: furosemide causes hypokalemia which potentiates digoxin toxicity (not in her list but a classic combo). Consider de-prescribing — does she still need all three? Check orthostatic BPs, BMP.
