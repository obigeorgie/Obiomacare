NCLEX Nutrition & Therapeutic Diets

Therapeutic diets, enteral and parenteral nutrition, electrolyte imbalances, and fluid management for NCLEX success.

2. Enteral Nutrition (Tube Feeding)

📋 Types of Enteral Access

Tube TypePlacementIndicationsNursing Considerations
NG (Nasogastric)Nose → stomachShort-term (<4-6 weeks)Verify placement before EVERY feeding (X-ray, pH testing, auscultation NOT definitive)
NJ (Nasojejunal)Nose → jejunumGastroparesis, pancreatitis, aspiration riskContinuous feeding only; no boluses
PEG (Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy)Stomach through abdominal wallLong-term (>4-6 weeks)Site care; risk of peristomal infection
PEJ/J-tubeJejunum through abdominal wallGastric dysfunction, severe refluxContinuous feeding; flush meticulously
✅ Enteral Feeding Nursing Priorities:
• Verify tube placement before EVERY feeding/medication
• HOB ≥30° during and 1 hour after feeding
• Check gastric residual (if ordered) — hold if >250-500 mL
• Flush with 30 mL water before and after meds/feedings
• Monitor for diarrhea, constipation, distention, N/V
• Oral care q2-4h (even if NPO)
• Monitor blood glucose (formula is high in carbs)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: The ONLY reliable method to confirm NG tube placement is CHEST X-RAY. Auscultation ("whoosh test") is NOT reliable and should not be used as the sole verification method. If tube dislodges, DON'T reinsert blindly — get X-ray confirmation.

3. Parenteral Nutrition (PN)

📋 Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)

Indications: Non-functioning GI tract, severe malabsorption, bowel obstruction, prolonged ileus, severe pancreatitis.

✅ TPN Nursing Priorities:
• Dedicated central line (PICC, CVC, or PORT) — NEVER peripheral (except PPN)
• Strict aseptic technique during bag changes
• Do NOT interrupt infusion abruptly (risk of rebound hypoglycemia)
• Wean off gradually when transitioning to enteral feeds
• Monitor blood glucose q6h initially
• Daily weights, I&O
• Monitor for refeeding syndrome (phosphate, potassium, magnesium drop)
• Monitor liver function, triglycerides
• Inspect catheter site for infection
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Refeeding syndrome occurs when malnourished patients receive aggressive nutrition. Phosphate, potassium, and magnesium shift intracellularly → cardiac failure, arrhythmias, respiratory failure. Start TPN slowly and monitor electrolytes closely. NEVER mix medications with TPN — use separate line or pause TPN.

5. Fluid Balance

📋 Dehydration Types

TypeLost FluidSerum Na+Clinical SignsTreatment
IsotonicWater + electrolytes equallyNormal (135-145)Orthostatic hypotension, poor skin turgor, dry mucous membranesIsotonic fluids (NS, LR)
HypotonicMore water than sodiumLow (<135)Headache, confusion, seizures, nausea, lethargyReplace sodium SLOWLY (<12 mEq/L/24h)
HypertonicMore sodium than waterHigh (>145)Thirst, restlessness, weakness, seizuresHypotonic fluids (½ NS, D5W) + treat cause
✅ IV Fluid Selection Quick Guide:
• Hypovolemic shock: Isotonic crystalloids (NS, LR) — 1-2 L bolus
• Mild dehydration: PO fluids if possible
• Hyponatremia: Calculate free water deficit; replace SLOWLY
• Hypernatremia: D5W or ½ NS; replace over 48h
• DKA: NS first, then ½ NS when glucose <250
• CHF: Diuretics + fluid restriction; avoid excessive IVF
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Correct sodium SLOWLY in both hyponatremia AND hypernatremia. Rapid correction of hyponatremia causes osmotic demyelination syndrome. Rapid correction of hypernatremia causes cerebral edema. Both are potentially fatal!
