Advanced Oncology Pharmacology

Chemotherapy, biologics, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and comprehensive nursing management for the NCLEX.

☢️ Chemotherapy Basics

Safety Principles

  • OSHA Hazardous Drugs: Use biosafety cabinet (BSC) or closed system transfer device (CSTD) for preparation
  • PPE: Double chemo-tested gloves, gown, eye protection. Never recapping needles.
  • Spill kit: Available on every unit administering chemotherapy
  • Patient body fluids: Treat as hazardous for 48 hours post-chemotherapy (double flush toilet, wear gloves when handling)
  • Vesicants vs. irritants: Vesicants (doxorubicin, vincristine) cause tissue necrosis if extravasated — CRITICAL to verify blood return before and during administration
Extravasation Management: STOP infusion immediately. Do NOT remove catheter. Aspirate residual drug. Administer antidote per protocol. Elevate extremity. Warm compress for vinca alkaloids, cold for anthracyclines. Mark area. Photograph. Notify provider. Document thoroughly.

☢️ Major Chemotherapy Classes

Alkylating Agents

Examples: Cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, cisplatin, carboplatin, busulfan

Mechanism: Cross-link DNA strands → prevent replication

Side Effects:
  • Cyclophosphamide/ifosfamide: Hemorrhagic cystitis (mesna protective agent), SIADH
  • Cisplatin: Nephrotoxicity (aggressive hydration), ototoxicity, peripheral neuropathy, severe nausea/vomiting
  • Busulfan: Pulmonary fibrosis, hepatic veno-occlusive disease (VOD)

Antimetabolites

Examples: Methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), capecitabine, cytarabine, gemcitabine

Mechanism: Interfere with DNA/RNA synthesis by mimicking normal metabolites

Side Effects:
  • Methotrexate: Mucositis, myelosuppression, hepatic/renal toxicity. Leucovorin rescue for high-dose. Monitor methotrexate levels
  • 5-FU: Hand-foot syndrome (palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia), mucositis, diarrhea, photosensitivity
  • Cytarabine: Cerebellar toxicity (high-dose), conjunctivitis (steroid eye drops prophylaxis)

Anthracyclines

Examples: Doxorubicin (Adriamycin), daunorubicin, epirubicin, idarubicin

Mechanism: Intercalate DNA, inhibit topoisomerase II, generate free radicals

Side Effects:
  • Cardiotoxicity (DOSE-LIMITING): Dilated cardiomyopathy, CHF. Cumulative lifetime dose: doxorubicin 450–550 mg/m². Monitor LVEF (MUGA or echo) before and during therapy
  • Vesicant: Severe tissue necrosis if extravasated — cold compress
  • Red-orange urine: Normal — not hematuria. Warn patient.
  • Myelosuppression, mucositis, alopecia
Mnemonic: "Doxorubicin = Doxo-RUB-in" — Turns urine red. "Anthracyclines Attack the Heart" — cardiotoxicity is dose-limiting.

Plant Alkaloids

Vinca alkaloids (vincristine, vinblastine): Inhibit microtubule formation (mitotic spindle). Vesicants. Vincristine: peripheral neuropathy (dose-limiting), constipation/ileus (autonomic neuropathy), SIADH. Vinblastine: more myelosuppression.

Taxanes (paclitaxel, docetaxel): Stabilize microtubules → prevent disassembly. Hypersensitivity reactions (premedicate with steroids, diphenhydramine, H2 blocker). Peripheral neuropathy. Alopecia. Fluid retention (docetaxel — premedicate with steroids).

☢️ Immunotherapy

Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

Mechanism: Block inhibitory signals that tumors exploit to evade immune system → unleash T-cell attack on cancer

DrugTargetKey Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs)
Pembrolizumab, nivolumabPD-1Pneumonitis, colitis/diarrhea, hepatitis, hypothyroidism/hyperthyroidism, rash, adrenal insufficiency, type 1 diabetes
IpilimumabCTLA-4Colitis (most common), rash, hypophysitis, hepatitis
Atezolizumab, durvalumabPD-L1Similar to PD-1 inhibitors
Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs): Can affect ANY organ system. Early recognition is KEY. Treat with corticosteroids (prednisone 1–2 mg/kg) for moderate-severe irAEs. Do NOT stop immunotherapy for mild rash — topical steroids suffice. Hold for grade 2+ and start systemic steroids. May need infliximab for steroid-refractory colitis. Always rule out infection before attributing to irAE.
NCLEX Trap: Immunotherapy side effects are DIFFERENT from chemotherapy side effects. Checkpoint inhibitors typically do NOT cause myelosuppression, severe nausea, or alopecia. Instead, watch for autoimmune-type inflammation (pneumonitis, colitis, rash, endocrinopathies). A patient on nivolumab with new cough and dyspnea needs immediate evaluation for pneumonitis — not just "viral URI."

☢️ CAR-T Cell Therapy

Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS)

Pathophysiology: Massive cytokine release from activated T-cells → systemic inflammatory response

Signs: Fever (first sign), hypotension, tachycardia, hypoxia, capillary leak, organ dysfunction

Grading: Mild (fever) → severe (hypotension requiring multiple vasopressors, hypoxia requiring mechanical ventilation)

Treatment: Tocilizumab (IL-6 receptor antagonist) + corticosteroids for moderate-severe. ICU-level care for severe.

ICANS (Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome)

Signs: Encephalopathy, confusion, aphasia, tremor, seizures, cerebral edema

Treatment: Corticosteroids (high-dose methylprednisolone). Tocilizumab does NOT cross blood-brain barrier well.

☢️ Supportive Care Medications

Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea & Vomiting (CINV) Prophylaxis

  • High emetogenic chemo: NK1 antagonist (aprepitant) + 5-HT3 antagonist (ondansetron) + dexamethasone
  • Moderate: 5-HT3 + dexamethasone ± NK1
  • Low: Dexamethasone or 5-HT3 or dopamine antagonist
  • Delayed CINV (days 2–5): Dexamethasone ± aprepitant

Myelosuppression Support

  • G-CSF (filgrastim, pegfilgrastim): Stimulates neutrophil production. Give 24 hours after chemo. Bone pain common. Pegfilgrastim: single dose per cycle
  • Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (darbepoetin, epoetin): For chemotherapy-induced anemia. Target Hgb 10–12 g/dL. Risk of thrombosis. Not for curative intent patients per FDA
  • Thrombopoietin agonists (romiplostim, eltrombopag): For thrombocytopenia
  • Transfusions: PRBCs for Hgb <7–8 (symptomatic or cardiac disease → higher threshold). Platelets <10,000 (prophylactic) or <50,000 with active bleeding

🎯 Clinical Judgment Case Study

Oncology Chemotherapy

Case: Extravasation

During administration of doxorubicin (vesicant), the patient reports burning at the IV site. The site is swollen, red, and there is no blood return. The infusion is running through a peripheral IV.

What is the nurse's immediate action?

  1. Stop the infusion immediately and aspirate remaining drug
  2. Apply warm compresses to promote vasodilation
  3. Flush the line with normal saline and continue infusion
  4. Apply cold compress and elevate the extremity
Answer: 1 — Stop the infusion and aspirate remaining drug.

Doxorubicin extravasation causes severe tissue necrosis. STOP the infusion immediately, aspirate any residual drug from the line, remove the IV, and notify the physician. For doxorubicin (anthracycline): cold compress (not warm) — causes vasoconstriction to limit spread. Apply cold for 15-20 min qid x 24-48h. Elevate the extremity. Dexrazoxane (Totect) is the antidote for anthracycline extravasation. Document everything with photos.
