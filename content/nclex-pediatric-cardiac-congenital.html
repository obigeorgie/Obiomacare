Pediatric Cardiac & CHD

Congenital heart defects, cyanotic vs acyanotic lesions, ductal-dependent defects, and pediatric cardiac nursing for the NCLEX.

❤️ Congenital Heart Defects Overview

Mnemonic: "5 T's of Cyanotic CHD" — Tetralogy of Fallot, Transposition of the Great Vessels, Truncus Arteriosus, Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Return, Tricuspid Atresia

Acyanotic Defects (L → R Shunt)

Pathophysiology: Oxygenated blood shunts from left (higher pressure) to right (lower pressure) side. No cyanosis initially. Increased pulmonary blood flow.

Examples:

  • Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD) — MOST COMMON CHD. Harsh holosystolic murmur at LLSB. May close spontaneously (small defects).
  • Atrial Septal Defect (ASD) — Wide fixed split S2, systolic ejection murmur. Often asymptomatic until adulthood.
  • Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA) — Continuous machinery murmur at LUSB. Prematurity is major risk factor. Treat with indomethacin or ibuprofen (in preemies).
  • Atrioventricular Canal Defect (AVCD) — Common in Down syndrome. Large defect with L → R shunt.
Manifestations: Failure to thrive, tachypnea, diaphoresis with feeding, recurrent respiratory infections, hepatomegaly
Management: Diuretics (furosemide), ACE inhibitors (reduce afterload), surgical repair if symptomatic or large defect. Prevent endocarditis (antibiotic prophylaxis for dental procedures).

Cyanotic Defects (R → L Shunt)

Pathophysiology: Deoxygenated blood shunts from right to left side, bypassing the lungs → systemic cyanosis.

Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) — MOST COMMON cyanotic CHD

4 Defects:

  1. Pulmonary stenosis (right ventricular outflow obstruction)
  2. Ventricular septal defect
  3. Overriding aorta (aorta sits over VSD)
  4. Right ventricular hypertrophy
Manifestations: Cyanosis (worsens with crying), clubbing, polycythemia, "tet spells" (hypercyanotic episodes), harsh systolic ejection murmur at LUSB
Tet Spell Management:
  1. Place infant in knee-to-chest position (increases SVR, reduces R → L shunt)
  2. Administer oxygen (limited benefit but standard)
  3. Give morphine (reduces crying, decreases RR)
  4. IV fluids (increases preload)
  5. Phenylephrine or squatting (increases SVR)
  6. Propranolol (reduces infundibular spasm)
Mnemonic: "PROVe" — Pulmonary stenosis, RV hypertrophy, Overriding aorta, VSD

Transposition of the Great Arteries (TGA)

Pathophysiology: Aorta arises from RV, pulmonary artery from LV. Two parallel circuits — no mixing of blood.

Manifestations: Severe cyanosis within hours of birth, tachypnea, single loud S2, normal heart size
Management: Prostaglandin E₁ (alprostadil) to keep PDA open (maintains mixing). Balloon atrial septostomy (Rashkind procedure) to create ASD. Arterial switch operation (Jatene procedure) within first 2 weeks.

Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome (HLHS)

Pathophysiology: Underdeveloped left ventricle, aortic atresia, mitral atresia. Systemic circulation depends on PDA.

Management: Prostaglandin E₁ to maintain PDA patency (life-dependent). Norwood procedure (stage 1) within first week, Glenn procedure (3-6 months), Fontan procedure (2-4 years). Heart transplant is definitive.

💊 Ductal-Dependent Lesions

Prostaglandin E₁ (Alprostadil)

Indication: Maintain ductus arteriosus patency in ductal-dependent CHD (HLHS, TGA, critical coarctation, critical pulmonary stenosis)

Side Effects:
  • Apnea (most common — intubation readiness)
  • Hypotension, flushing
  • Fever
  • Bradycardia
Nursing Priority: Monitor for apnea (may need intubation). Continuous cardiac monitoring. Keep emergency airway equipment at bedside. Monitor BP closely.
NCLEX Trap: PGE₁ is given to KEEP PDA OPEN in ductal-dependent lesions. Indomethacin/ibuprofen is given to CLOSE PDA in preterm infants. These are opposites — know which condition requires which drug.

🔍 Congestive Heart Failure in Infants

Manifestations

Feeding difficulties: Poor feeding, diaphoresis during feeds, fatigue, prolonged feeding time
Respiratory: Tachypnea, retractions, nasal flaring, grunting, wheezing
Cardiac: Tachycardia, gallop rhythm, cardiomegaly, murmur
Other: Hepatomegaly, poor weight gain/failure to thrive, edema (periorbital, sacral), cool extremities

Management

  • Diuretics: Furosemide (loop), spironolactone (K⁺-sparing)
  • ACE inhibitors: Captopril, enalapril (reduce afterload, improve cardiac output)
  • Digoxin: Positive inotrope (narrow therapeutic window — monitor levels)
  • High-calorie formula: 24-30 kcal/oz to promote growth with less volume
  • Frequent small feedings: Decrease energy expenditure
  • Caloric rest: NG feeds if needed
Digoxin Toxicity in Infants: Vomiting, poor feeding, bradycardia, lethargy, arrhythmias. Hold digoxin if HR <90-100 (infants) or <70 (children). Antidote: Digoxin immune Fab.

🩺 Infective Endocarditis Prophylaxis

Indications (AHA Guidelines)

Prophylaxis ONLY for patients with:

  • Prosthetic cardiac valve
  • Prior infective endocarditis
  • Unrepaired cyanotic CHD (including palliative shunts)
  • Repaired CHD with residual defect at/ adjacent to prosthetic patch
  • Cardiac transplant with valvulopathy

Procedures requiring prophylaxis: Dental (manipulation of gingival tissue), respiratory tract (tonsillectomy), infected skin/soft tissue

Single dose amoxicillin 2g PO (or ampicillin/cefazolin if unable to take PO)
