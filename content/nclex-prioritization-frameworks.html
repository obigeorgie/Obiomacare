NCLEX Prioritization Strategies: ABCDE vs Maslow vs Nursing Process

Introduction

The #1 skill the NCLEX Next Generation tests is prioritization. Not memorization. Not content recall. The ability to look at a complex scenario and know — instantly — what needs to happen first.

After 15 years in the ER, I've seen nurses who can do this in their sleep. And I've seen new grads freeze when three patients need attention at once.

The difference? Frameworks. Three specific frameworks that turn chaos into clarity:

  1. ABCDE — For emergencies and deteriorating patients
  2. Maslow's Hierarchy — For stable patients with multiple needs
  3. Nursing Process — For systematic care planning

This guide breaks down exactly when to use each one, with real NCLEX-style scenarios.

Framework 1: ABCDE — The Emergency Approach

Use ABCDE when a patient is unstable, deteriorating, or in acute distress. This is the ER nurse's default framework. It should be yours too.

A — Airway

Question: Is the airway open and protected?

Red flags:

  • Stridor, gurgling, or absent breath sounds
  • Facial/neck swelling (angioedema, anaphylaxis)
  • Foreign body obstruction
  • Decreased LOC with inability to protect airway
  • Burns to face/neck (risk of airway compromise)

NCLEX scenario:

"A patient arrives after a house fire with facial burns and soot around the mouth. What is your FIRST action?"

Answer: Assess airway patency. Facial burns + soot = inhalation injury. Airway can close rapidly. Intubation may be needed BEFORE it closes.

B — Breathing

Question: Is the patient ventilating and oxygenating adequately?

Red flags:

  • RR < 10 or > 30
  • SpO2 < 92% on room air
  • Accessory muscle use
  • Asymmetric chest expansion
  • Cyanosis (late sign — don't wait for this)

NCLEX scenario:

"A post-op patient has RR 28, SpO2 89% on 2L NC. What is your FIRST action?"

Answer: Increase O2 and reassess. Breathing problem identified — intervene before it becomes a circulation problem.

C — Circulation

Question: Is perfusion adequate?

Red flags:

  • SBP < 90 or MAP < 65
  • HR > 120 or < 50
  • Cool, clammy skin
  • Altered LOC
  • Urine output < 30 mL/hr
  • Capillary refill > 3 seconds

NCLEX scenario:

"A patient with sepsis has BP 82/48, HR 128, cool extremities. What is your FIRST action?"

Answer: Start fluids (30 mL/kg crystalloid). This is septic shock — circulation is compromised.

D — Disability (Neurological)

Question: What is the level of consciousness?

Quick check: AVPU

  • Alert
  • Verbal response
  • Pain response
  • Unresponsive

NCLEX scenario:

"A patient with a head injury opens eyes to pain, mumbles words, and withdraws from stimulus. What is the Glasgow Coma Scale score?"

Answer: Eye opening = 2, Verbal = 3, Motor = 4. Total = 9. (Severe head injury — neurosurgical consult needed.)

E — Exposure

Question: Are there other injuries or conditions?

Actions:

  • Remove clothing to assess fully
  • Check for bleeding, burns, rashes
  • Maintain privacy and warmth
  • Logroll if spinal injury suspected

When ABCDE Overrides Everything Else

Even if a patient has severe pain (10/10), a non-healing wound, or emotional distress — ABCDE comes first every time.

A patient with:

  • Chest pain (8/10) + clear airway + normal breathing + stable vitals → Pain management is priority
  • Chest pain (8/10) + RR 32 + SpO2 88% + BP 88/52 → ABC first, then pain

Same symptom. Different priority. Context changes everything.

Framework 2: Maslow's Hierarchy — The Stable Patient Approach

When a patient is hemodynamically stable, use Maslow to prioritize competing needs.

Maslow Levels (Bottom = Highest Priority)

  1. Physiological — Air, water, food, sleep, elimination, shelter
  2. Safety — Security, stability, protection from harm
  3. Love/Belonging — Relationships, intimacy, community
  4. Esteem — Self-respect, achievement, recognition
  5. Self-Actualization — Fulfillment, creativity, purpose

NCLEX Application

Physiological needs ALWAYS come before safety needs.

  • Pain (physiological) > Fear of procedure (safety)
  • Hypoglycemia (physiological) > Anxiety about diagnosis (safety)
  • Urinary retention (physiological) > Need for privacy (safety/esteem)

Safety needs come before psychosocial needs.

  • Fall risk (safety) > Loneliness (belonging)
  • Medication error prevention (safety) > Need for praise (esteem)

NCLEX Scenario Using Maslow

"A patient has the following needs: severe pain (9/10), fear of surgery, urinary retention, and requests a chaplain visit. In what order should the nurse address these?"

Answer:

  1. Urinary retention (physiological — elimination)
  2. Severe pain (physiological — comfort, but elimination is more immediately harmful)
  3. Fear of surgery (safety — psychologic)
  4. Chaplain visit (belonging/esteem — psychosocial)

Wait — why urinary retention before pain?

Both are physiological. But urinary retention can cause:

  • Bladder rupture
  • Autonomic dysreflexia (in spinal patients)
  • Cardiac reflex (vagal response)
  • Infection

Severe pain is urgent but rarely acutely dangerous in the same way. Pain is 9/10 but stable vitals = manage after life-threatening issues.

Framework 3: Nursing Process — The Systematic Approach

Use the nursing process for care planning and long-term prioritization, not emergencies.

The 5 Steps

  1. Assessment — Collect data (subjective + objective)
  2. Diagnosis — Identify nursing diagnosis (NOT medical diagnosis)
  3. Planning — Set goals and outcomes
  4. Implementation — Execute interventions
  5. Evaluation — Did the interventions work?

NCLEX Scenario Using Nursing Process

"A nurse is caring for a patient 2 days post-op abdominal surgery. The patient has not ambulated, is refusing to deep breathe, and the incision is red with purulent drainage. What is the FIRST step the nurse should take?"

Answer: Assess further. (Assessment comes before action.)

  • Check vital signs (fever?)
  • Check white blood cell count
  • Assess pain level
  • Check if patient understands the importance of ambulation and deep breathing

Then: Based on assessment, intervene. Wound infection + immobility + shallow breathing = high risk for pneumonia and wound dehiscence.

Quick Reference: Which Framework When?

Situation Framework Example
Unstable/deteriorating patient ABCDE Code blue, trauma, sepsis
Stable patient with multiple needs Maslow Med-surg floor, clinic
Care planning, documentation Nursing Process Discharge planning, care plans
Acute change in condition ABCDE first, then reassess Post-op complication
Patient education priorities Maslow Teach-back sessions

Practice Questions

Question 1:

"A nurse has four patients. Which should be assessed FIRST?"

  • Patient A: Post-op day 2, pain 3/10, vitals stable
  • Patient B: Fresh post-op, BP 88/52, HR 128, cool skin
  • Patient C: COPD exacerbation, SpO2 89% on 2L, RR 28
  • Patient D: Diabetic, glucose 312, asymptomatic

Answer: Patient B — Circulation compromised (shock). ABCDE framework. Then Patient C (Breathing).

Question 2:

"A stable patient needs: wound dressing change, pain medication (6/10), emotional support after cancer diagnosis, and assistance to bathroom. What is the FIRST priority?"

Answer: Assistance to bathroom — Physiological (elimination) > Pain (physiological) > Emotional support (safety/psychosocial). Maslow framework.

Question 3:

"A patient arrives with chest pain, diaphoresis, and nausea. The nurse's FIRST action should be:"

A) Administer morphine

B) Obtain 12-lead ECG

C) Start IV access

D) Assess airway, breathing, circulation

Answer: D — ABC first. Always. Even with classic MI symptoms, rule out airway/breathing/circulation issues first. Then B (ECG within 10 minutes for MI).

Summary

The NCLEX doesn't just test if you know these frameworks. It tests if you know when to use each one.

  • Unstable → ABCDE
  • Stable with multiple needs → Maslow
  • Care planning → Nursing Process

Master these three frameworks, and prioritization questions become automatic.
