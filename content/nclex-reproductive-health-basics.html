NCLEX Reproductive Health Basics

Female reproductive anatomy, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy basics, STIs, and contraception — the fundamentals the NCLEX expects you to know cold.

1. Female Reproductive Anatomy

  • Ovaries — produce ova and estrogen/progesterone; ovulation occurs mid-cycle.
  • Fallopian tubes — site of fertilization; ectopic pregnancy risk if blocked.
  • Uterus — endometrium sheds during menstruation; fundus landmarks matter in postpartum assessment.
  • Cervix — dilates/effaces in labor; screening via Pap smear (HPV link).
  • Vagina — acidic pH protects against infection.

2. The Menstrual Cycle

  • Follicular phase (days 1–13): FSH stimulates follicle growth; estrogen rises.
  • Ovulation (day ~14): LH surge triggers release of the ovum.
  • Luteal phase (days 15–28): progesterone dominates; corpus luteum forms; hCG maintains the corpus luteum if pregnancy occurs.
  • NCLEX angle: timing of ovulation, basal body temperature shift, and fertile window teaching.

3. Pregnancy Basics

  • Signs of pregnancy: presumptive (amenorrhea, nausea), probable (positive hCG), positive (fetal heart tones, ultrasound).
  • Key labs: hCG, rubella titer, blood type/Rh, CBC, GBS screening at 35–37 weeks.
  • Warning signs requiring immediate care: vaginal bleeding, severe headache, visual changes, epigastric pain, decreased fetal movement, ruptured membranes.
  • Rh-negative mothers: RhoGAM at 28 weeks and within 72 hours after delivery if the infant is Rh-positive.

4. Common STIs — Nursing Considerations

  • Chlamydia/Gonorrhea: often asymptomatic; screen sexually active women <25; treat partner(s); reportable.
  • Syphilis: stages (primary chancre → secondary rash → latent → tertiary); treat with penicillin; congenital syphilis prevention in pregnancy.
  • HIV: prevention counseling, PrEP discussion, viral suppression during pregnancy to reduce transmission.
  • HSV: suppressive therapy; cesarean consideration with active lesions near delivery.
  • Patient education: vaccination (HPV), condom use, partner treatment, follow-up testing.

5. Contraception Overview

  • Hormonal (pill, ring, patch, implant, IUD): contraindications include history of thromboembolism, certain cancers, liver disease.
  • Barrier (condoms, diaphragm): only condoms prevent STIs.
  • Emergency contraception: within 72 hours (copper IUD up to 5 days).
  • Teaching: adherence, side effects, when to seek help (e.g., leg pain with hormonal methods).

6. Nursing Considerations

  • Screen and educate without judgment; use inclusive, evidence-based language.
  • Confidentiality: adolescent rights vary by jurisdiction — know your state.
  • Preconception counseling: folic acid 400–800 mcg daily before conception.
