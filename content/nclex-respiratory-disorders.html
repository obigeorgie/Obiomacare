NCLEX Respiratory Disorders: Complete Study Guide

COPD, asthma, pneumonia, tuberculosis, and pulmonary embolism — pathophysiology, nursing priorities, and NCLEX traps.

1. COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)

📋 Chronic Bronchitis vs. Emphysema

FeatureChronic Bronchitis ("Blue Bloater")Emphysema ("Pink Puffer")
Primary issueExcessive mucus productionAlveolar wall destruction
AppearanceCyanotic, edematousThin, barrel-chested
PaCO₂Elevated (retained)Normal or low
ComplicationCor pulmonaleRespiratory failure
💡 Mnemonic — "COPD"
Chronic cough | O₂ carefully (keep 1-2L, avoid suppressing hypoxic drive) | Pursed-lip breathing | Diet high-calorie, low-carb
✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Low-flow O₂ (1-2L NC) — NEVER high flow in chronic CO₂ retainers!
• Pursed-lip and diaphragmatic breathing
• High-Fowler's or tripod position
• Bronchodilators first, then mucolytics, then chest PT
• Monitor ABGs — watch for respiratory acidosis
• Smoking cessation #1 intervention
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Giving high-flow O₂ to a COPD patient can cause apnea. These patients rely on hypoxic drive. Always use low-flow O₂ (1-2L) unless acute PE or other exception.

2. Asthma

📋 Acute Asthma Exacerbation

Symptoms: Wheezing, dyspnea, chest tightness, tachypnea, use of accessory muscles, anxiety

Patho: Bronchospasm + inflammation + mucus plugging → airway obstruction → ventilation-perfusion mismatch.

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• SABA (albuterol) — first line, rapid relief
• Corticosteroids (systemic if severe)
• O₂ to keep SpO₂ > 92%
• Peak expiratory flow monitoring
• IV fluids if dehydrated
• Epinephrine or magnesium sulfate for severe cases
🚨 NCLEX Trap: A "silent chest" (no wheezing) in asthma is a LATE, DANGEROUS sign — means no air movement. Also, anxiety can precede respiratory failure — don't just reassure, reassess!

3. Pneumonia

📋 Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

TypeOrganismClassic Presentation
Lobar (typical)Streptococcus pneumoniaeSudden onset, high fever, rusty sputum, lobar consolidation
Bronchopneumonia (atypical)Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, LegionellaGradual onset, dry cough, GI symptoms, normal WBC
ViralInfluenza, RSVInterstitial pattern, wheezing, crackles
AspirationAnaerobesRight lower lobe, foul-smelling sputum, alcoholism/LOC risk
✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Antibiotics within 4-6 hours of admission (CMS core measure)
• O₂ therapy, respiratory monitoring
• Incentive spirometry q1h while awake
• Turn, cough, deep breathe
• Hydration (unless contraindicated)
• Pneumonia vaccine for high-risk patients before discharge
💡 CURB-65 Severity Criteria:
Confusion | Urea >19 | Respiratory rate ≥30 | BP <90/60 | Age ≥65 — Score ≥2 = admit

4. Pulmonary Embolism (PE)

📋 Acute PE

Symptoms — "PLEURITIC"
Pleuritic chest pain | Low SpO₂ | Elevated HR | Unexplained dyspnea | Restlessness | Immobilized (recent surgery, travel) | Tachypnea | Increased D-dimer | Calf pain (DVT)

Patho: Thrombus (usually from DVT) lodges in pulmonary vasculature → V/Q mismatch → hypoxemia → right heart strain.

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• High-flow O₂ (non-rebreather if needed)
• IV access, labs (D-dimer, ABG, troponin, BNP)
• Anticoagulation (heparin drip or LMWH)
• Absolute bed rest — NO leg massage
• Prepare for thrombolytics or embolectomy if massive PE
• Monitor for hemodynamic instability
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Massive PE presents with hypotension, syncope, or cardiac arrest. Don't wait for imaging — if suspected with hemodynamic compromise, treat immediately. And NEVER massage a leg with suspected DVT!

5. Tuberculosis (TB)

📋 Active TB vs. Latent TB

FeatureLatent TBActive TB
SymptomsNoneCough >3 weeks, hemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss, fever
ContagiousNoYes (airborne precautions)
Chest X-rayNormal or calcified granulomaCavitary lesions, upper lobe infiltrates
TreatmentINH or rifapentine (3-4 months)RIPE: Rifampin, INH, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol (2 months), then RI (4 months)
✅ Nursing Priorities:
• AIRBORNE precautions (N95, negative pressure room)
• Sputum AFB x3 for diagnosis
• DOT (Directly Observed Therapy) for adherence
• Monitor liver function (hepatotoxicity from RIPE)
• Educate: Rifampin turns body fluids orange-red
🚨 NCLEX Trap: TB is AIRBORNE, not droplet. Use N95, not surgical mask. A patient is no longer contagious after 2-3 weeks of effective therapy and 3 negative sputum AFB smears.
