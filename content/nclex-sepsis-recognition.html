NCLEX Sepsis Recognition: High-Yield Guide

What sepsis is, the warning signs the NCLEX expects you to recognize, and when patients need immediate care.

1. What Sepsis Is

Sepsis is the body's overactive and extreme response to an infection.

2. Warning Signs

Get medical care right away if the patient might have sepsis or if the infection is not getting better or is getting worse. Warning signs include:

  • Shortness of breath
  • Confusion or disorientation
  • Extreme pain or discomfort
  • Fever, shivering, or feeling very cold
  • Clammy or sweaty skin

Many of the signs and symptoms of sepsis can also be caused by other medical conditions — but the presence of these signs in a patient with an infection warrants urgent evaluation.

3. Provider Evaluation

  • Check vital signs: temperature, blood pressure, heart rate, and breathing
  • Likely order lab tests that check for signs of infection or organ damage
  • May order imaging tests such as an x-ray or a CT scan to find the location of the infection

4. Nursing Priority

Sepsis is a medical emergency. When a patient with an infection reports or exhibits the warning signs, escalate immediately — do not wait for the infection to worsen. If the patient thinks they might have sepsis, or their infection is not getting better or is getting worse, medical care is needed right away.
