NCLEX Trauma Nursing

Complete clinical judgment guide: triage, shock, burns, spinal injuries, head trauma, and mass casualty

NCLEX Clinical Judgment Focus: Trauma questions test rapid assessment, prioritization, and life-saving interventions. The NGN loves trauma scenarios with multiple system involvement. Expect 2-4 questions.

1. Primary Survey: ABCDE

ABCDE Trauma Assessment:
Airway with cervical spine protection
Breathing and ventilation
Circulation with hemorrhage control
Disability (neurologic status)
Exposure/environmental control

B: BreathingRate, effort, breath sounds, SpO2O2, needle decompression if tension pneumothorax, chest tube

C-Spine Rule: All trauma patients are presumed to have a spinal injury until proven otherwise. Maintain inline stabilization during airway maneuvers.

Neurogenic Shock (NOT spinal shock):
• Loss of sympathetic tone below injury
• Bradycardia + hypotension + warm dry skin (unique combination)
• Treat: Fluid resuscitation + vasopressors (phenylephrine, dopamine)
• No atropine needed — bradycardia is physiologic

Fluid Resuscitation Targets:
• SBP > 90 mmHg (or MAP > 65)
• HR < 120
• Urine output > 0.5 mL/kg/hr
• Improved mental status
• Lactate clearance

4. Head Trauma

Signs of Basilar Skull Fracture:
• Raccoon eyes (periorbital ecchymosis)
• Battle's sign (postauricular ecchymosis)
• CSF rhinorrhea/otorrhea
• Hemotympanum
• Cranial nerve deficits

5. Spinal Cord Injury

SPINAL SHOCK vs NEUROGENIC SHOCK:
• Spinal shock: Temporary loss of reflexes below injury (flaccid paralysis, areflexia)
• Neurogenic shock: Loss of sympathetic tone (bradycardia, hypotension, warm skin)

Autonomic Dysreflexia

Emergency! Occurs in injuries at T6 or above.
• Trigger: Bladder distension, constipation, tight clothing, ingrown toenail
• Signs: Severe hypertension (up to 300 systolic), bradycardia, pounding headache, flushing above injury, pale skin below injury
• Treatment: Sit patient up, loosen clothing, check bladder, check bowels, nifedipine or nitroglycerin PRN

6. Burns

Parkland Formula (First 24 Hours)

Fluid = 4 mL × kg × %TBSA burned
• Give 1/2 in first 8 hours (from time of burn)
• Give 1/2 in next 16 hours
• PLUS maintenance fluids

Inhalation Injury Suspect:
• Burns in enclosed space
• Facial burns, singed nasal hairs, soot in mouth
• Hoarseness, stridor, cough
• Carbonaceous sputum

Action: Early intubation before airway edema develops

7. NGN Clinical Judgment Case Study

Case: Multi-System Trauma

Scenario: A 28-year-old male is brought to the ED after a motorcycle accident. He was not wearing a helmet. GCS is 12 (E3, V4, M5). HR 118, BP 88/60, RR 28, SpO2 91% on room air. There is obvious deformity of the left femur and blood pooling under the patient. He complains of severe abdominal pain and can't feel his legs.

1. What is the priority intervention?
A. Splint the femur fracture
B. Establish two large-bore IVs and begin fluid resuscitation
C. Prepare for intubation
D. Obtain a CT of the head
2. After initial stabilization, the patient's BP is 72/48 and HR is 132. His abdomen is distended and rigid. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A. Tension pneumothorax
B. Hemorrhagic shock from intra-abdominal bleeding
C. Neurogenic shock
D. Cardiogenic shock
3. The patient is taken to surgery. Post-op day 2, his urine output drops to 15 mL/hr despite adequate fluids. His potassium is 6.2 mEq/L. What is the priority intervention?
A. Administer calcium gluconate
B. Give insulin and D50
C. Start dialysis
D. Administer sodium polystyrene sulfonate (Kayexalate)
