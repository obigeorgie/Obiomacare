Trauma Nursing & ATLS

Primary survey, hemorrhage control, shock, burns, spinal injuries, and triage — emergency nursing at its most intense.

💡 Remember: "In trauma, minutes matter. The first hour is the Golden Hour. Do the right things in the right order."

🚨 ATLS Primary Survey — ABCDE

The ONLY acceptable order. Never deviate.

A — Airway with C-Spine Protection

Assess: Is airway patent? Can patient speak? Look for obstruction (blood, vomit, teeth, tongue), facial fractures, stridor, altered LOC.

Interventions:
  1. Jaw thrust (NOT head tilt-chin lift if spinal injury suspected)
  2. Remove visible obstructions (suction, forceps)
  3. Oropharyngeal (Guedel) or nasopharyngeal airway if unconscious
  4. Intubation if GCS ≤8, severe facial trauma, or airway edema
  5. Surgical airway (cricothyroidotomy) if can't intubate
CRITICAL: ALL trauma patients get manual inline stabilization (MILS) until C-spine is cleared. Collar + backboard. Any patient with blunt trauma and altered LOC gets full spinal precautions.

B — Breathing & Ventilation

Assess: Rate, depth, symmetry, breath sounds, SpO₂, chest wall movement, tracheal position.

Life-Threatening Issues to Find:
  • Tension pneumothorax: Tracheal deviation, absent breath sounds, distended neck veins, hypotension → needle decompression NOW
  • Massive hemothorax: Dullness to percussion, absent breath sounds, hypotension → chest tube + blood for MTP
  • Flail chest: Paradoxical chest wall movement → intubation + PEEP
  • Open pneumothorax (sucking chest wound): Cover with occlusive dressing, tape 3 sides (flutter valve), chest tube
  • Cardiac tamponade: Beck's triad (hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds) → pericardiocentesis

C — Circulation with Hemorrhage Control

Assess: Pulse (rate, quality), BP, skin (color, temp, moisture), capillary refill, external bleeding.

Immediate Actions:
  1. Apply direct pressure to external bleeding
  2. Two large-bore IVs (14g or 16g) — antecubital or saphenous cutdown if needed
  3. Draw blood for type & cross, CBC, coags, BMP, lactate, β-hCG (all females)
  4. Start warm crystalloids (0.9% NS or LR). If no response after 2L → blood products
  5. Tourniquet for extremity hemorrhage (if direct pressure fails). Note time applied.
NCLEX Trap: In hypotensive trauma patients, warm fluids prevent hypothermia (which worsens coagulopathy). Never give cold fluids. Also, permissive hypotension in penetrating trauma: maintain SBP ~90 (just enough to perfuse organs) until hemorrhage is controlled. Too much fluid dilutes clotting factors and raises BP, dislodging clots.

D — Disability (Neurologic Status)

Assess: GCS, pupillary response, motor/sensory function, spinal level.

E — Exposure & Environmental Control

Completely undress patient. Look for hidden injuries: posterior stab wounds, seatbelt marks (bowel/mesenteric injury), burns, deformities.

Prevent hypothermia:
  • Remove wet clothing
  • Warm blankets, Bair Hugger
  • Warm IV fluids
  • Keep room temperature elevated
Hypothermia + acidosis + coagulopathy = Lethal Triad of Trauma. Prevent at all costs.

🧠 Neurogenic Shock vs Spinal Shock: "Neurogenic = circulatory problem (hypotension, bradycardia). Spinal = neurologic problem (flaccid paralysis, areflexia below injury)." They're different things. A patient can have both.

Parkland Formula (24-hour fluid resuscitation):
4 mL × weight(kg) × %TBSA burned
Give half in first 8 hours from time of burn (not time of arrival). Give second half over next 16 hours.
Use Lactated Ringer's (not NS — LR has lactate which converts to bicarbonate).

Burn Priorities

First 24 Hours:
  1. Airway first — inhalation injury? (soot in mouth/nose, singed nasal hairs, hoarseness, stridor, carboxyhemoglobin)
  2. Fluid resuscitation (Parkland)
  3. Foley catheter — monitor UO (goal: 0.5 mL/kg/hr adults, 1 mL/kg/hr children)
  4. Tetanus prophylaxis
  5. Pain management (IV opioids — IM absorption unpredictable)
  6. Escharotomy if circumferential burns restricting chest/limb
Inhalation Injury: Thermal burns above the glottis (face, nares). Chemical/smoke injury below glottis. Both require intubation early (before edema closes airway). Carboxyhemoglobin >15% or COHb symptoms = 100% O₂ (reduces CO half-life from 4 hours to 40 minutes).

🏥 Triage

START Triage (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)

Order of assessment:
  1. Can they walk? → If yes → GREEN (Minor/Delayed)
  2. Respirations: If RR >30 or absent → RED (Immediate) (open airway first for absent)
  3. Perfusion (radial pulse): If absent → RED (Immediate)
  4. Mental status: Can't follow commands → RED (Immediate)
  5. Can walk + normal RR + has pulse + follows commands → YELLOW (Delayed)
Black Tag: Dead or expectant (unsalvageable given resources). In mass casualty, resources go to those most likely to survive. This is ethically difficult but necessary.

🎯 Clinical Judgment Case Studies

NCLEX-Style Trauma

Case 1: MVC Patient

A 25-year-old restrained driver arrives after head-on collision. GCS 13 (E3, V4, M6). BP 98/60, HR 128, RR 24, SpO₂ 94% on RA. Chest contusion from seatbelt. Abdomen soft but tender. Pelvis stable.

What is your priority after airway is confirmed patent?

  1. Chest X-ray to assess for pneumothorax
  2. CT scan of the abdomen
  3. Start two large-bore IVs and crystalloid bolus
  4. Insert nasogastric tube
Answer: 3 — Start two large-bore IVs and crystalloid bolus.

HR 128 + BP 98/60 = Class III hemorrhagic shock (30-40% blood loss). After airway (A) and breathing (B), circulation (C) is next. Two 14-16g IVs + warm LR/NS bolus. Then blood if no response. CXR, CT, NG can wait — shock kills first.
NCLEX-Style Burns

Case 2: Burn Patient

A 30-year-old (70 kg) has burns: anterior chest (9%), both arms (18%), anterior thighs (9%). Burn occurred 2 hours ago. Current vitals: HR 118, BP 104/68, UO 20 mL/hr.

How much fluid should he receive in the first 8 hours?

  1. 3,780 mL
  2. 5,040 mL
  3. 7,560 mL
  4. 10,080 mL
Answer: 3 — 7,560 mL total, with 3,780 mL in first 8 hours.

TBSA = 9 + 18 + 9 = 36%.
Parkland: 4 mL × 70 kg × 36 = 10,080 mL in 24 hours.
Half in first 8 hours: 5,040 mL.
Wait — burn occurred 2 hours ago, so 2 hours have passed. He should have received 1,260 mL already. Remaining for first 8 hours: 3,780 mL over next 6 hours = 630 mL/hr.

NCLEX usually asks total first 8 hours = 5,040 mL (half of total). But since 2 hours passed, remaining is 3,780 mL. Always clarify timing!
SATA: A patient with T4 complete spinal cord injury presents with BP 82/50, HR 52, warm dry skin. Which interventions are appropriate? (Select all that apply.)
  1. Rapid fluid bolus 1-2L
  2. Atropine for bradycardia
  3. Trendelenburg position
  4. Norepinephrine infusion
  5. Warm blankets
  6. High-flow oxygen
Correct: A, B, D, E, F

This is neurogenic shock (T4 = above T6). Loss of sympathetic tone → vasodilation + bradycardia.
A: Yes — fluids to fill dilated vasculature.
B: Yes — atropine for symptomatic bradycardia.
C: NO — Trendelenburg is controversial, not recommended.
D: Yes — vasopressors for persistent hypotension.
E: Yes — warm blankets (vasodilation causes heat loss).
F: Yes — O₂ for tissue perfusion.
