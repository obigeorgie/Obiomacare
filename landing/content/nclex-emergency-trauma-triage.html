NCLEX Emergency & Trauma Nursing

Trauma assessment, triage, mass casualty incidents, shock, and hemorrhage control for NCLEX success.

1. Primary Survey (ABCDE)

📋 Trauma Primary Survey

✅ A — Airway with C-spine protection:
• Assess patency; clear obstruction
• Jaw thrust (NOT head tilt-chin lift if spinal injury suspected)
• Insert oral/nasopharyngeal airway if needed
• Intubate if GCS ≤8 or airway compromise
• Assume C-spine injury until cleared — maintain inline stabilization
✅ B — Breathing:
• Inspect, palpate, auscultate chest
• Look for: tracheal deviation, JVD, chest wall movement, crepitus, open wounds
• Treat immediately: tension pneumothorax (needle decompression), open pneumothorax (occlusive dressing), flail chest (positive pressure ventilation)
✅ C — Circulation:
• Control external bleeding (direct pressure, tourniquet if extremity)
• Assess pulses, skin color, capillary refill, BP
• Two large-bore IVs (14–16g); warmed fluids; blood products if ongoing hemorrhage
• Foley catheter (after urethral injury ruled out)
• Baseline Hgb/Hct (may be normal initially — hemodilution hasn't occurred yet)
✅ D — Disability:
• GCS, pupil size/reactivity, motor function
• Blood glucose (hypoglycemia can mimic neurological deficit)
✅ E — Exposure/Environment:
• Completely undress patient
• Prevent hypothermia (warm blankets, warmed fluids, room temp)
• Log-roll to inspect back

2. Shock

📋 Types of Shock

TypePathoSignsTreatment
HypovolemicFluid/blood loss↓ BP, ↑ HR, cool/clammy, ↓ UOP, anxiousFluid resuscitation, control bleeding, blood products
CardiogenicHeart failure/pump failure↓ BP, ↑ HR, JVD, crackles, cool/clammyInotropes, diuretics, IABP, treat cause
Distributive (Septic)Vasodilation, leaky capillaries↓ BP, ↑ HR, WARM/flushed, bounding pulses, feverAntibiotics within 1h, fluids, vasopressors, source control
Distributive (Anaphylactic)Massive vasodilation↓ BP, bronchospasm, angioedema, urticariaEpinephrine IM, fluids, O₂, antihistamines
Distributive (Neurogenic)Loss of sympathetic tone (spinal cord injury)↓ BP, bradycardia, WARM/dry skin, priapism, paralysisFluids, vasopressors (phenylephrine, norepinephrine), atropine for bradycardia
ObstructivePhysical blockage of circulation↓ BP, ↑ HR, JVD, pulsus paradoxus (tamponade)Relieve obstruction (pericardiocentesis, thrombolysis, needle decompression)
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Neurogenic shock (from spinal cord injury above T6) causes BRADYCARDIA + hypotension + WARM DRY SKIN (loss of sympathetic tone). This is the ONLY shock where the skin is warm and dry. Do NOT give atropine unless symptomatic bradycardia (HR <50 with symptoms). Priority is fluids and vasopressors.

3. Triage

📋 Emergency Department Triage (ESI — Emergency Severity Index)

LevelDescriptionExamplesTime to Provider
ESI 1ResuscitationCardiac arrest, intubation, severe traumaImmediate
ESI 2EmergentChest pain, respiratory distress, altered mental status, severe pain<15 min
ESI 3UrgentAbdominal pain, asthma (moderate), moderate laceration<30 min
ESI 4Less urgentSimple laceration, UTI symptoms, minor complaints<60 min
ESI 5Non-urgentSuture removal, medication refill, minor rashes<120 min

📋 START Triage (Mass Casualty)

💡 Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment:
Step 1: Can patient walk? → MINOR (green tag) — move to treatment area
Step 2: Respirations? No → open airway. Still no? → EXPECTANT/DECEASED (black)
Step 3: RR >30? → IMMEDIATE (red tag)
Step 4: Perfusion — radial pulse absent or cap refill >2 sec? → IMMEDIATE (red tag)
Step 5: Mental status — unable to follow commands? → IMMEDIATE (red tag)
If none of above: → DELAYED (yellow tag)
Tag ColorPriorityMeaning
🔴 RedImmediateLife-threatening but salvageable; needs treatment within 1 hour
🟡 YellowDelayedSerious but stable; can wait for treatment
🟢 GreenMinorWalking wounded; minimal treatment needed
⚫ BlackExpectant/DeceasedUnsurvivable or already deceased
🚨 NCLEX Trap: In mass casualty triage, resources are limited. The goal is to do the MOST GOOD for the MOST PEOPLE. This means some patients who would normally be prioritized may be triaged as expectant (black tag) if they would consume resources that could save multiple others. This is ethically difficult but necessary in disaster triage.

4. Hemorrhage Control

📋 Stop the Bleed

✅ Hierarchy of Hemorrhage Control:
1. Direct pressure: First and best method; hold firm pressure for at least 5 minutes
2. Wound packing + pressure: For junctional wounds (groin, axilla, neck)
3. Tourniquet: For extremity hemorrhage not controlled by pressure; apply 2–3 inches above wound; mark time
4. Hemostatic dressings: Impregnated with clotting agents (kaolin, chitosan)
5. Pelvic binder: For suspected pelvic fracture with hemorrhage
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Tourniquets are safe for up to 2 hours without significant complications. Do NOT loosen a tourniquet "to let blood flow" — this causes reperfusion injury and can worsen bleeding. The tourniquet stays ON until the patient reaches definitive care. Write the time of application on the patient.

5. Specific Trauma

📋 Head Injury

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Maintain cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP = MAP − ICP; goal >60)
• Prevent secondary injury: hypoxia, hypotension, hyperthermia, hypoglycemia
• HOB 30° (promotes venous drainage, lowers ICP)
• Avoid neck flexion/rotation, Trendelenburg
• Mannitol or hypertonic saline for ↑ ICP
• Serial neuro checks (GCS, pupils, motor)
• Prevent agitation/coughing/straining (all increase ICP)

📋 Spinal Cord Injury

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Maintain inline stabilization until spine cleared
• Assess level of injury (highest level with intact sensation + motor)
• Monitor for neurogenic shock (see Shock section above)
• Monitor for spinal shock (areflexia, flaccid paralysis below injury — temporary, resolves in days-weeks)
• Autonomic dysreflexia (T6 and above): sudden severe HTN, headache, bradycardia, diaphoresis, flushing — EMERGENCY; sit patient up, loosen clothing, check for bladder/bowel distention, remove noxious stimuli
🚨 NCLEX Trap: Autonomic dysreflexia is a MEDICAL EMERGENCY in patients with spinal cord injury at T6 or above. The most common trigger is a DISTENDED BLADDER or bowel. Priority actions: sit patient up (lowers BP), check/relieve bladder/bowel, remove tight clothing, monitor BP q2-5 min, notify provider. Nifedipine or nitroglycerin for severe HTN. Do NOT let patient lie flat — this worsens hypertension.
