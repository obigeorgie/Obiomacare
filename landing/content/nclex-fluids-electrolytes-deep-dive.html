NCLEX Fluids & Electrolytes Deep Dive

IV therapy, fluid imbalances, electrolyte replacement, TPN, and calculations for NCLEX success.

1. Fluid Compartments

💡 Fluid Movement: Water follows sodium (osmosis) and moves toward areas of higher solute concentration. Albumin is the main plasma protein that maintains oncotic pressure (keeps fluid in vessels).

🚨 NCLEX Trap: D5W is isotonic in the bag but becomes hypotonic once dextrose is metabolized. It does NOT replace electrolytes. For a patient needing both fluids AND electrolytes, use LR or NS, not D5W alone. For hyponatremia, give NS (isotonic) not D5W (which dilutes sodium further).

📋 IV Complications

ComplicationSignsActions
InfiltrationCool, pale, edema, pain, slow infusionStop infusion, remove catheter, elevate limb, warm compress, restart at new site

✅ Air Embolism Response: This is an EMERGENCY. Position patient on LEFT SIDE with head down (Durant maneuver) — this traps air in the right atrium, preventing it from entering pulmonary circulation.

📋 Dehydration vs Overhydration

Hypovolemia (Dehydration)Hypervolemia (Overhydration)
SignsDry mucous membranes, decreased skin turgor, orthostatic BP, tachycardia, decreased UOP, concentrated urine, weaknessDyspnea, crackles, JVD, edema, weight gain, bounding pulse, HTN
Lab findings↑ BUN, ↑ Hct, ↑ Na⁺ (if hypernatremic), ↑ urine specific gravity↓ Hct (hemodilution), ↓ Na⁺, ↓ serum osmolality
CausesVomiting, diarrhea, hemorrhage, burns, diuretics, NPOHeart failure, renal failure, excess IVF, corticosteroids

🚨 CRITICAL NCLEX Trap: Potassium chloride (KCl) is NEVER given IV push or undiluted. It causes cardiac arrest. Always dilute in appropriate volume (NS or D5W), never exceed max infusion rate, and monitor cardiac rhythm. This is one of the most tested safety concepts on NCLEX.

5. TPN & Enteral Nutrition

📋 Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)

✅ TPN Nursing Priorities:
• Dedicated central line (PICC, CVC, port) — HIGH dextrose irritates peripheral veins
• NEVER piggyback medications into TPN line
• Check blood glucose q6h (high dextrose → hyperglycemia)
• Monitor for refeeding syndrome (hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia when nutrition restarted after starvation)
• Wean TPN gradually (don't stop abruptly — risk hypoglycemia)
• Strict aseptic technique — lipid emulsion supports bacterial growth
• Change tubing q24h; lipid tubing q12h
🚨 NCLEX Trap: If TPN is interrupted or stopped, do NOT just turn it off. Give D10W at same rate to prevent hypoglycemia until TPN is resumed or PO intake is adequate. This "bridge" is critical for patient safety.

📋 Enteral Nutrition (Tube Feeding)

✅ Nursing Priorities:
• Confirm tube placement BEFORE each feeding (pH <5.5 or X-ray for initial placement)
• Elevate HOB 30–45° during feeding and 1 hour after
• Check gastric residual q4h (hold if >500 mL or per protocol)
• Flush with 30 mL water before and after meds/feeding
• Never add medications directly to formula bag
• Monitor for diarrhea, constipation, aspiration
• Daily weight, I&O, electrolytes

6. IV Flow Rate Calculations

📋 Dimensional Analysis Method

💡 Formula: gtt/min = (Total mL × Drop factor) ÷ Total minutes
Example: 1000 mL NS over 8 hours with macrodrip (15 gtt/mL)
(1000 mL × 15 gtt/mL) ÷ 480 min = 31 gtt/min
✅ Pump Settings:
• mL/hr = Total mL ÷ Total hours
• Example: 1000 mL over 8 hours = 125 mL/hr
• Always round to nearest whole number for pumps
