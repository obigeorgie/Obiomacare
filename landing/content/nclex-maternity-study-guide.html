NCLEX Maternity Study Guide

Pregnancy, labor & delivery, postpartum, newborn care, and high-risk OB conditions — everything you need for the NCLEX.

1. Pregnancy Physiology

📋 Cardiovascular Changes

  • Cardiac output ↑ 30-50% (peaks at 28-32 weeks)
  • Blood volume ↑ 40-50% (physiologic anemia is normal)
  • BP ↓ slightly in 2nd trimester (lowest at 24-32 weeks), returns to baseline by 3rd trimester
  • HR ↑ 10-15 bpm
⚠️ BP >140/90 after 20 weeks = hypertension in pregnancy
• Chronic hypertension: Before 20 weeks or persists 12 weeks postpartum
• Gestational hypertension: After 20 weeks, no proteinuria
• Preeclampsia: Hypertension + proteinuria (>300mg/24h) or end-organ dysfunction

2. Preeclampsia & Eclampsia

📋 Preeclampsia Assessment

ParameterMildSevere
BP≥140/90 (×2, 4h apart)≥160/110 (×2, 4h apart)
Proteinuria≥300 mg/24h or 1+ dipstick≥2 g/24h or 3-4+ dipstick
SymptomsUsually asymptomaticHA, visual changes, RUQ/epigastric pain, oliguria, hyperreflexia
PlateletsNormal<100,000
LFTsNormalElevated (AST/ALT >2× normal)
CreatinineNormal>1.1 mg/dL
✅ Nursing Priorities for Preeclampsia:
• Seizure precautions (padded side rails, dim lights, quiet environment)
• Magnesium sulfate prophylaxis (check deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate >12, urine output >30 mL/hr)
• Monitor BP q15-30min if severe
• Monitor for signs of worsening (HELLP syndrome)
• Strict I&O, daily weights
• Fetal monitoring (NST, BPP)
🧠 MNEMONIC: "MAG" — Magnesium Toxicity Signs
Mental status changes (lethargy, confusion)
Absent reflexes (patellar reflex lost first)
Greatly decreased respirations (<12/min)
Antidote: Calcium gluconate 1g IV

4. Fetal Heart Rate Patterns

PatternDescriptionSignificanceNursing Action
Early DecelerationsMirror image of contraction, gradual onsetHead compression — NORMALContinue monitoring
Late DecelerationsBegin after peak of contraction, gradualUteroplacental insufficiency — NONREASSURINGPosition change (left lateral), O₂, IV fluids, stop oxytocin, notify provider
Variable DecelerationsAbrupt onset, V-shaped, variable timingCord compression — NONREASSURINGPosition change (knee-chest, left lateral), amnioinfusion if recurrent, notify provider
Prolonged Deceleration>2 min but <10 minUteroplacental insufficiency, cord compression, maternal hypotensionImmediate interventions, prepare for delivery if not resolved
⚠️ LATE DECELERATIONS = Priority Action
When you see late decelerations on the NCLEX, think: STOP the oxytocin, turn patient to left side, give O₂, increase IV fluids, and notify the provider. Late decels indicate placental insufficiency and require immediate intervention.

5. Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

📋 Causes: "4 T's"

CauseDescriptionIntervention
Tone (most common)Uterine atony — boggy, soft uterusUterine massage, oxytocin, methylergonovine (contraindicated if HTN), carboprost (contraindicated if asthma), misoprostol
TraumaLacerations, hematomas, uterine inversionRepair lacerations, manual replacement of inverted uterus
TissueRetained placenta, clotsManual extraction, D&C
ThrombinCoagulopathy (DIC)Blood products, treat underlying cause
✅ Nursing Priorities for PPH:
• Massage fundus until firm
• Check for bladder distention (catheterize if needed)
• Monitor vital signs q15 min
• Measure blood loss (weigh pads: 1 g = 1 mL)
• Maintain IV access, prepare for blood transfusion
• Keep patient warm (prevent coagulopathy from hypothermia)

7. High-Risk Conditions

📋 Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)

  • Screen at 24-28 weeks (1-hour 50g glucose challenge)
  • If >130-140 mg/dL → 3-hour 100g OGTT (need 2 abnormal values)
  • Target glucose: Fasting <95, 1-hour <140, 2-hour <120
  • Diet first, then insulin if needed (glyburide and metformin may be used but insulin is gold standard)
  • Complications: Macrosomia, shoulder dystocia, birth trauma, neonatal hypoglycemia

📋 Placenta Previa vs Abruptio Placentae

Placenta PreviaAbruptio Placentae
BleedingPainless, bright redPainful, dark red (concealed or apparent)

Fetal statusUsually normalMay show distress or death
