💊 Antihypertensives: ABCD

A — ACE inhibitors (pril) B — Beta blockers (lol) C — Calcium channel blockers (pine) D — Diuretics
  • ACE: "pril" family — lisinopril, enalapril, captopril. Watch for dry cough and hyperkalemia.
  • Beta blockers: "lol" family — metoprolol, atenolol, propranolol. Watch for bradycardia, hypotension, mask hypoglycemia.
  • CCB: "pine" family — amlodipine, nifedipine. Watch for peripheral edema, hypotension, reflex tachycardia.
  • Diuretics: Loop (furosemide) → K⁺ wasting. Thiazide (HCTZ) → K⁺ wasting. Spironolactone (K⁺ sparing).
Clinical Pearl: ACE inhibitors end in "-pril", ARBs in "-sartan". Both can cause hyperkalemia — monitor K⁺ levels.

🩸 Blood Thinners: HEP-LOW-WAR

HEP — Heparin (IV, fast, reversible with protamine) LOW — Lovenox (enoxaparin, subQ) WAR — Warfarin (PO, slow, reversible with vitamin K)
  • HEParin: IV only. Therapeutic PTT 1.5-2.5x normal. Antidote: protamine sulfate.
  • LOWenox: SubQ. No antidote. Monitor for bleeding, avoid IM injections.
  • WARfarin: PO. Therapeutic INR 2-3. Antidote: vitamin K (takes hours) or FFP (immediate).
Clinical Pearl: Bridge therapy: Start heparin + warfarin together. Stop heparin when INR is therapeutic (2-3) for 2 consecutive days.

🧠 Anti-Seizure: PHEN-BARB-DILANTIN

PHEN — Phenytoin (Dilantin): therapeutic 10-20 mcg/mL BARB — Phenobarbital: sedating, long-acting DILANTIN — Watch for gingival hyperplasia
  • Phenytoin: Highly protein-bound. Give with food. Side effects: gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, nystagmus (toxicity).
  • Phenobarbital: Induces liver enzymes → interacts with many drugs. Causes sedation, dependency.
  • Therapeutic levels: Phenytoin 10-20 mcg/mL. > 20 = nystagmus, ataxia. > 30 = lethargy. > 40 = coma.
Clinical Pearl: Never mix phenytoin with D5W — causes precipitation. Use normal saline only. Administer slowly (max 50 mg/min).

💉 Insulin: RAPID-SHORT-INTERMEDIATE-LONG

RAPID — Lispro, Aspart, Glulisine (15 min onset) SHORT — Regular (30 min onset) INTERMEDIATE — NPH (1-2 hr onset) LONG — Glargine, Detemir (1-2 hr onset, 24 hr duration)
  • Rapid: Lispro (Humalog), Aspart (NovoLog), Glulisine (Apidra). Onset 15 min. Given at mealtime.
  • Short: Regular. Onset 30 min. Only insulin given IV.
  • Intermediate: NPH. Cloudy. Onset 1-2 hr. Peak 4-8 hr.
  • Long: Glargine (Lantus), Detemir (Levemir). Clear. NO PEAK. Given at bedtime. Do NOT mix with other insulins.
Clinical Pearl: Clear to cloudy = NPH. Roll gently (don't shake). Only Regular can be given IV. Hypoglycemia treatment: Rule of 15 — 15g fast carbs, recheck in 15 min.

🫀 Cardiac Emergency Drugs: LMNOP

L — Lidocaine (ventricular arrhythmias) M — Morphine (chest pain, MI) N — Nitroglycerin (chest pain, vasodilator) O — Oxygen (always first in MI) P — Aspirin (Plavix, antiplatelet)
  • Lidocaine: Class IB antiarrhythmic. For V-tach, V-fib. Monitor for CNS toxicity (tinnitus, seizures).
  • Morphine: Reduces preload/afterload, relieves pain, decreases O₂ demand. Monitor RR, BP.
  • Nitroglycerin: Vasodilator. SL q5min x 3 for chest pain. SBP must be > 90. Contraindicated with Viagra (hypotension).
  • Oxygen: 2-4L NC. Titrate to SpO₂ > 90%. First intervention in suspected MI.
  • Aspirin: 325 mg chewable (if no allergy). Antiplatelet, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory.
Clinical Pearl: MONA is the classic MI mnemonic (Morphine, Oxygen, Nitro, Aspirin). But don't forget: 12-lead EKG within 10 minutes is the REAL priority.

🦴 Corticosteroids: Can't Stop Growing (Side Effects)

C — Cushings syndrome A — Adrenal suppression (don't stop abruptly) T — Thin skin, easy bruising S — Sugar elevation (hyperglycemia) G — Growth suppression in children R — Risk of infection (immunosuppressed) O — Osteoporosis W — Weight gain (moon face, buffalo hump)
  • Never stop abruptly — causes adrenal crisis. Taper gradually.
  • Always give in AM — mimics natural cortisol peak (8 AM).
  • Monitor: Blood glucose, BP, weight, signs of infection, bone density.
Clinical Pearl: Steroids mask infection signs (no fever). Prednisone 5-20 mg = physiologic dose. > 20 mg = pharmacologic (side effects more likely).

🧪 Antibiotics: CELL WALL Synthesis Inhibitors

CELL — Cephalosporins, Erythromycin (macrolides), Lincosamides, Lincosamides WALL — Weakens cell wall → bacterial lysis PENICILLIN — Prototype beta-lactam
  • Penicillins: "-cillin" — amoxicillin, ampicillin. Cross-allergy with cephalosporins (10%).
  • Cephalosporins: Generations 1-5. "-cef" or "-ceph" — cefazolin, ceftriaxone, cefepime.
  • Vancomycin: MRSA coverage. RED MAN syndrome (histamine release) — give slowly, premedicate with antihistamine.
  • Monitor: Vancomycin trough (before 4th dose). Goal: 10-20 mcg/mL.
Clinical Pearl: Penicillin + probenecid = increased penicillin levels. Always ask about penicillin allergy before giving cephalosporins (10% cross-reactivity).

😰 Anxiolytics: BENZOS (CIWA Protocol)

B — Benzos (lorazepam, diazepam) for alcohol withdrawal E — Evaluate with CIWA scale (score > 8 = treat) N — Never mix with alcohol or opioids (respiratory depression) Z — Zero sudden stops (seizure risk — taper) O — Overdose antidote: Flumazenil S — Short-term use only (dependency risk)
  • CIWA-Ar: > 8 = mild withdrawal, treat with benzos. > 15 = severe, aggressive treatment.
  • Lorazepam: Preferred in liver disease (doesn't require hepatic metabolism).
  • Flumazenil: Benzo antidote. Can precipitate seizures in chronic users — use cautiously.
Clinical Pearl: Alcohol withdrawal timeline: Last drink → tremors (6-12 hr) → seizures (12-48 hr) → DTs (48-72 hr). DTs are LIFE-THREATENING.
