Psychiatric Medications

Antipsychotics, antidepressants, mood stabilizers, anxiolytics, and nursing considerations for the NCLEX.

💊 Antipsychotics (Neuroleptics)

First-Generation (Typical) Antipsychotics

Examples: Haloperidol (Haldol), chlorpromazine (Thorazine), fluphenazine

Mechanism: Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism

Uses: Schizophrenia (positive symptoms), acute agitation, Tourette syndrome

Side Effects (EPS):
  • Acute dystonia: Muscle spasms, torticollis, oculogyric crisis (treat with benztropine or diphenhydramine)
  • Akathisia: Restlessness, inability to sit still (treat with propranolol or benztropine)
  • Pseudoparkinsonism: Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia (treat with benztropine)
  • Tardive dyskinesia: Involuntary movements (irreversible) — stop antipsychotic
Other Side Effects:
  • Anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision)
  • Orthostatic hypotension, sedation
  • Hyperprolactinemia (galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction)
  • Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)
Mnemonic: "4 A's of EPS" — Acute dystonia, Akathisia, Pseudoparkinsonism, Tardive dyskinesia. All treatable with anticholinergics EXCEPT tardive dyskinesia (stop drug).

Second-Generation (Atypical) Antipsychotics

Examples: Risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole, ziprasidone, clozapine

Mechanism: Dopamine D2 + serotonin 5-HT2A antagonism (some partial agonists like aripiprazole)

Uses: Schizophrenia (positive AND negative symptoms), bipolar disorder, autism-related irritability

Side Effects:
  • Metabolic syndrome — weight gain, hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia (olanzapine > clozapine > quetiapine > risperidone > ziprasidone > aripiprazole)
  • Less EPS than typical antipsychotics
  • QT prolongation (ziprasidone, quetiapine)
  • Sedation (quetiapine, olanzapine, clozapine)
  • Orthostatic hypotension
Clozapine Special Considerations:
  • Only antipsychotic for treatment-resistant schizophrenia
  • Agranulocytosis — weekly CBC for 6 months, then every 2 weeks
  • Seizures (dose-related)
  • Myocarditis
  • Constipation (can be severe)
  • Requires REMS program registration

Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)

Medical emergency — rare but life-threatening reaction to antipsychotics

Manifestations (FEVER):
  • Fever (>38°C/100.4°F)
  • Elevated CK (muscle breakdown)
  • Vital signs unstable (tachycardia, BP changes)
  • Encephalopathy (altered mental status)
  • Rigidity (lead-pipe muscle rigidity)
Management:
  1. STOP antipsychotic immediately
  2. Supportive care — cooling, hydration, monitor CK, renal function
  3. Dantrolene (muscle relaxant) for rigidity and hyperthermia
  4. Bromocriptine (dopamine agonist)
NCLEX Trap: NMS vs Serotonin Syndrome — NMS has slow onset (days), bradykinesia, lead-pipe rigidity, normal pupils. Serotonin syndrome has rapid onset (hours), hyperreflexia, clonus, dilated pupils, agitation.

😔 Antidepressants

Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs)

Examples: Sertraline, fluoxetine, escitalopram, paroxetine, citalopram

Mechanism: Block serotonin reuptake → increased synaptic serotonin

Uses: Depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD, PMDD

Side Effects:
  • GI upset (nausea, diarrhea) — usually transient
  • Sexual dysfunction (decreased libido, delayed ejaculation, anorgasmia)
  • Insomnia or somnolence
  • Headache, agitation (early)
  • Hyponatremia (SIADH) — especially elderly
  • Weight changes
NCLEX Trap: Black box warning for increased suicidal ideation in children, adolescents, and young adults (ages 18-24) during first few weeks. Monitor closely. Do NOT stop abruptly — taper to avoid discontinuation syndrome (dizziness, nausea, paresthesias, irritability).
Mnemonic: "FLUoxetine makes you FLUsh" — Sexual dysfunction is common with SSRIs

Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs)

Examples: Venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine

Mechanism: Block serotonin AND norepinephrine reuptake

Uses: Depression, generalized anxiety, fibromyalgia (duloxetine), neuropathic pain (duloxetine)

Side Effects:
  • Similar to SSRIs + increased BP and HR (norepinephrine effect)
  • Diaphoresis
  • Discontinuation syndrome (worse than SSRIs, especially venlafaxine)

Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)

Examples: Amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine

Mechanism: Block serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake + anticholinergic, antihistamine, alpha-1 blockade

Uses: Depression (2nd line), neuropathic pain, migraine prophylaxis

Side Effects:
  • Anticholinergic (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision, tachycardia)
  • Orthostatic hypotension
  • Sedation
  • Weight gain
  • QT prolongation, arrhythmias
NCLEX Trap: TCA overdose is LIFE-THREATENING. Triad: anticholinergic effects + seizures + cardiotoxicity (QRS widening, arrhythmias). Treat with sodium bicarbonate (for QRS widening), activated charcoal, supportive care.

Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)

Examples: Phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline (transdermal)

Mechanism: Inhibit MAO-A and/or MAO-B → prevent breakdown of serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine

Uses: Atypical depression, treatment-resistant depression, Parkinson's (selegiline)

Side Effects:
  • Orthostatic hypotension
  • Weight gain
  • Insomnia, agitation
  • Sexual dysfunction
CRITICAL: Tyramine Restrictions

Avoid tyramine-rich foods (aged cheese, cured meats, fermented foods, red wine, soy sauce, yeast extracts) — can cause hypertensive crisis.

Symptoms: Severe headache, hypertensive crisis, possible stroke

Also avoid meperidine, dextromethorphan, pseudoephedrine, SSRIs, SNRIs (serotonin syndrome risk)

Mnemonic: "Cheese and wine will make you whine" — Aged cheese and wine are high in tyramine — avoid with MAOIs

🎢 Mood Stabilizers

Lithium

Uses: Bipolar disorder (mania prevention, maintenance), augmentation for depression

Mechanism: Unclear — affects ion transport, neurotransmitter release, second messenger systems

Therapeutic Level: 0.6-1.2 mEq/L (acute mania: 0.8-1.2; maintenance: 0.6-1.0)
Side Effects:
  • Tremor (fine hand tremor)
  • Polyuria, polydipsia (nephrogenic DI)
  • Weight gain
  • Hypothyroidism (check TSH)
  • Acne, psoriasis exacerbation
  • Cognitive dulling
Lithium Toxicity (Level >1.5 mEq/L):
  • Early: GI upset, coarse tremor, ataxia, confusion
  • Severe (>2.0): Seizures, coma, arrhythmias, hypotension, death

Management: Hold lithium, hydrate aggressively, monitor levels q4h, hemodialysis if severe. NEVER give with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, thiazide diuretics, NSAIDs (reduce lithium excretion).

Mnemonic: "Lithium makes you lithium-thy" — Tremor, thirst (DI), thyroid issues, weight gain

Anticonvulsants as Mood Stabilizers

Valproic acid/divalproex: Bipolar mania, mixed episodes, maintenance

  • Side effects: Weight gain, tremor, hair loss, hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, teratogenic (neural tube defects)
  • Monitor LFTs, platelets, ammonia levels

Carbamazepine: Bipolar disorder, seizure disorder

  • Side effects: Hyponatremia (SIADH), rash (SJS/TEN risk), hepatotoxicity, bone marrow suppression
  • Autoinducer — doses increase over first month
  • Many drug interactions (CYP450 inducer)

Lamotrigine: Bipolar depression maintenance

  • Side effects: Rash (SJS/TEN risk — slow titration), dizziness, headache
  • Slow titration mandatory to prevent rash

😰 Anxiolytics & Sedative-Hypnotics

Benzodiazepines (e.g., lorazepam, alprazolam, diazepam, clonazepam)

Mechanism: Enhance GABA-A receptor activity → CNS depression

Uses: Anxiety, panic disorder, insomnia, alcohol withdrawal, seizures (lorazepam, diazepam), muscle spasm

Side Effects:
  • Sedation, drowsiness
  • Confusion (especially elderly)
  • Ataxia
  • Respiratory depression (especially with opioids, alcohol)
  • Dependence and tolerance
NCLEX Trap: NEVER combine benzodiazepines with alcohol or opioids — respiratory depression and death risk. Flumazenil reverses benzo overdose but can precipitate seizures in dependent patients. Taper gradually — abrupt withdrawal can cause seizures.
Alcohol Withdrawal Protocol: CIWA-Ar assessment q4h. Lorazepam PRN based on score. Thiamine before glucose (Wernicke's prevention). Monitor for DTs (delirium tremens) — fever, tachycardia, hypertension, hallucinations, seizures.
