## Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| **Eye Opening** | |
| Spontaneous | 4 |
| To speech | 3 |
| To pain | 2 |
| None | 1 |
| **Verbal Response** | |
| Oriented | 5 |
| Confused | 4 |
| Inappropriate words | 3 |
| Incomprehensible sounds | 2 |
| None | 1 |
| **Motor Response** | |
| Obeys commands | 6 |
| Localizes pain | 5 |
| Withdraws from pain | 4 |
| Flexion (decorticate) | 3 |
| Extension (decerebrate) | 2 |
| None | 1 |

**Interpretation:** 13–15 = Mild, 9–12 = Moderate, 3–8 = Severe

**NCLEX Trap:** A change in GCS of 2+ points is significant — even if still "normal." Report immediately.

## Stroke Assessment: BE FAST

| Letter | Sign | What to Look For |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| **B** | Balance | Sudden loss of balance/coordination |
| **E** | Eyes | Sudden vision loss, double vision |
| **F** | Face | Facial droop (ask to smile) |
| **A** | Arm | Arm weakness/numbness (drift test) |
| **S** | Speech | Slurred speech, difficulty finding words |
| **T** | Time | Time is brain — call 911, note last known well |

**Door-to-Needle Time:** ≤ 60 minutes for tPA eligibility (4.5 hour window)

## Seizure First Aid

**DO:**
- Note start time
- Protect from injury (clear area, pad head)
- Turn on side after seizure (recovery position)
- Stay with patient until fully awake
- Document: type, duration, postictal state

**DO NOT:**
- Put anything in mouth
- Restrain the patient
- Give food/water until fully awake
- Leave alone

**Status Epilepticus:** Seizure > 5 minutes OR recurrent without recovery → EMERGENCY
- First-line: Lorazepam 4 mg IV (or diazepam 10 mg PR)
- Second-line: Fosphenytoin or levetiracetam
- Protect airway, O2, prepare for intubation

## Increased ICP: Early vs Late Signs

### Early Signs
- **Headache** (worse in morning, coughing/straining)
- **Nausea/vomiting** (projectile, without nausea)
- **Altered LOC** (earliest sign!)
- **Papilledema** (swollen optic disc)
- **Cushing's Triad** (late!): ↑ BP, ↓ HR, irregular respirations

### Emergency Interventions
1. **HOB 30–45°** (promotes venous drainage)
2. **Avoid neck flexion/hip flexion** (impedes drainage)
3. **Maintain PaCO2 35–45 mmHg** (hyperventilate only briefly if herniating)
4. **Mannitol 0.25–1 g/kg IV** (osmotic diuretic)
5. **Hypertonic saline** (3% NaCl)
6. **Prepare for craniotomy/EVD**

**NCLEX Trap:** Do NOT hyperventilate long-term — causes cerebral vasoconstriction and ischemia. Only for acute herniation.

## Parkinson's vs Alzheimer's vs ALS

| Feature | Parkinson's | Alzheimer's | ALS |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| **Onset** | 50–60s | Gradual, >65 | 40–70 |
| **Key Feature** | TRAP symptoms | Memory loss | Mixed UMN + LMN |
| **Cognition** | Intact until late | Early impairment | Intact |
| **Motor** | Bradykinesia, rigidity, tremor, postural instability | Normal until late | Progressive weakness |
| **Speech** | Hypophonia, monotone | Word-finding difficulty | Dysarthria |
| **Swallowing** | Late | Late | Early |
| **Life Expectancy** | 10–20 years | 8–10 years | 3–5 years |

## Multiple Sclerosis (MS) Nursing Priorities

**Common Symptoms:**
- Fatigue (most common!)
- Optic neuritis (painful vision loss)
- Numbness/tingling
- Spasticity
- Bladder/bowel dysfunction
- Cognitive changes

**Relapse Management:**
- High-dose corticosteroids (IV methylprednisolone 500–1000 mg/day × 3–5 days)
- Plasma exchange if refractory

**Disease-Modifying Therapies:**
- Interferon beta (flu-like symptoms)
- Glatiramer acetate
- Natalizumab (PML risk — monitor JC virus)
- Ocrelizumab

**NCLEX Priority:** Avoid heat (Uhthoff's phenomenon). Teach energy conservation. Fall risk assessment.

## Myasthenia Gravis (MG)

**Pathophysiology:** Autoantibodies against acetylcholine receptors → muscle weakness

**Key Feature:** **Fatigable weakness** — worse with repeated use, better with rest

**Crisis Warning Signs:**
- Dysphagia/dysarthria
- Respiratory weakness (tachypnea, shallow breathing)
- Inability to lift head
- **Myasthenic crisis:** Respiratory failure → intubation

**Treatment:**
- Pyridostigmine (Mestinon) — cholinesterase inhibitor
- Prednisone, IVIG, plasmapheresis
- Thymectomy if thymoma

**Cholinergic Crisis vs Myasthenic Crisis:**

| Feature | Cholinergic Crisis | Myasthenic Crisis |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Cause | Too much pyridostigmine | Too little pyridostigmine |
| Salivation | Excessive | Normal |
| Sweating | Excessive | Normal |
| Pupils | Constricted (miotic) | Normal |
| Cramping/Diarrhea | Present | Absent |
| Treatment | Hold pyridostigmine, atropine | More pyridostigmine, IVIG/plasmapheresis |
