Emergency Drugs Every Nurse Must Know

ACLS medications, overdose antidotes, anaphylaxis treatment, and critical care drips — the drugs that save lives

🚨 Critical Note

This reference is for educational purposes. Always follow your facility's protocols, ACLS guidelines, and provider orders. Drug dosages and protocols change — verify current recommendations.

🫀 ACLS Cardiac Arrest Drugs

Drug Dose When to Give Key Notes
Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO q3-5 min ALL cardiac arrests Vasopressor. Increases coronary perfusion. No max dose in arrest.
Amiodarone 300 mg IV → 150 mg IV Refractory VF/pulseless VT After 3rd shock. Can cause hypotension, bradycardia.
Lidocaine 1-1.5 mg/kg IV Alternative to amiodarone Class IB antiarrhythmic. Reduce dose in liver disease, elderly.
Atropine 1 mg IV q3-5 min (max 3 mg) Symptomatic bradycardia NOT for cardiac arrest (PEA/asystole). Only for bradycardia with symptoms.
Adenosine 6 mg → 12 mg IV rapid push Stable SVT Fast push + flush. Causes transient asystole. Contraindicated in WPW, AFib.

💡 ACLS Pearl

High-Quality CPR is more important than drugs. Compress 2-2.4 inches deep, 100-120/min, full recoil, minimize interruptions. Epinephrine every 3-5 minutes, but do NOT delay compressions to give meds.

💉 Overdose Antidotes

Poison/OD Antidote Dose Key Notes
Opioids Naloxone (Narcan) 0.4-2 mg IV/IM/IN, repeat q2-3 min Can precipitate withdrawal. Short half-life — monitor for re-sedation.
Benzodiazepines Flumazenil 0.2 mg IV, repeat to 1 mg max Caution with chronic use — can precipitate seizures. NOT first-line.
Beta blockers Glucagon 3-10 mg IV, then infusion Hyperglycemia risk. Give with dextrose if hypoglycemic.
Calcium channel blockers Calcium chloride/gluconate + glucagon + insulin 1 g CaCl₂ IV High-dose insulin therapy + lipid emulsion for severe cases.
Acetaminophen N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) 140 mg/kg loading → 70 mg/kg q4h x 17 Most effective within 8 hours. Protects liver. Smells like rotten eggs.
Digoxin Digoxin immune Fab (Digibind) Based on ingested dose or serum level Expensive. For life-threatening toxicity: K⁺ > 5, arrhythmias.
Heparin Protamine sulfate 1 mg per 100 units heparin Can cause hypotension, bradycardia. Give slowly over 10 min.
Warfarin Vitamin K + FFP/PCC 10 mg Vitamin K IV/PO Vitamin K takes hours. FFP/PCC for immediate reversal.
Tricyclic antidepressants Sodium bicarbonate 1-2 mEq/kg IV For QRS > 100 ms, ventricular arrhythmias, hypotension. Alkalinizes serum.
Organophosphates Atropine + Pralidoxime (2-PAM) Atropine 2-4 mg IV, double q3-5 min Atropine until secretions dry (titrated to effect). 2-PAM reactivates acetylcholinesterase.

😰 Anaphylaxis Protocol

First-Line: Epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg IM (1:1000) in lateral thigh

Repeat q5-15 min if needed. IM absorption faster than SC. IV only for refractory cases in monitored setting.

Drug Dose Purpose
Epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg IM (1:1000) LIFE-SAVING. Alpha-1: vasoconstriction. Beta-1: cardiac support. Beta-2: bronchodilation.
Diphenhydramine 25-50 mg IV/IM H1 blocker. Blocks histamine effects (itching, hives, vasodilation).
Famotidine (Pepcid) 20 mg IV H2 blocker. Blocks gastric acid, synergistic with H1 blocker.
Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV Steroid. Prevents biphasic reaction. Takes 4-6 hours to work — NOT for immediate effect.
Albuterol 2.5 mg nebulizer Bronchodilator for wheezing. Adjunct, NOT replacement for epinephrine.

💡 Anaphylaxis Pearl

Epinephrine is the ONLY drug that treats ALL aspects of anaphylaxis: airway edema (alpha-1), hypotension (alpha-1), bronchospasm (beta-2), cardiac support (beta-1). Antihistamines and steroids are adjuncts — they do NOT save lives in acute anaphylaxis.

💧 Critical Care Drips

Drug Typical Dose Range Indication Monitor
Norepinephrine (Levophed) 0.01-3 mcg/kg/min Septic shock (first-line vasopressor) MAP > 65, urine output, lactate
Epinephrine 0.1-1 mcg/kg/min Cardiac arrest, anaphylaxis, severe shock HR, rhythm, glucose (hyperglycemia)
Dopamine 2-20 mcg/kg/min Shock (renal dose 2-5, cardiac 5-10, pressor > 10) Arrhythmias, especially at high doses
Dobutamine 2-20 mcg/kg/min Cardiogenic shock, heart failure (inotrope) BP, HR (can cause tachycardia), arrhythmias
Nitroglycerin 5-200 mcg/min ACS, pulmonary edema (afterload reduction) BP (SBP > 90), headache, hypotension
Nitroprusside 0.3-10 mcg/kg/min Hypertensive emergency BP q1-2 min, cyanide toxicity with prolonged use
Milrinone 0.125-0.75 mcg/kg/min Heart failure (inodilator) BP (vasodilation), arrhythmias
Phenylephrine 0.5-9 mcg/kg/min Hypotension (pure alpha, no cardiac effect) BP, reflex bradycardia

💡 Vasopressor Pearl

Norepinephrine is first-line for septic shock per Surviving Sepsis guidelines. Add vasopressin (0.03 units/min) for refractory shock. Add epinephrine as third-line. Goal MAP ≥ 65 mmHg (or higher if chronic hypertension).

🫁 Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) Meds

Drug Dose Purpose Caution
Etomidate 0.3 mg/kg IV Induction (hemodynamically neutral) Adrenal suppression (single dose OK)
Ketamine 1-2 mg/kg IV Induction (bronchodilator, preserves BP) Emergence reactions, increased ICP
Propofol 1.5-2.5 mg/kg IV Induction (short-acting) Hypotension, respiratory depression, triglycerides
Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg IV Paralysis (longer-acting) Can be reversed with sugammadex
Succinylcholine 1.5 mg/kg IV Paralysis (rapid, short) Hyperkalemia risk (burns, paralysis, crush injury). Avoid in these patients.
Midazolam 0.3 mg/kg IV Sedation/amnesia Respiratory depression, hypotension
Fentanyl 2-5 mcg/kg IV Analgesia Chest wall rigidity with rapid push
