The ABCDE Method for NCLEX Prioritization

When everything is urgent, how do you choose? The ABCDE framework eliminates guesswork.

Picture this: You're the only nurse on the floor. Four patients need you. Everyone's situation sounds emergent. Who do you see first?

This is the exact scenario the NGN NCLEX tests. And the ABCDE method is the framework that eliminates the guesswork.

What is ABCDE?

ABCDE is a systematic prioritization framework used in emergency and critical care. The NCLEX expects you to apply it — even if the question doesn't explicitly say "prioritize."

ABCDE vs. Maslow: When to Use Which

Students often confuse ABCDE with Maslow's hierarchy. Here's the simple rule:

Use ABCDE when:

  • The patient is actively deteriorating
  • Multiple patients need prioritization
  • The question asks "Who do you see FIRST?"
  • Vital signs are abnormal or trending wrong

Use Maslow when:

  • The patient is stable but has multiple needs
  • The question asks "What is the PRIORITY intervention?"
  • Comparing psychosocial vs. physiological needs

The ABCDE Decision Tree

When you get a prioritization question, run through this mentally:

  1. Is anyone's airway compromised? → See them FIRST. Airway always wins.
  2. Is anyone not breathing adequately? → See them SECOND.
  3. Is anyone in circulatory collapse? (BP crashing, HR irregular, unresponsive) → See them THIRD.
  4. Is anyone's mental status changing? (Confused, unresponsive, seizure) → See them FOURTH.
  5. Is anyone exposed to harm? (Bleeding, hypothermia, burns) → See them FIFTH.

NCLEX Practice Scenarios

Scenario 1: Who Do You See FIRST?

You have 4 patients. Which do you assess first?

  • Room 1: Post-op appendectomy, stable, asking for pain medication
  • Room 2: COPD exacerbation, O2 sat 88% on 2L nasal cannula, RR 28
  • Room 3: New admission, chest pain, BP 142/88, HR 96
  • Room 4: Diabetic, blood glucose 68, alert and oriented, eating lunch
Answer: Room 2 FIRST

ABCDE Analysis:
- Room 1: Pain = comfort need (Maslow), not emergent
- Room 2: Breathing compromised (O2 88%, RR 28) = B in ABCDE = PRIORITY
- Room 3: Chest pain concerning but stable vitals = assess after Room 2
- Room 4: Hypoglycemia but alert, eating = can wait 5 minutes

Room 2's breathing is actively compromised. That's a B problem. B comes before C (circulation) and D (disability).

Scenario 2: Multiple Emergencies

Code blue is called. Two patients need you simultaneously:

  • Patient A: Choking, cannot speak, clutching throat
  • Patient B: HR 160, BP 70/40, diaphoretic, confused
Answer: Patient A FIRST

ABCDE Analysis:
- Patient A: Airway obstruction (A in ABCDE) = IMMEDIATE DEATH RISK
- Patient B: Circulatory collapse (C in ABCDE) = CRITICAL but has minutes

Airway obstruction kills in 4-6 minutes. Circulatory collapse gives you slightly more time. A always beats C.

Scenario 3: The Trap Question

A nurse enters a room and finds:

  • Patient unresponsive
  • No pulse
  • Not breathing

What is the FIRST action?

Answer: Start CPR (CAB, not ABC)

The 2020 AHA Update: For cardiac arrest, the sequence is CAB — Compressions, Airway, Breathing.

Why? Because in cardiac arrest, the blood already has oxygen. The priority is circulating it. Start compressions immediately, then open airway and give breaths.

NCLEX Trap: Students who memorized "ABC" from old textbooks pick "open airway" first. The NGN tests current evidence-based practice.

Why This Matters for the NGN

The Next Generation NCLEX doesn't just ask "Who do you see first?" It embeds prioritization into case studies:

  • "A nurse receives report on 4 patients. Which patient should be assessed FIRST?" (Recognize + Analyze + Prioritize)

The ABCDE framework is the thinking tool that gets you through all of these — not memorization, not intuition, but systematic clinical judgment.
