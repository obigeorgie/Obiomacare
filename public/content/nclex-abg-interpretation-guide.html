1. ABG Basics & Normal Values

You must memorize these values. The NCLEX expects you to know them without prompts.

ParameterNormal RangeWhat It Tells You
pH7.35 – 7.45Overall acidity/alkalinity. <7.35 = acidosis, >7.45 = alkalosis
PaCO₂35 – 45 mmHgRespiratory component. CO₂ is acidic. High = retaining acid (acidosis), Low = blowing off acid (alkalosis)
HCO₃⁻22 – 26 mEq/LMetabolic component. Bicarbonate is basic. Low = losing base (acidosis), High = gaining base (alkalosis)
PaO₂80 – 100 mmHgOxygenation status on room air
SaO₂95 – 100%Oxygen saturation
Base Excess-2 to +2 mEq/LMetabolic buffer reserve
🧠 MNEMONIC: "pH 7.4, CO₂ 40, HCO₃ 24"
Think of it as a phone number: 7.4 - 40 - 24. If you remember this trio, you can figure out everything else.

2. The ROME Method

The ROME method is the fastest way to determine if a disorder is respiratory or metabolic.

ROMEMeaningHow It Works
RespiratoryOppositepH and PaCO₂ move in opposite directions
MetabolicEqualpH and HCO₃⁻ move in the same direction
💡 Quick Examples:
• pH 7.30 (↓) + PaCO₂ 50 (↑) = Opposite = Respiratory acidosis
• pH 7.50 (↑) + HCO₃⁻ 32 (↑) = Equal = Metabolic alkalosis
• pH 7.25 (↓) + HCO₃⁻ 18 (↓) = Equal = Metabolic acidosis

3. The 4-Step Interpretation Method

Use this systematic approach on every ABG question:

Step 1: Look at the pH

  • < 7.35 = Acidosis
  • 7.35 – 7.45 = Normal (or fully compensated)
  • > 7.45 = Alkalosis

Step 2: Determine Respiratory Component (PaCO₂)

  • > 45 = Retaining CO₂ (acid) → Respiratory acidosis
  • < 35 = Blowing off CO₂ (acid) → Respiratory alkalosis
  • 35-45 = Normal respiratory component

Step 3: Determine Metabolic Component (HCO₃⁻)

  • < 22 = Losing bicarbonate (base) → Metabolic acidosis
  • > 26 = Retaining bicarbonate (base) → Metabolic alkalosis
  • 22-26 = Normal metabolic component

5. Understanding Compensation

💡 Key Rules:
• Lungs compensate for metabolic problems by changing PaCO₂ (fast — minutes to hours)
• Kidneys compensate for respiratory problems by changing HCO₃⁻ (slow — days)
• The body never overcompensates. If pH is normal but both values are abnormal, it's fully compensated.
• If pH is still abnormal, compensation is only partial.

6. 10 Practice Cases

📝 Case 1: Post-Op Patient

ABG: pH 7.32 | PaCO₂ 48 | HCO₃⁻ 24 | PaO₂ 85

Question: What is the acid-base disorder?

Answer: Uncompensated Respiratory Acidosis

Rationale: pH is low (7.32 = acidosis). PaCO₂ is high (48 = respiratory). HCO₃⁻ is normal (24). Using ROME: pH ↓ and PaCO₂ ↑ = opposite directions = respiratory. Since HCO₃⁻ is normal, there is no metabolic compensation yet. This is common in post-op patients with shallow breathing from anesthesia/pain.

Nursing Action: Encourage deep breathing, incentive spirometry, pain management, repositioning.

📝 Case 2: DKA Patient

ABG: pH 7.25 | PaCO₂ 28 | HCO₃⁻ 14 | PaO₂ 92

Question: Interpret this ABG and identify the priority intervention.

Answer: Metabolic Acidosis with Partial Respiratory Compensation

Rationale: pH 7.25 = acidosis. HCO₃⁻ 14 = low = metabolic acidosis. PaCO₂ 28 = low. The lungs are compensating by blowing off CO₂ (Kussmaul respirations). Using ROME: pH ↓ and HCO₃⁻ ↓ = same direction = metabolic.

Priority Intervention: IV insulin and IV fluids. The underlying cause is DKA — must treat the hyperglycemia and dehydration.

📝 Case 3: COPD Exacerbation

ABG: pH 7.36 | PaCO₂ 58 | HCO₃⁻ 33 | PaO₂ 62

Question: What type of compensation is present?

Answer: Fully Compensated Respiratory Acidosis

Rationale: pH 7.36 is within normal range (7.35-7.45). PaCO₂ 58 is high (respiratory acidosis). HCO₃⁻ 33 is high (metabolic compensation). The kidneys have increased bicarbonate reabsorption to normalize the pH. This is common in chronic COPD patients — they live with elevated CO₂ and compensated bicarbonate.

Nursing Action: Low-flow O₂ (2L NC), bronchodilators, steroids, monitor for respiratory fatigue.

📝 Case 4: Panic Attack

ABG: pH 7.50 | PaCO₂ 28 | HCO₃⁻ 24 | PaO₂ 100

Question: What is the disorder and priority nursing action?

Answer: Uncompensated Respiratory Alkalosis

Rationale: pH 7.50 = alkalosis. PaCO₂ 28 = low = respiratory (blowing off CO₂). HCO₃⁻ 24 = normal. ROME: pH ↑ and PaCO₂ ↓ = opposite = respiratory.

Priority Action: Have patient breathe into a paper bag or cupped hands (rebreathing CO₂). Address anxiety. Reassure. Do NOT give sedatives without ruling out medical causes first.

📝 Case 5: Vomiting Patient

ABG: pH 7.48 | PaCO₂ 42 | HCO₃⁻ 30 | PaO₂ 88

Question: Identify the acid-base disorder.

Answer: Uncompensated Metabolic Alkalosis

Rationale: pH 7.48 = alkalosis. HCO₃⁻ 30 = high = metabolic. PaCO₂ 42 = normal (slightly high but not compensating yet). ROME: pH ↑ and HCO₃⁻ ↑ = same direction = metabolic. Cause: vomiting leads to loss of HCl (acid), leading to alkalosis.

Nursing Action: Monitor electrolytes (especially K⁺ — alkalosis causes hypokalemia), IV fluids with KCl, antiemetics.

7. ABG in NGN Case Studies

The Next Generation NCLEX presents ABG values within unfolding case studies. You'll see:

  • ABG values alongside vital signs and MAR data
  • Multiple patients with different disorders to prioritize
  • Bow-tie items requiring you to identify the correct acid-base disorder AND the correct intervention
💡 NGN Strategy: When you see an ABG in a case study:
1. Quickly classify using ROME (3 seconds)
2. Ask: "Is this acute or chronic?" (chronic COPD = compensated)
3. Look at the patient condition — are they symptomatic?
4. Match the disorder to the correct intervention
