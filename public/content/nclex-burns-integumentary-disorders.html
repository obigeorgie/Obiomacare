NCLEX Burns & Integumentary Disorders

Burn classification, fluid resuscitation, Parkland formula, skin disorders, and wound healing for NCLEX success.

1. Burn Classification

Depth of Burn

DepthAppearanceSensationHealing
Superficial (1st°)Erythema, dry, no blistersPainful3-7 days, no scarring
Partial-thickness (2nd°)Blisters, moist, weeping, red/pinkVery painful2-3 weeks, possible scarring
Full-thickness (3rd°)White, brown, charred, leathery, dryPAINLESS (nerve destruction)Requires grafting, significant scarring
Deep full-thickness (4th°)Into muscle, tendon, bonePainlessExtensive reconstruction or amputation
NCLEX Trap: Full-thickness burns are PAINLESS because nerve endings are destroyed. If an NCLEX question describes a painless, leathery, charred burn — it's full-thickness. Pain does NOT mean the burn is less severe in this case.

2. Rule of Nines

Adult BSA Estimation

Body PartAdultChild
Head and neck9%18%
Each arm9%9%
Anterior trunk18%18%
Posterior trunk18%18%
Each leg18%14%
Perineum1%1%
Remember: 9 + 9 + 18 + 18 + 18 + 18 + 9 + 9 + 1 = 100%
Head = 9% | Each arm = 9% | Front = 18% | Back = 18% | Each leg = 18% | Perineum = 1%
NCLEX Trap: For CHILDREN, the head is LARGER proportionally (18% vs 9%) and legs are SMALLER (14% vs 18%). Use the Lund-Browder chart for more accurate pediatric BSA. Palmar surface of patient's hand (including fingers) = ~1% BSA — useful for patchy burns.

3. Fluid Resuscitation — Parkland Formula

Parkland Formula (First 24 Hours)

4 mL × kg × %TBSA (2nd° and 3rd° only)
Half in first 8 hours (from time of injury, not arrival)
Half in next 16 hours
Example: 70 kg patient with 40% TBSA
• Total fluids: 4 × 70 × 40 = 11,200 mL
• First 8 hours: 5,600 mL (700 mL/hr)
• Next 16 hours: 5,600 mL (350 mL/hr)
Monitoring During Resuscitation:
• Urine output: 0.5-1 mL/kg/hr (adults), 1-2 mL/kg/hr (children)
• MAP ≥65 mmHg
• Heart rate <120 bpm
• Adequate peripheral perfusion (warm extremities, good capillary refill)
• Mental status improvement
NCLEX Trap: The clock starts at TIME OF INJURY, not time of arrival at hospital. If a patient arrives 2 hours after injury, give the first half over the remaining 6 hours — NOT 8 hours. Also, 1st° burns are NOT included in %TBSA calculation for Parkland.

4. Burn Phases and Nursing Care

Emergent Phase (First 24-72 Hours)

Priorities (ABCs):
• Airway: Assess for inhalation injury (facial burns, singed nasal hairs, soot in mouth, hoarseness, stridor). Intubate early if suspected!
• Breathing: Carbon monoxide poisoning (cherry-red skin is LATE sign; carboxyhemoglobin levels). 100% O₂ until CO levels normalize.
• Circulation: Fluid resuscitation (Parkland), two large-bore IVs
• Remove clothing and jewelry (swelling)
• Cover burns with clean dry sheet (NOT adhesive)
• Keep patient warm (hypothermia risk from heat loss through burns)
• Tetanus prophylaxis
• NG tube if >20% TBSA (ileus risk)

Acute Phase (Day 3 to Wound Closure)

Nursing Priorities:
• Wound care (hydrotherapy, debridement, dressing changes)
• Infection prevention (topical antimicrobials: silver sulfadiazine, mafenide)
• Pain management (around-the-clock dosing, pre-medicate before dressing changes)
• Nutrition: HIGH calorie, HIGH protein (2x basal needs). Enteral feeding preferred.
• Positioning and splinting to prevent contractures
• Psychological support
NCLEX Trap: Silver sulfadiazine (Silvadene) is contraindicated in patients with sulfa allergy AND in newborns/infants. Mafenide acetate (Sulfamylon) is painful on application and can cause metabolic acidosis. Know your topical antimicrobial contraindications!

Rehabilitation Phase (Wound Closure to Return to Function)

Nursing Priorities:
• Pressure garments (reduce hypertrophic scarring)
• ROM exercises
• Scar management (silicone sheets, massage)
• Psychological support (PTSD, body image)
• Vocational rehabilitation

5. Inhalation Injury

Thermal vs Chemical Inhalation

Warning Signs: Facial burns, singed nasal hairs, soot in oropharynx, hoarseness, stridor, carbonaceous sputum, confusion, history of fire in enclosed space
Nursing Priorities:
• Early intubation (edema can rapidly obstruct airway)
• 100% humidified O₂
• Arterial blood gas
• Carboxyhemoglobin level (normal <5%; >15% significant)
• Bronchoscopy may be needed
• Watch for ARDS
NCLEX Trap: Carbon monoxide has 200x affinity for hemoglobin vs O₂. Pulse oximetry reads FALSELY NORMAL (COHb reads as oxyhemoglobin). Trust ABG and CO levels, NOT SpO₂, in smoke inhalation.

Clinical Judgment Case Study

Burns Fluid Resuscitation

Case: Scald Burns in Child

A 3-year-old pulled a pot of boiling water onto himself. He has partial-thickness burns covering his entire anterior trunk and both anterior legs. Weight: 14 kg. He is crying but responsive. HR 140, BP 88/52, RR 32.

Using the Parkland formula, what is the TOTAL fluid requirement for the first 24 hours?

  1. 560 mL
  2. 1,120 mL
  3. 2,240 mL
  4. 4,480 mL
Answer: 3 — 2,240 mL.

TBSA: anterior trunk (9%) + both anterior legs (9% each) = 27% TBSA. Parkland formula: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA = 4 × 14 × 40 = 2,240 mL total in 24h. (Note: child's palm = 1%, rule of nines: anterior trunk = 18%, each leg = 14%. But for anterior only: trunk 9%, each leg 7% = 23%. Use Lund-Browder for accuracy in peds.) Give half in first 8 hours from time of injury, half in next 16 hours.
