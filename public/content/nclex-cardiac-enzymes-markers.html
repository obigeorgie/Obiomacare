Troponin I & T — The Gold Standard

Why It's #1

Troponin is a cardiac-specific regulatory protein found only in heart muscle. When cardiac cells die, troponin leaks into the bloodstream.

Key Facts

  • Most sensitive AND specific marker for myocardial injury
  • Rises in 3-6 hours after MI onset
  • Stays elevated 7-10 days (good for late presenters)
  • Even minor elevations indicate cardiac damage

Mnemonic: TROPONIN = TRuly OPtimized for cardiac INjury

Non-MI Causes of Elevated Troponin

  • Myocarditis/pericarditis
  • Heart failure exacerbation
  • Pulmonary embolism (right heart strain)
  • Severe sepsis/shock
  • Cardiac contusion
  • Cardioversion
  • Strenuous exercise (extreme cases)
  • Chronic kidney disease (troponin T more than I)

Nursing implication: Troponin elevated ≠ MI. Interpret in clinical context!

CK-MB (Creatine Kinase, MB Isoenzyme)

The "Rising and Falling" Pattern

CK-MB is found mainly in cardiac muscle but also in skeletal muscle. Less specific than troponin but useful for detecting reinfarction.

Why CK-MB Still Matters

  • Rises 4-6 hours, peaks 12-24 hours
  • Clears in 48-72 hours (vs 7-10 days for troponin)
  • If CK-MB rises again after falling → reinfarction!
  • CK-MB index >2.5% suggests cardiac vs skeletal source

Important: After MI, troponin stays up for days. If a patient has recurrent chest pain 3 days later, a rising CK-MB indicates new infarction — troponin won't help because it's still elevated from the first event.

BNP / NT-proBNP — Heart Failure Marker

Not for MI — For Heart Failure!

BNP (B-type Natriuretic Peptide) is released by ventricular myocardium when it's stretched — i.e., in volume overload and heart failure.

BNP Level Interpretation
<100 pg/mL HF unlikely
100-400 pg/mL HF possible (consider other causes)
>400 pg/mL HF likely

NT-proBNP (More Sensitive)

Age NT-proBNP Interpretation
<75 years >450 pg/mL HF likely
>75 years >900 pg/mL HF likely
Any age >1800 pg/mL Severe HF

Clinical Pearls

  • Obesity: Lower BNP (fat tissue breaks it down faster)
  • CKD: Higher BNP (reduced clearance)
  • AFib: Slightly elevated
  • Pulmonary embolism: Can elevate (right heart strain)

Myoglobin — The Early Riser

First to Rise, First to Fall

  • Rises in 1-3 hours — earliest marker
  • Peaks at 6-12 hours
  • Cleared by kidneys in 24-36 hours
  • NOT cardiac specific (found in all muscle)

Think of myoglobin as the "smoke alarm" — it goes off first but doesn't tell you what room is on fire. A negative myoglobin at 3-6 hours rules OUT MI with high confidence.

Why It's Less Used Now

High-sensitivity troponin assays can detect MI almost as early with much better specificity. Myoglobin is largely historical but may appear on NCLEX.

Timeline After MI

0-1 hour

Patient has chest pain. All markers normal.

1-3 hours

Myoglobin starts rising. Troponin still normal.

3-6 hours

Troponin starts rising. This is when it becomes detectable.

4-6 hours

CK-MB starts rising.

6-12 hours

All three markers elevated. Myoglobin peaks.

12-24 hours

Troponin and CK-MB peak. Best time to draw for confirmation.

24-36 hours

Myoglobin normalizes.

48-72 hours

CK-MB normalizes. If it rises again → reinfarction!

7-10 days

Troponin normalizes. Stays elevated longest.

NCLEX Tip: If a patient presents 8 hours after chest pain onset, troponin is the best marker — it's peaked and highly sensitive at this point.

NGN Case Study: Chest Pain Differential

Patient Presentation

Mr. Kim, 58 y/o, presents with dyspnea and chest tightness for 6 hours. History of hypertension and smoking.

  • VS: BP 160/95, HR 102, RR 24, SpO₂ 91% on RA
  • Troponin I: 0.04 ng/mL (normal <0.04)
  • BNP: 850 pg/mL
  • Chest X-ray: Bilateral pulmonary edema

What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: B

Rationale: The key discriminator is the normal troponin with markedly elevated BNP (850) plus clinical findings of pulmonary edema. In MI, troponin would be elevated by 6 hours. The dyspnea, hypoxemia, and pulmonary edema on CXR point to acute decompensated CHF. While PE can cause dyspnea, the BNP elevation and CXR findings are more consistent with heart failure.
