NCLEX Clinical Judgment Framework: The Complete 2026 Guide

The NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model (CJMM) is the secret to passing NCLEX Next Gen. Here's how to master all 6 cognitive skills.

By Nnamdi Okorafor, RN · 15-year ER nurse · Updated July 2026 · Obioma

Why the CJMM matters: The Next Generation NCLEX doesn't test what you memorized — it tests how you think. The NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model is the exact framework the exam uses to write questions. Learn it, and you'll recognize patterns in every case study.

What Is the Clinical Judgment Measurement Model?

The Clinical Judgment Measurement Model (CJMM) is a framework developed by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN) to measure how nurses think through patient scenarios. It has 6 cognitive skills that form a continuous loop:

  1. Recognize Cues — What data matters?
  2. Analyze Cues — What does this data mean?
  3. Prioritize Hypotheses — What's the most likely problem?
  4. Generate Solutions — What actions could fix this?
  5. Take Action — What will I do right now?
  6. Evaluate Outcomes — Did my action work?

Every NGN case study on the NCLEX tests one or more of these skills. When you learn to identify which skill a question is testing, you can eliminate wrong answers faster.

The 6 Cognitive Skills Explained

1. Recognize Cues

This is data collection. Cues can be:

  • Objective: Vital signs, lab values, physical assessment findings
  • Subjective: What the patient says (pain level, symptoms, history)
  • Contextual: Risk factors, medications, recent procedures

NCLEX tip: The exam will give you MORE data than you need. Your job is to filter out the noise and identify the cues that point to the actual problem.

2. Analyze Cues

This is pattern recognition. You're looking at the cues and asking: "What do these findings mean together?"

Example: A patient has BP 88/52, HR 118, RR 24, and reports abdominal pain. Together, they suggest shock — likely hypovolemic or septic.

NCLEX tip: Look for clusters of abnormal findings. One abnormal vital might not matter. Three abnormal vitals in the same direction always matter.

3. Prioritize Hypotheses

This is differential diagnosis for nurses. You generate possible explanations for the patient's condition, then rank them by urgency.

The ABC rule: Always prioritize Airway, Breathing, Circulation first. Then think about safety, pain, and psychosocial needs.

NCLEX tip: When a question asks "What is the nurse's priority?" the answer is almost always the option that addresses the most life-threatening problem first.

4. Generate Solutions

This is care planning. Based on your prioritized hypothesis, what interventions will address the problem?

Independent nursing actions are usually the correct answers on NCLEX:

  • Positioning the patient
  • Providing education
  • Monitoring and reassessment
  • Non-pharmacological comfort measures

5. Take Action

This is implementation. You select the BEST action from your list of solutions and execute it.

NCLEX tip: "Take Action" questions often test whether you know what to do FIRST. The first action is usually assessment (gather more data) or the most immediate life-saving intervention.

6. Evaluate Outcomes

This is reassessment. Did your intervention work? What data tells you the patient's condition is improving or worsening?

NCLEX tip: Look for measurable outcomes. "The patient reports feeling better" is subjective. "BP increased from 88/52 to 110/70" is objective and measurable.

How NCLEX Tests Each Skill

  • "Which findings are relevant?" → Recognize Cues
  • "What is the significance of...?" → Analyze Cues
  • "What is the priority problem?" → Prioritize Hypotheses
  • "Which actions should the nurse take?" → Generate Solutions / Take Action
  • "What outcome indicates improvement?" → Evaluate Outcomes
