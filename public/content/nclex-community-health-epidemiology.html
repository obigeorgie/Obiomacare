NCLEX Community Health & Epidemiology

Levels of prevention, epidemiology, public health, and community assessment for NCLEX success.

1. Levels of Prevention

Three Levels of Prevention

LevelGoalExamples
Primary PreventionPrevent disease before it occursImmunizations, health education, smoking cessation, seatbelts, handwashing, safe sex, nutrition counseling
Secondary PreventionEarly detection and prompt treatmentScreening (mammography, colonoscopy, BP checks), TB testing, STI screening, well-child visits
Tertiary PreventionReduce complications and disability; rehabilitationCardiac rehab, support groups, physical therapy, diabetic foot care, stroke rehabilitation
NCLEX Trap: "Teaching a diabetic patient foot care" = TERTIARY prevention (preventing complications of existing disease). "Teaching a community about diabetes risk factors" = PRIMARY prevention. "Offering free BP screenings at a health fair" = SECONDARY prevention. Know the difference — it's tested constantly.

2. Epidemiology Basics

Key Terms

TermDefinition
IncidenceNumber of NEW cases in a population over time (risk of developing disease)
PrevalenceTotal number of existing cases (new + old) at a point in time (burden of disease)
MorbidityIllness, disease, or disability rate
MortalityDeath rate
EndemicDisease consistently present in a population/area
EpidemicMore cases than expected in an area
PandemicEpidemic spreading across countries/continents
SporadicOccasional, scattered cases
Attack rateNumber of people exposed who become ill (used in outbreaks)
Case fatality rateProportion of diagnosed cases that die

Chain of Infection

Break the Chain:
1. Infectious agent (bacteria, virus, fungus)
2. Reservoir (human, animal, environment)
3. Portal of exit (respiratory, GI, blood, skin)
4. Mode of transmission (contact, droplet, airborne, vector, vehicle)
5. Portal of entry (respiratory, GI, mucous membranes, breaks in skin)
6. Susceptible host (immunocompromised, unvaccinated, elderly)
Nursing role: Break any link to prevent infection spread

3. Community Assessment

Community Health Nursing Process

Steps:
1. Assessment: Windshield survey, census data, key informant interviews, health statistics
2. Diagnosis: Community-focused nursing diagnosis ("Risk for ____ among ____ related to ____")
3. Planning: Prioritize based on community input, available resources, evidence
4. Implementation: Work WITH the community, not FOR the community
5. Evaluation: Measurable outcomes; did the intervention work?
NCLEX Trap: Community health nurses do NOT impose solutions. The community identifies its own priorities. The nurse facilitates, educates, and connects resources. "The nurse decides the community needs a diabetes program" is WRONG unless the community identified this need.

Windshield Survey

What to Observe:
• Housing conditions and age
• Transportation availability
• Grocery stores and food access
• Parks/recreation facilities
• Schools and libraries
• Healthcare facilities
• Industry and pollution
• People on the street (activity level, interactions)
• Signs of vandalism or decay
• Animal control

4. Disaster Preparedness

Disaster Management Phases

PhaseFocusNursing Role
Prevention/MitigationReduce risk before disasterCommunity education, vaccination programs, drills
PreparednessPlan and prepare resourcesStockpile supplies, train volunteers, develop protocols
ResponseImmediate actions during/afterTriage, first aid, shelter management, medication dispensing
RecoveryReturn to normal; long-term supportMental health support, disease surveillance, rebuilding
Personal/Family Preparedness:
• 3-day supply of water (1 gal/person/day)
• 3-day supply of non-perishable food
• Medications, first aid kit, flashlight, radio
• Important documents in waterproof container
• Family communication plan
• Pet supplies

Clinical Judgment Case Study

Community Health Epidemiology

Case: Foodborne Outbreak Investigation

A community health nurse receives reports of 15 people who ate at a local restaurant and developed diarrhea, vomiting, and fever within 6-24 hours. Stool cultures are pending. The restaurant serves chicken, rice, salads, and seafood.

What is the nurse's FIRST priority action?

  1. Close the restaurant immediately
  2. Report to the local health department
  3. Interview all affected individuals about what they ate
  4. Collect stool specimens from all 15 people
Answer: 2 — Report to the local health department.

Foodborne illness outbreaks are reportable conditions. The health department coordinates investigation, lab testing, and control measures. While interviewing patients and collecting specimens are important, the FIRST step is reporting to the authorities who have legal authority to inspect the restaurant, close it if needed, and prevent further illness. The 6-24 hour incubation suggests Staphylococcus aureus (1-6h) or Salmonella (6-72h).
