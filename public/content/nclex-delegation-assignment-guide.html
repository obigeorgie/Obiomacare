NCLEX Delegation and Assignment: RN vs LPN vs UAP

Scope of practice rules that eliminate guessing on delegation questions.

Delegation questions are the NCLEX's way of testing whether you understand who can do what. And the rules are clearer than you think.

Here's the complete scope of practice breakdown — what RNs, LPNs, and UAPs can and cannot do — plus the 10 rules that answer 90% of delegation questions.

The 10 Rules of Delegation

Rule 1: The RN is ULTIMATELY responsible for all delegated tasks.
If a UAP makes a mistake, the RN is accountable. Always.
Rule 2: Assess BEFORE you delegate.
You cannot delegate to someone who hasn't been assessed by an RN first.
Rule 3: The RIGHT task = predictable outcome, minimal risk.
If the task requires judgment, assessment, or clinical decision-making → RN only.
Rule 4: The RIGHT person = appropriate education, skills, competence.
Just because a UAP CAN take vitals doesn't mean THIS UAP has been checked off on it.
Rule 5: The RIGHT communication = clear, specific, expected outcomes.
"Check Mr. Jones" is not delegation. "Take vitals on Mr. Jones, report back if temp >101 or BP >160" is.
Rule 6: The RIGHT supervision = monitoring, evaluation, feedback.
Delegation doesn't mean "set it and forget it." You must follow up.
Rule 7: NEVER delegate: Assessment, planning, evaluation, or teaching.
These are RN-only functions. The nursing process belongs to the RN.
Rule 8: NEVER delegate: Medication administration to UAP.
No exceptions. Not PRN, not routine, not topical. Never.
Rule 9: NEVER delegate: Unstable patients.
Any patient whose condition is changing needs RN eyes on them.
Rule 10: LPNs can reinforce teaching but cannot do INITIAL teaching.
LPN: "Remember to take your medication with food." ✓
LPN: "Here's how your new medication works..." ✗ (RN only)

NCLEX Practice Scenarios

Scenario 1: Who Gets This Task?

A nurse on a busy med-surg floor needs to delegate tasks. Which task is appropriate for a UAP?

  • A. Administering a scheduled PO medication to a stable patient
  • B. Obtaining vital signs on a patient 2 hours post-op
  • C. Documenting intake and output for 4 patients
  • D. Changing a sterile wound dressing
Answer: C

A: Medication administration = NEVER delegated to UAP. Incorrect.
B: Post-op < 24 hours = unstable. Vital signs require RN or LPN assessment. Incorrect.
C: I&O documentation = predictable, no clinical judgment. UAP scope. Correct.
D: Sterile dressing = requires sterile technique. UAP scope in some facilities, but NCLEX typically reserves this for RN/LPN. Incorrect.

Scenario 2: The Delegation Mistake

A nurse delegates the following to a UAP. Which is an inappropriate delegation?

  • A. "Please assist Mr. Smith with his breakfast. He's stable and feeds himself."
  • B. "Take Mrs. Johnson's vital signs. Let me know if her BP is above 160."
  • C. "Give Mr. Davis his 0900 medications. The MAR is at the bedside."
  • D. "Help Ms. Brown ambulate to the bathroom. She had PT this morning and is steady."
Answer: C

C is NEVER appropriate: Medication administration to a UAP violates Rule 8. No exceptions. Even with the MAR present. This is a patient safety issue and the RN would be held accountable.

All other options (feeding assistance with stable patient, vitals with clear parameters, ambulation with stable gait) are appropriate UAP tasks.

Scenario 3: The LPN Question

Which task can an LPN appropriately perform?

  • A. Developing the initial plan of care for a new admission
  • B. Administering an IV push medication
  • C. Reinforcing discharge instructions previously given by the RN
  • D. Assessing a patient with a new chest tube
Answer: C

A: Initial plan of care = RN assessment/planning. LPN cannot. Incorrect.
B: IV push = RN only. LPN scope varies by state but NCLEX considers this RN-only. Incorrect.
C: Reinforcing teaching = LPN scope. The RN did initial teaching; LPN confirms understanding. Correct.
D: Assessment of new chest tube = requires RN judgment and evaluation. Incorrect.

The NCLEX Trap

The NGN NCLEX doesn't ask "Can a UAP do this?" directly. It embeds delegation into case studies:

"A nurse is caring for a group of patients. Which task is most appropriate to delegate to the UAP?"

To answer correctly, you must:

  1. Identify which tasks require RN judgment (eliminate these)
  2. Identify which tasks are stable/predictable (UAP scope)
  3. Match the task to the right person based on the 10 rules
