NCLEX Lab Values: Complete Reference

Normal ranges, critical values, and what abnormal results mean — all in one place.

The NCLEX tests lab values constantly. Not "What is normal K+?" but "Your patient has K+ of 6.2. What do you do FIRST?"

This guide gives you the normal ranges AND the clinical significance. Know these cold.

🔴 Neutropenic Precautions

ANC (Absolute Neutrophil Count) <1,000:

  • Private room, positive pressure
  • Hand hygiene (staff + visitors)
  • No fresh flowers, fruits, vegetables
  • No sick visitors
  • Daily temp checks

⚡ Hypoglycemia Protocol

Blood glucose <70 mg/dL:

  1. Conscious: 15g fast-acting carbs (glucose tablets, juice)
  2. Recheck in 15 minutes
  3. Repeat if still <70
  4. Unconscious: Glucagon 1mg IM or IV D50
  5. NEVER give food/drink to unconscious patient

🧠 ABG Interpretation Shortcut (ROME)

  • Respiratory Opposite: pH ↓ = PaCO2 ↑ (acidosis) | pH ↑ = PaCO2 ↓ (alkalosis)
  • Metabolic Equal: pH ↓ = HCO3 ↓ (acidosis) | pH ↑ = HCO3 ↑ (alkalosis)

📝 How the NCLEX Tests Lab Values

The NGN doesn't ask "What's normal K+?" It embeds lab values in scenarios:

"A patient on lisinopril has K+ of 5.8, Na+ of 138, and creatinine of 1.8. What is the nurse's FIRST action?"

To answer, you need to:

  1. Recognize K+ 5.8 is high (normal 3.5-5.0)
  2. Link to medication (ACE inhibitors cause K+ retention)
  3. Know the action (hold medication, call provider)
