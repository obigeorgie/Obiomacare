This guide covers the 25 lab values that actually appear on the exam, what the numbers mean clinically, and how the NGN uses them in case studies.

The "Must-Know Cold" List

If you only memorize 10 values, make it these. They appear in 80% of NCLEX questions:

1. Potassium (K+)

Normal: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L

Critical high (>6.0): Cardiac arrest risk. Kayexalate, insulin + glucose, calcium gluconate.

Critical low (<2.5): Arrhythmias, muscle weakness. IV KCl replacement (NEVER push IV potassium).

NGN tip: A patient on furosemide with muscle cramps → check K+ first.

2. Sodium (Na+)

Normal: 135-145 mEq/L

High (>145): Dehydration. Give fluids slowly. Rapid correction causes cerebral edema.

Low (<135): Fluid overload, SIADH. Restrict fluids. Correct slowly (<12 mEq/L per day).

NGN tip: Confusion + low Na+ → check if they're on SSRIs (cause SIADH).

3. Glucose

Normal: 70-100 mg/dL (fasting)

Critical high (>300): DKA risk. Insulin, fluids, K+ monitoring.

Critical low (<40): Give 15g fast carbs, recheck in 15 min. If unconscious → glucagon IM.

NGN tip: Patient on insulin with confusion → check glucose before anything else.

4. Hemoglobin

Normal: F 12-16, M 14-18 g/dL

Critical low (<7): Transfuse. <10 with symptoms (chest pain, dyspnea) → transfuse.

NGN tip: Post-op patient with tachycardia and low Hgb → suspect bleeding, not just pain.

5. Platelets

Normal: 150,000-400,000

<50,000: Bleeding precautions (no IM injections, soft toothbrush).

<20,000: Spontaneous bleeding risk. Platelet transfusion.

NGN tip: Patient with petechiae → check platelets first.

6. INR (Warfarin monitoring)

Normal: 0.8-1.1

Therapeutic on warfarin: 2.0-3.0 (3.0-4.5 for mechanical valves).

>4: Hold warfarin. Vitamin K if bleeding. Fresh frozen plasma if severe.

NGN tip: Patient on warfarin with black tarry stools → INR STAT.

7. Creatinine / eGFR

Creatinine: F 0.5-1.1, M 0.6-1.2

eGFR <30: Severe kidney disease. Dose-adjust meds. Nephrology consult.

eGFR <15: End-stage renal disease. Dialysis.

NGN tip: Before giving contrast dye → check creatinine/eGFR.

8. Troponin

Normal: <0.04 ng/mL

Elevated: Myocardial injury. Check trend (serial troponins q6-8h).

NGN tip: Chest pain + elevated troponin → activate cath lab (STEMI protocol).

9. ABG: pH, PaCO2, HCO3-

pH: 7.35-7.45 | PaCO2: 35-45 | HCO3-: 22-26

Remember ROME:

  • Respiratory Opposite (pH ↓, PaCO2 ↑ = respiratory acidosis)
  • Metabolic Equal (pH ↓, HCO3- ↓ = metabolic acidosis)

NGN tip: COPD patient with pH 7.32, PaCO2 55 → respiratory acidosis, expected.

10. WBC

Normal: 4,500-11,000/mm³

>11,000: Infection, inflammation, stress. >30,000 → leukemia workup.

<4,500: Immunosuppression risk. <2,000 → neutropenic precautions.

NGN tip: Post-chemo patient with fever + low WBC → sepsis protocol immediately.

How the NGN Tests Lab Values

The Next Generation NCLEX doesn't ask "What is the normal range for potassium?" It gives you a case study:

Scenario: A 68-year-old patient is admitted for chest pain. Labs show: K+ 5.8, Na+ 138, Cr 1.8, Glucose 210, Troponin 0.2. The patient is on lisinopril, metformin, and furosemide at home.

What do you do first?

The answer isn't "call the doctor." It's: Address the hyperkalemia (K+ 5.8) because it's immediately life-threatening. Draw an ECG, hold ACE inhibitors, prepare kayexalate or insulin + glucose.

The elevated troponin is important but stable (not STEMI level). The elevated creatinine + metformin is concerning but not emergent.

The Lab Value Mnemonics That Work

  • Potassium: "3.5 to 5 is ALIVE" — anything outside needs action
  • Sodium: "135 to 145 keeps you alive"
  • Glucose: "70 to 100 is good for you"
  • ABG: ROME (Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal)
  • Platelets: "150K is the key" — below = precautions

Bottom Line
