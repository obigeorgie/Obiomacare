Mechanical Ventilation

Ventilator modes, settings, alarms, ABG management, and weaning — the high-acuity content that separates pass from fail.

💡 Remember: "The ventilator does the breathing work. The nurse ensures the patient and machine work together safely."

🌬️ Ventilator Basics

Indications for Mechanical Ventilation

  • Respiratory failure: PaO₂ <60 on 60% FiO₂ or PaCO₂ >50 with acidosis
  • Apnea or respiratory arrest
  • Airway protection: GCS ≤8, inability to protect airway
  • Increased work of breathing: Severe asthma, COPD exacerbation, pulmonary edema
  • Surgical: During anesthesia, post-op with unstable airway
NCLEX Trap: A patient with COPD and chronic CO₂ retention (baseline PaCO₂ 55) does NOT automatically need intubation. The key is acute change + acidosis (pH <7.35). Chronic retention is expected.

Key Ventilator Settings (Know These Cold)

SettingWhat It ControlsTypical Starting Value
FiO₂Fraction of inspired oxygen (% oxygen delivered)40-60% (start high, wean to SpO₂ 92-96%)
TV / VtTidal volume (mL per breath)6-8 mL/kg ideal body weight
RRRespiratory rate (breaths/min)12-16
PEEPPositive end-expiratory pressure (cmH₂O)5-10 (prevents alveolar collapse)
PIPPeak inspiratory pressure (cmH₂O)<35 (alarm if >40)
Plateau PressurePressure at end-inspiration (hold)<30 cmH₂O
CRITICAL: Plateau pressure >30 cmH₂O increases risk of barotrauma (pneumothorax) and volutrauma (lung injury). If plateau >30, ↓ tidal volume or ↓ PEEP (if SpO₂ allows).

🔧 Ventilator Modes

Volume-Controlled Modes

ModeHow It WorksBest For
AC (Assist-Control)Patient gets a SET tidal volume every breath — either triggered by patient or machine. Every breath is a FULL breath.Full ventilatory support. Patients with no spontaneous effort or weak effort.
VC-SIMVMachine delivers set TV at set rate. Patient can breathe spontaneously between — but those spontaneous breaths are NOT assisted.Weaning. Allows patient to "exercise" respiratory muscles.
NCLEX Trap: In AC mode, if the patient breathes 30 times/min, they get 30 FULL tidal volumes. This can cause respiratory alkalosis (blowing off too much CO₂) and auto-PEEP (air trapping). If RR is too high, increase sedation or switch to SIMV.

Pressure-Controlled Modes

ModeHow It WorksBest For
PCVDelivers set pressure (not volume). Volume varies based on lung compliance.ARDS, poor lung compliance, risk of barotrauma. Protects lungs from high pressures.
PSVPatient initiates breath, ventilator adds set pressure support. No set rate.Weaning. Patient must have reliable spontaneous effort.
CPAPContinuous positive airway pressure. Patient breathes spontaneously at elevated baseline pressure.Non-invasive (BiPAP/CPAP mask). Sleep apnea, CHF, COPD exacerbation.
BiPAPTwo pressures: IPAP (inspiratory) and EPAP (expiratory). Assists inhalation.COPD, CHF, obesity hypoventilation. Avoid if GCS <8 or unable to protect airway.

High-Acuity Modes

ModeHow It WorksBest For
APRVAirway Pressure Release Ventilation. High pressure for long time, brief "releases" to low pressure. Allows spontaneous breathing at high pressure.Severe ARDS, refractory hypoxemia. Improves oxygenation while reducing ventilator-induced lung injury.
HFOVHigh-Frequency Oscillatory Ventilation. Tiny breaths at very high rates (3-15 Hz). Constant mean airway pressure.Severe ARDS when conventional ventilation fails. "Rescue" therapy.
ECMOExtracorporeal membrane oxygenation. Blood oxygenated outside body. NOT a ventilator mode — it's lung bypass.Refractory respiratory failure when all ventilation strategies fail.
🧠 Mnemonic — Mode Selection: "AC for full support, SIMV for weaning, PC for protection, PSV for breathing practice, CPAP/BiPAP for non-invasive."

🚨 Ventilator Alarms — Priority Actions

High-Pressure Alarm

Cause: Increased resistance or decreased compliance

DO THIS FIRST (in order):
  1. Check the patient first — look, listen, feel
  2. Look for: biting ET tube, coughing, secretion plugging, bronchospasm, pneumothorax, decreased lung compliance
  3. Check for kinked tubing or water in tubing
  4. Suction if secretions suspected
  5. Auscultate for pneumothorax (absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation)
  6. If still high — notify RT/provider, may need sedation or bronchodilator
NCLEX Trap: When a ventilator alarms, never silence it first. Always assess the patient before troubleshooting equipment. The patient is the priority, not the machine.

Low-Pressure Alarm

Cause: Leak in the system

Check (in order):
  1. Disconnected circuit (most common)
  2. ET tube cuff leak (check cuff pressure, may need reintubation if deflated)
  3. Loose connections anywhere in circuit
  4. Chest tube leak (if present)
  5. Artificial airway dislodged

Low Exhaled Volume Alarm

Cause: Air leak OR patient not getting set tidal volume

Same causes as low-pressure alarm. Check circuit integrity, cuff, and patient-ventilator synchrony.

Apnea Alarm

Cause: Patient not triggering breaths

Actions:
  1. Assess LOC — is patient oversedated?
  2. Check sedation score (RASS target is usually -2 to 0)
  3. Stimulate patient, encourage breathing
  4. If apnea continues — patient may need AC mode or increased support

🩸 ABG Management on the Ventilator

Quick ABG Adjustment Guide

ProblemABG FindingVentilator Change
HypoxemiaPaO₂ <60, SpO₂ <90%↑ FiO₂ first, then ↑ PEEP
Hypercapnia (acute)PaCO₂ >50 + pH <7.35↑ TV or ↑ RR (increases minute ventilation)
Respiratory alkalosisPaCO₂ <35 + pH >7.45↓ RR or ↓ TV, decrease sedation
Metabolic acidosisHCO₃⁻ <22 + pH <7.35Compensatory hyperventilation (↑ RR). Treat underlying cause.
Metabolic alkalosisHCO₃⁻ >26 + pH >7.45↓ RR slightly (compensatory hypoventilation holds CO₂)
NCLEX Trap: In COPD patients with chronic CO₂ retention, target SpO₂ is 88-92% (not 94-98%). Higher SpO₂ suppresses their hypoxic respiratory drive. Also, don't "correct" their PaCO₂ to normal — their kidneys have compensated (high bicarb).

The PaO₂/FiO₂ Ratio (P/F Ratio)

Used to assess oxygenation and severity of ARDS.

Formula: PaO₂ ÷ FiO₂ (as decimal)

  • Normal: 300-500
  • Mild ARDS: 200-300
  • Moderate ARDS: 100-200
  • Severe ARDS: <100
Example: PaO₂ 80 on 60% FiO₂ (0.6) → 80 ÷ 0.6 = 133 → Moderate ARDS

😴 Sedation & Ventilator Synchrony

Sedation Goals (RASS Scale)

RASS ScoreDescriptionTarget
+4 to +1Combative to restless↓ Sedation, reassess pain/anxiety cause
0Alert and calm✅ Ideal for weaning
-1 to -2Drowsy, light sedation✅ Acceptable for most ventilated patients

Daily Sedation Interruption: Stop sedation daily to assess neurologic status, respiratory drive, and readiness for weaning. This reduces ventilator days and ICU delirium.

🚶 Weaning from Mechanical Ventilation

Readiness Criteria (Must Meet ALL)

  • Resolution of underlying cause for intubation
  • Hemodynamically stable (minimal or no vasopressors)
  • SpO₂ >90% on FiO₂ ≤40% and PEEP ≤8
  • Adequate cough and airway reflexes
  • Afebrile
  • No significant respiratory acidosis
  • Adequate mental status (follows commands)

Weaning Parameters

ParameterGood Weaning Candidate
RSBI (Rapid Shallow Breathing Index)<105 (RR/Vt in L). Lower is better.
Minute Ventilation (Ve)<10 L/min
Maximal Inspiratory Pressure (MIP/NIF)<-20 to -25 cmH₂O (more negative = stronger)
Tidal Volume (spontaneous)>5 mL/kg
Vital Capacity>10-15 mL/kg
RSBI: "If it's HIGH, don't let them FLY." RSBI >105 = poor weaning candidate.

Weaning Methods

  • T-piece/SBT (Spontaneous Breathing Trial): Disconnect ventilator, place on T-piece with supplemental O₂. Assess for 30-120 min. Pass = no distress, RR <35, SpO₂ >90%, HR <140, no arrhythmias, no diaphoresis/anxiety.
  • Pressure Support Weaning: Gradually ↓ pressure support level (e.g., from 15 to 5 cmH₂O). When patient tolerates PS 5-8, extubate.
  • SIMV Weaning: Gradually ↓ SIMV rate (e.g., from 12 to 4). Patient takes over more breaths. Slower method, less favored now.
NCLEX Trap: After extubation, the priority is airway patency. Monitor for stridor (post-extubation laryngeal edema), voice changes, ability to clear secretions. Have reintubation equipment at bedside. Racemic epinephrine for stridor.

🫁 Complications of Mechanical Ventilation

Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP)

Occurs ≥48 hours after intubation. Leading cause of death in ventilated patients.

VAP Bundle (Prevention):
  1. Head of bed elevated 30-45° (prevents aspiration)
  2. Daily sedation assessment + spontaneous breathing trials
  3. Peptic ulcer prophylaxis (if indicated)
  4. DVT prophylaxis
  5. Oral care with chlorhexidine q2-6h
  6. Subglottic suctioning ETT (if available)

Barotrauma & Volutrauma

  • Pneumothorax: Sudden ↑ peak pressure, ↓ SpO₂, absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation away from affected side, subcutaneous emphysema. STAT chest X-ray.
  • Pneumomediastinum: Chest pain, Hamman's crunch (crackling sound over heart)
  • Prevention: Plateau pressure <30, appropriate TV (6 mL/kg), avoid excessive PEEP

Oxygen Toxicity

FiO₂ >60% for >24 hours can cause:

  • Absorption atelectasis
  • ARDS-like lung injury
  • Retinopathy of prematurity (in neonates)
Goal: Wean FiO₂ to maintain SpO₂ 92-96% using the lowest possible setting.

🎯 Clinical Judgment Case Studies

NCLEX-Style Critical Care

Case 1: Ventilator Alarm

A ventilated patient's high-pressure alarm sounds. Assessment reveals: anxious, diaphoretic, using accessory muscles, SpO₂ 86%. Breath sounds are diminished on the right. Trachea deviated to the left.

What is your priority action?

  1. Suction the airway
  2. Administer a bronchodilator
  3. Prepare for needle decompression/chest tube insertion
  4. Increase sedation
Answer: 3 — Prepare for needle decompression/chest tube insertion.

This is a tension pneumothorax: high pressure alarm + absent breath sounds + tracheal deviation away from affected side = classic presentation. This is a medical emergency. Needle decompression (14g needle, 2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line) followed by chest tube. Don't wait for CXR.
NCLEX-Style ABG

Case 2: ABG Interpretation

A patient on AC ventilation has this ABG: pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 55, PaO₂ 68, HCO₃⁻ 24, on FiO₂ 50%.

What ventilator change is most appropriate?

  1. Increase FiO₂ to 60%
  2. Increase tidal volume or respiratory rate
  3. Increase PEEP
  4. Decrease respiratory rate
Answer: 2 — Increase tidal volume or respiratory rate.

ABG shows respiratory acidosis (pH low, PaCO₂ high, HCO₃ normal). The problem is ventilation (CO₂ retention), not oxygenation (PaO₂ 68 is acceptable on 50% FiO₂). To blow off more CO₂, increase minute ventilation (↑ TV or ↑ RR). Increasing FiO₂ or PEEP won't fix the acidosis.
SATA: A nurse is caring for a patient being weaned from mechanical ventilation. Which findings indicate the patient is tolerating weaning? (Select all that apply.)
  1. Respiratory rate of 32 breaths/min
  2. SpO₂ of 94% on 40% FiO₂
  3. Heart rate increase from 78 to 110
  4. Patient appears comfortable and alert
  5. Blood pressure 148/92 (baseline 120/80)
  6. Absence of diaphoresis or accessory muscle use
Correct: B, D, F

A: NO — RR 32 is too high (tachypnea = distress).
B: Yes — SpO₂ >90% on acceptable FiO₂.
C: NO — HR increase >20 bpm indicates stress/failure.
D: Yes — Comfortable and alert = good sign.
E: NO — Hypertension suggests stress response.
F: Yes — No distress signs = tolerance.
