NCLEX Mental Health Nursing

Psychiatric disorders, therapeutic communication, medications, and safety priorities for the NCLEX mental health questions.

Therapeutic Communication: The Foundation

Core Principles:
• Active listening — full attention, eye contact, appropriate silence
• Empathy — understand the patient's perspective without judgment
• Acceptance — accept the patient as they are, not approval of behavior
• Genuineness — be authentic and honest
• Positive regard — respect the patient's dignity and worth
NON-THERAPEUTIC TECHNIQUES (Avoid on NCLEX!):
• Giving advice ("You should...")
• False reassurance ("Everything will be fine")
• Minimizing feelings ("It's not that bad")
• Asking "why" questions (sounds accusatory)
• Changing the subject
• Offering opinions instead of exploring feelings
THERAPEUTIC RESPONSES (Choose on NCLEX!):
• "Tell me more about that..."
• "You seem upset. What's happening?"
• "That sounds difficult. How are you coping?"
• "What I'm hearing is..." (reflection)
• "Help me understand what you're experiencing."

1. Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders

Positive vs Negative Symptoms

Positive Symptoms (Excess)Negative Symptoms (Deficit)
Hallucinations (auditory most common)Flat affect
Delusions (paranoid, grandiose)Alogia (poverty of speech)
Disorganized thinking/speechAvolition (lack of motivation)
Bizarre behaviorSocial withdrawal
Anhedonia (inability to feel pleasure)
Safety Priority: Command hallucinations telling patient to harm self/others = immediate intervention. Assess for suicidal/homicidal ideation.
Nursing Interventions:
• Establish trust, be consistent
• Use clear, simple communication
• Do NOT argue with delusions or hallucinations ("I don't hear the voices, but I understand they seem real to you")
• Medications: Antipsychotics (haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine, clozapine)
• Monitor for extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS),NMS, metabolic syndrome

• Financial exploitation (spending sprees)

• Accidents (reckless driving)

MNEMONIC: "DIG FAST" — Mania Symptoms
Distractibility | Indiscretion | Grandiosity | Flight of ideas | Activity increase | Sleep decrease | Talkativeness

3. Major Depressive Disorder

SIGECAPS Criteria

SymptomDescription
SleepInsomnia or hypersomnia
InterestAnhedonia (loss of interest)
GuiltFeelings of worthlessness
EnergyFatigue
ConcentrationDifficulty concentrating
AppetiteChanges in weight/appetite
PsychomotorAgitation or retardation
SuicidalityThoughts of death or suicide

Diagnosis: ≥5 symptoms for 2 weeks, including depressed mood OR anhedonia.

SUICIDE ASSESSMENT IS PRIORITY #1
Always assess: Plan, Means, Intent, Timeline
NEVER promise confidentiality if patient is suicidal — duty to warn/protect.
Remove all potentially harmful objects. 1:1 observation if high risk.
Nursing Interventions for Depression:
• Encourage expression of feelings
• Structure daily activities (combat withdrawal)
• Monitor sleep, nutrition, self-care
• Involve family in care (with permission)
• Watch for signs of improvement (energy returns before mood = suicide risk increases)
• Medications: SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs, atypical antidepressants

• Stay with patient (don't leave alone)
• Speak in short, simple sentences

• Reduce environmental stimuli

Borderline Personality Disorder

Key features: Unstable relationships, self-image, and emotions; fear of abandonment; impulsivity; self-harm; splitting (idealizing then devaluing).

Safety: High risk for self-harm and suicide. Frequent ED visits. Develop written safety plan.
Nursing Approach:
• Set clear, consistent boundaries (most important!)
• Do NOT rescue or be manipulated
• Validate feelings while maintaining limits
• Use same staff when possible (reduce splitting)
• DBT (Dialectical Behavior Therapy) is treatment of choice

Alcohol Withdrawal Can Be FATAL
• Delirium tremens (DTs): Onset 48-72h after last drink
• Symptoms: Severe agitation, hallucinations, autonomic instability (tachycardia, fever, HTN), seizures
• Requires ICU-level care, continuous monitoring
• CIWA-Ar score ≥15 = severe withdrawal

NGN Case Study: Acute Psychiatric Crisis

Client Scenario

Ms. Taylor, 24-year-old female, is brought to the ED by police after attempting to jump from a bridge. She is agitated, pacing, and repeatedly states, "I can't do this anymore. Nobody would miss me." She has a history of bipolar disorder and stopped taking her lithium 2 weeks ago. She has not slept in 4 days.

Vital Signs Mental Status History

Vital Signs: BP 142/88, HR 118, RR 22, Temp 37.2°C

Mental Status: Agitated, pressured speech, flight of ideas, poor eye contact. Threatens to "walk out" if not released. Has multiple superficial lacerations on forearms (horizontal, non-lethal pattern). No active psychosis.

History: Bipolar I disorder, last manic episode 8 months ago. Lithium 900mg daily (non-adherent). No substance use. Lives alone. No family support locally. Previous suicide attempt at age 19 (overdose).

Question 1 — Recognize Cues: What is the highest priority nursing concern?

  • A. Medication non-adherence leading to mood instability
  • B. Acute risk of suicide and need for safety precautions
  • C. Sleep deprivation causing psychotic symptoms
  • D. Lack of family support affecting discharge planning

Correct: B. Acute risk of suicide and need for safety precautions

Safety is ALWAYS the priority in psychiatric emergencies. This patient has active suicidal ideation with a plan (jumping from bridge), means (height), and intent ("I can't do this anymore"). Previous attempt increases risk. Immediate 1:1 observation, removal of all potential ligatures/sharp objects, and placement in a safe room are required. While medication adherence and sleep are important, they are secondary to preventing immediate self-harm.

Question 2 — Analyze Cues: The patient demands to leave, stating, "I have rights. You can't hold me here." Which response is most appropriate?

  • A. "You're right. I'll get your discharge papers ready"
  • B. "I understand you want to leave, but I'm concerned about your safety. Let's talk about what's going on"
  • C. "You're on a legal hold and cannot leave. Sit down"
  • D. "If you leave, I'll have to call the police"

Correct: B. "I understand you want to leave, but I'm concerned about your safety. Let's talk about what's going on"

Therapeutic communication uses empathy, sets boundaries without being punitive, and invites dialogue. Option A is unsafe — patient is actively suicidal. Option C is authoritarian and will escalate agitation. Option D is threatening. The nurse should validate feelings while maintaining safety. If the patient attempts to leave, an involuntary hold may be necessary, but the first approach is always therapeutic communication.

Question 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses: The provider orders lithium 600mg PO, risperidone 2mg PO, and lorazepam 1mg IM PRN agitation. Which medication should the nurse question?

  • A. Lithium — patient was non-adherent, may need lower dose to restart
  • B. Risperidone — antipsychotic may worsen mania
  • C. Lorazepam — benzodiazepines are contraindicated in bipolar disorder
  • D. No medications need to be questioned

Correct: A. Lithium — patient was non-adherent, may need lower dose to restart

Restarting lithium at 600mg after 2 weeks of non-adherence could cause toxicity because the patient may have lost tolerance. Lithium levels should be checked before reinitiating, and the dose may need to be lower or titrated. Risperidone is appropriate for acute mania. Lorazepam is appropriate PRN for agitation in bipolar disorder (not contraindicated). However, the provider may have intentionally ordered this dose — the nurse should clarify, not necessarily refuse.

Question 4 — Generate Solutions: Ms. Taylor is admitted to the psychiatric unit. She is placed on suicide precautions. Which intervention is most appropriate?

  • A. Allow her to keep her phone and charger for distraction
  • B. Check on her every 15 minutes with visual observation
  • C. Place her in a private room at the end of the hall
  • D. Remove her clothing and provide a hospital gown only

Correct: B. Check on her every 15 minutes with visual observation

Standard suicide precautions include q15min checks with visual observation (not just door checks). A charger cord is a ligature risk and should be removed. A room at the end of the hall reduces staff visibility — place near the nurses' station. Clothing is typically allowed unless it has strings, belts, or sharp items. The gown-only approach is unnecessarily restrictive unless the patient has attempted self-harm with clothing.

Question 5 — Take Action: On day 3, Ms. Taylor is calmer and requests to have her shoelaces back. She states, "I'm feeling much better. I just had a bad day." What is the nurse's best response?

  • A. "That's great! I'll return your belongings"
  • B. "I'm glad you're feeling better, but we need to keep safety precautions in place per protocol"
  • C. "Have you talked to your doctor about this?"
  • D. "You tried to kill yourself 3 days ago. You're not getting anything back"

Correct: B. "I'm glad you're feeling better, but we need to keep safety precautions in place per protocol"

A rapid improvement in mood can actually indicate increased suicide risk — the patient may have decided on a plan and feels relief. Alternatively, it could be genuine improvement. Either way, safety precautions should not be discontinued based on patient self-report alone. The treatment team makes that decision. Option A is unsafe. Option C passes responsibility. Option D is judgmental and non-therapeutic.

Question 6 — Evaluate Outcomes: Ms. Taylor is being discharged on day 7. Which discharge instruction is highest priority?

  • A. "Take your lithium exactly as prescribed — never stop abruptly"
  • B. "Avoid alcohol and recreational drugs"
  • C. "Call 911 if you have thoughts of harming yourself"
  • D. "Attend all follow-up appointments with your psychiatrist"

Correct: C. "Call 911 if you have thoughts of harming yourself"

While ALL of these are important discharge instructions, the highest priority is ensuring the patient knows what to do in a crisis. Providing a crisis plan with specific phone numbers (911, crisis hotline, psychiatrist) empowers the patient to seek help before acting on suicidal thoughts. Medication adherence (A), substance avoidance (B), and follow-up (D) are critical for long-term management, but crisis safety planning is the immediate priority for a patient with recent suicidal ideation.
