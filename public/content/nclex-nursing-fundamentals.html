NCLEX Nursing Fundamentals

Vital signs, health assessment, hygiene, safety, and infection control for NCLEX success.

1. Vital Signs

Normal Adult Vital Signs

Vital SignNormal RangeKey Notes
Temperature36.0–37.5°C (96.8–99.5°F)Oral, tympanic, temporal, rectal, axillary sites vary
Pulse60–100 bpmRate, rhythm, strength, equality
Respirations12–20/minRate, rhythm, depth, effort
Blood Pressure<120/<80 mmHgHypertension: ≥130/≥80 (Stage 1)
Oxygen Saturation95–100%<90% = hypoxemia (supplemental O₂ needed)
Pain0/105th vital sign; assess using appropriate scale
NCLEX Trap: "The patient has a temp of 37.8°C (100°F). What is the priority nursing action?" — This is a LOW-GRADE fever. Priority depends on context (post-op day 1 = expected; neutropenic patient = emergency). Always consider the clinical situation, not just the number.

Blood Pressure Measurement

Correct Technique:
• Patient seated, arm supported at heart level
• Cuff width = 40% of arm circumference; bladder covers 80% of arm
• Cuff too small → FALSE HIGH reading
• Cuff too large → FALSE LOW reading
• Palpate brachial artery; inflate 20–30 mmHg above estimated SBP
• Deflate at 2–3 mmHg/sec
• Wait 1–2 minutes between measurements
• Use same arm for serial measurements
Factors Falsely ELEVATING BP:
Full bladder   Arm below heart   Cuff too small   Emotional stress   Smoking (within 30 min)

2. Health Assessment

Head-to-Toe Assessment Order

Systematic Approach (Inspection → Palpation → Percussion → Auscultation):
1. General: LOC, appearance, distress, nutrition, mobility
2. Vital signs: Full set + pain + SpO₂
3. Skin: Color, temp, turgor, lesions, edema
4. HEENT: Pupils (PERRLA), mucous membranes, neck veins
5. Cardiovascular: Heart sounds, pulses (radial, pedal), capillary refill
6. Respiratory: Breath sounds, effort, symmetry, cough
7. GI: Bowel sounds, distention, tenderness, last BM
8. GU: Urinary output, color, last void, catheter status
9. Neuro: GCS, orientation, motor strength, sensation
10. Musculoskeletal: ROM, strength, gait

3. Safety

Fall Risk Assessment & Prevention

Risk FactorPrevention Strategy
Age >65, history of fallsNon-skid footwear, bed alarm, frequent rounding
Medications (sedatives, diuretics)Review meds; toileting schedule for diuretics
Impaired mobilityAssistive devices, PT consult, call light within reach
Orthostatic hypotensionChange positions slowly; dangle before standing
Cognitive impairmentVisual cues, family involvement, low bed position
IncontinenceScheduled toileting (q2h); easy access to bathroom
Universal Fall Precautions:
• Bed in LOW position with brakes locked
• Call light, phone, personal items within reach
• Non-skid socks/footwear
• Clear pathway to bathroom
• Adequate lighting (night light)
• Teach patient to call before getting up

Restraint Use (Last Resort)

NCLEX Trap: Restraints require a PHYSICIAN ORDER, face-to-face evaluation within 1 hour, continuous monitoring, and renewal every 24 hours. NEVER tie restraints to bed rails. Alternatives first: sitter, family presence, distraction, frequent reorientation.
Restraint Safety:
• Quick-release knot only (never a standard knot)
• Assess circulation q15min (neurovascular checks)
• Release and reposition q2h
• Document rationale, alternatives tried, response
• Never apply to neck, chest, or across joints
• Discontinue ASAP

4. Hygiene & Comfort

Bathing Priorities

Order of Bathing (clean to dirty):
1. Eyes (inner to outer canthus, separate cloth per eye)
2. Face, ears, neck
3. Arms, hands, axillae
4. Chest, abdomen
5. Legs, feet
6. Perineal area (LAST — separate cloth, front to back)
• Change water before perineal care
NCLEX Trap: For a female patient, always wipe perineum FRONT TO BACK (urethra to anus) to prevent UTI. Never go back to front. For an uncircumcised male, retract foreskin to clean, then replace.

Clinical Judgment Case Study

Fundamentals Vital Signs

Case: Post-Op Vital Signs

A 45-year-old is 2 hours post-op laparoscopic appendectomy. Vital signs: BP 92/58, HR 118, RR 24, SpO₂ 95% on 2L NC, temp 37.2°C. Urine output last hour: 20 mL. Patient reports "feeling dizzy."

What is the nurse's FIRST action?

  1. Increase IV fluid rate and notify surgeon
  2. Place in Trendelenburg position
  3. Assess surgical dressing for bleeding
  4. Administer PRN antiemetic for dizziness
Answer: 1 — Increase IV fluids and notify surgeon.

Hypotension (SBP <100) + tachycardia + low UOP + dizziness = hypovolemia/hemorrhage until proven otherwise. This is a post-op patient — internal bleeding is the biggest concern. Increase IV fluids (crystalloid bolus), assess dressing/drains, check Hgb/Hct, notify surgeon. Trendelenburg is not recommended anymore (increases ICP, doesn't improve perfusion). Assess for bleeding simultaneously but don't delay fluid resuscitation.
