Nursing Informatics & Technology

Electronic health records, clinical decision support, telehealth, data security, and healthcare technology for the NCLEX.

Electronic Health Records (EHR)

EHR Benefits & Challenges

Benefits:

  • Improved access to patient information across care settings
  • Clinical decision support (alerts, reminders, guidelines)
  • Reduced medication errors (barcode medication administration)
  • Electronic prescribing (e-prescribing) — reduces transcription errors
  • Data analytics for quality improvement and research
  • Improved legibility and completeness of documentation

Challenges:

  • Alert fatigue (too many non-actionable alerts leads to ignoring important ones)
  • Workflow disruption and increased documentation burden
  • Technology failures and downtime procedures
  • Privacy and security concerns
  • Interoperability issues between systems
Barcode Medication Administration (BCMA): Scan patient wristband AND medication. Right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, right time, right documentation. If barcode doesn't scan — NEVER override without verifying with pharmacy. Common errors: scanning wristband but not medication, pre-scanning medications, working around system.

Clinical Decision Support (CDS)

Types of CDS

  • Reminders: Flu vaccine due, mammogram due, diabetic eye exam due
  • Alerts: Drug-drug interactions, allergy alerts, high-risk medication dosing
  • Order sets: Pre-built evidence-based order sets (sepsis bundle, stroke protocol)
  • Diagnostic support: Differential diagnosis suggestions, lab interpretation
  • Reference information: Drug monographs, clinical guidelines embedded in EHR
NCLEX Trap: Clinical decision support systems ASSIST nursing judgment — they do NOT replace it. A nurse must still use critical thinking. If an alert seems inappropriate (e.g., allergy to dye in a medication where dye is not present), verify rather than blindly accepting or overriding.

Telehealth & Remote Monitoring

Telehealth Applications

  • Synchronous: Live video consultation between provider and patient
  • Asynchronous (store-and-forward): Images, data, video sent for later review (dermatology images, radiology reads)
  • Remote patient monitoring (RPM): Continuous monitoring of vital signs, glucose, weight, SpO2 at home with data transmitted to providers

Nursing considerations:

  • Ensure patient has adequate technology and digital literacy
  • Teach patient how to use devices and troubleshoot
  • Establish protocols for abnormal values — who to contact, when to escalate
  • Document telehealth encounters same as in-person visits
  • Verify patient identity and privacy during virtual visits
Telehealth Limitations: Cannot perform physical examination (palpation, auscultation, deep tendon reflexes). Not appropriate for emergencies. Technology barriers for elderly, low-income, rural patients. Licensure requirements (provider must be licensed in patient's state).

Health Information Privacy & Security

HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)

Protected Health Information (PHI): Any information that can identify a patient and relates to health status, provision of care, or payment. Includes name, DOB, SSN, medical record number, photos, and any unique identifier.

Minimum necessary standard: Only access and share the minimum information needed for the task

Patient rights:

  • Access their own medical records (within 30 days, reasonable cost for copies)
  • Request amendments to records
  • Accounting of disclosures
  • Request restrictions (provider not required to agree)
  • Confidential communications

HIPAA Violations — What Nurses Must Know

  • Accessing records without need: Looking up family members, celebrities, coworkers, ex-partners — all violations
  • Discussing patients in public: Elevators, cafeterias, social media
  • Leaving screens unattended: Always log out or lock screen when stepping away
  • Sharing passwords: Never share login credentials
  • Social media: NEVER post patient information, photos, or details. Even "de-identified" stories can be traced
  • Texting PHI: Use encrypted, HIPAA-compliant messaging apps only
NCLEX Trap: A nurse CAN access their own medical record through normal patient portal/records request process. However, a nurse CANNOT access their own record through the EHR they use for work — that would be the same as accessing any patient record without a treatment/payment/healthcare operations reason. Go through proper channels (HIM/patient portal).

Nursing Informatics Competencies

Technology Informatics Guiding Education Reform (TIGER) Initiative

Core competencies for all nurses:

  • Information literacy: Ability to identify, locate, evaluate, and effectively use information
  • Computer literacy: Basic computer skills, EHR navigation, common software
  • Clinical information management: Documentation, data entry, retrieval, and analysis
  • Informatics knowledge: Understanding of information and communication technologies in healthcare

Quality Improvement & Data

  • Benchmarking: Comparing performance against standards or other organizations
  • Core measures: Standardized quality metrics (CMS core measures: AMI, heart failure, pneumonia, surgery)
  • Nursing-sensitive indicators: Falls, pressure injuries, CAUTI, CLABSI, restraint prevalence, patient satisfaction
  • Meaningful use: CMS incentive program for EHR adoption (now Promoting Interoperability)
  • Data integrity: Accurate, complete, consistent, timely documentation

Clinical Judgment Case Study

Informatics Patient Safety

Case: Barcode Scanning Override

A nurse is administering medications. The barcode scanner is not working for one medication. The patient states, "I take a different dose at home." The nurse is behind schedule and has 6 more patients to medicate.

What is the nurse's best action?

  1. Give the home dose since the patient knows best
  2. Override the scanner and document the reason
  3. Hold the medication and verify with pharmacy/physician
  4. Scan the medication manually later when the scanner works
Answer: 3 — Hold medication and verify with pharmacy/physician.

Two red flags: scanner malfunction AND patient reports different home dose. Never override safety systems when there is a discrepancy. The "5 Rights" of medication administration require verification. Home dose may be outdated or incorrect. Call pharmacy to verify current order. Document the hold and reason. Patient safety > schedule. This is a Just Culture principle — the system failure (broken scanner) should be reported, not worked around.
