NCLEX Pharmacology: Drug Classes You Must Know

The top 10 drug classes tested on the NCLEX, with memory tricks that actually work.

Pharmacology is one of the highest-yield topics on the NCLEX. But here's the secret: You don't need to memorize every drug. You need to know the drug classes, their suffixes, and what to watch for.

The NGN NCLEX tests pharmacology through clinical judgment scenarios — not "What does metoprolol do?" but "Your patient on metoprolol reports dizziness and HR 52. What do you do FIRST?"

The Top 10 Drug Classes

1. ACE Inhibitors -pril

Examples: Lisinopril, Enalapril, Captopril

Action: Block conversion of angiotensin I to II → vasodilation → ↓ BP

Use: Hypertension, heart failure, post-MI

Memory trick: "ACE = Ace of Hearts" — used for heart failure and BP

ADVERSE EFFECTS TO KNOW:

  • Dry cough (most common — switch to ARB)
  • Hyperkalemia (K+ >5.0 = hold medication, notify provider)
  • Angioedema (swelling of face/tongue = EMERGENCY)
  • First-dose hypotension (give at bedtime)

NCLEX Trap: "Patient on lisinopril has K+ of 5.8. What's the nurse's FIRST action?"
Answer: Hold the medication and notify the provider. ACE inhibitors cause K+ retention.

2. Beta Blockers -olol

Examples: Metoprolol, Atenolol, Propranolol

Action: Block beta-1 receptors → ↓ HR, ↓ BP, ↓ myocardial O2 demand

Use: Hypertension, angina, arrhythmias, heart failure

Memory trick: "OL OL OL my heart slows down"

ADVERSE EFFECTS TO KNOW:

  • Bradycardia (HR <60 = hold, notify provider)
  • Hypotension
  • Bronchospasm (contraindicated in asthma/COPD — non-selective like propranolol)
  • Masks hypoglycemia symptoms (diabetic patients won't feel tremors/tachycardia)

NEVER STOP ABRUPTLY → Rebound hypertension, tachycardia, MI risk. Taper over 1-2 weeks.

3. Statins -statin

Examples: Atorvastatin, Simvastatin, Rosuvastatin

Action: Inhibit HMG-CoA reductase → ↓ cholesterol synthesis

Use: Hyperlipidemia, CAD prevention

ADVERSE EFFECTS TO KNOW:

  • Myopathy/rhabdomyolysis (muscle pain + dark urine = EMERGENCY)
  • Elevated liver enzymes (monitor LFTs)
  • Contraindicated in pregnancy

NCLEX Trap: "Patient on atorvastatin reports muscle weakness and dark urine."
Answer: Hold statin, check CK levels, assess for rhabdomyolysis.

Answer: Hold warfarin, assess for bleeding, notify provider. INR >4 = high bleed risk.

6. Opioids -one, -idine

Examples: Morphine, Fentanyl, Oxycodone, Meperidine

Action: Bind to opioid receptors → pain relief, respiratory depression, sedation

ADVERSE EFFECTS TO KNOW:

  • Respiratory depression (RR <12 = hold opioid, prepare naloxone)
  • Hypotension
  • Constipation (prophylactic stool softener)
  • Urinary retention
  • Itching (histamine release — not allergy)

Antidote: Naloxone (Narcan) — reverses respiratory depression, NOT for pain control

NCLEX Trap: "Patient received regular insulin at 0800. When is the nurse most likely to see hypoglycemia?"
Answer: 1000-1200 (2-4 hour peak).

8. Proton Pump Inhibitors -prazole

Examples: Omeprazole, Pantoprazole, Esomeprazole

Action: Block H+/K+ ATPase pump → ↓ gastric acid

Use: GERD, peptic ulcer, stress ulcer prophylaxis

ADVERSE EFFECTS TO KNOW:

  • ↓ B12 absorption (long-term use)
  • ↑ C. diff risk
  • ↓ Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺ absorption (bone fracture risk)
  • ↑ PPIs linked to kidney disease (monitor renal function)

9. Corticosteroids -sone, -olone

Examples: Prednisone, Methylprednisolone, Hydrocortisone

Action: Anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive

ADVERSE EFFECTS TO KNOW:

  • Hyperglycemia (monitor glucose)
  • Immunosuppression (infection risk)
  • Fluid retention
  • Osteoporosis (long-term)
  • Adrenal suppression (NEVER stop abruptly — taper!)

NCLEX Trap: "Patient has been on prednisone for 3 weeks and feels well. Can they stop today?"
Answer: NO. Taper over weeks to prevent adrenal crisis.

How the NGN Tests Pharmacology

The Next Generation NCLEX doesn't ask "What is the mechanism of action of metoprolol?" Instead, it gives you a scenario:

"A patient on multiple medications reports muscle pain and dark urine. Which medication is MOST likely causing this?"

To answer, you need to:

  1. Recognize the pattern (muscle pain + dark urine = rhabdomyolysis)
  2. Link it to the drug class (statins)
  3. Know the action (hold drug, check CK)
