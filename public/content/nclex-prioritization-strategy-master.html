NCLEX Prioritization Strategy: Who Do You See First?

Prioritization is the #1 skill tested on the NGN NCLEX. Master these frameworks, and you'll answer every "who do you see first" question with confidence.

By Nnamdi Okorafor, RN · 15-year ER nurse · August 2026

The truth: Prioritization isn't about memorizing rules. It's about recognizing patterns. Experienced nurses don't think "ABCDE" — they FEEL who's the sickest. But until you have that intuition, frameworks bridge the gap.

The Three Frameworks Every Nurse Uses

1. ABCDE (Emergency Prioritization)

  • A - Airway: Is the airway patent? Stridor? Gurgling? Obstruction?
  • B - Breathing: Rate, depth, effort. O2 sat. Use of accessory muscles?
  • C - Circulation: BP, HR, pulses, capillary refill. Signs of shock?
  • D - Disability: LOC, neuro status. GCS if needed.
  • E - Exposure: Temperature, skin changes, hidden injuries.

When to use: Multiple patients with acute changes. Emergency situations. Code scenarios.

2. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

  • Physiological: Oxygen, circulation, nutrition, elimination — FIRST
  • Safety: Fall risk, infection control, medication safety — SECOND
  • Love/Belonging: Family support, communication — THIRD
  • Self-Esteem: Dignity, independence — FOURTH
  • Self-Actualization: Goals, education — LAST

When to use: When comparing patients who are all relatively stable but have different needs.

3. Safety First (NCSBN Priority Framework)

  • First priority: Immediate threat to life (airway, breathing, circulation)
  • Second priority: Actual problems that could become threats (infection, bleeding, falls)
  • Third priority: Potential problems (risk for, prevention)

Real Scenarios With Answers

Scenario 1: 4 Patients, 1 Nurse

  • Patient A: Post-op day 1, BP 92/58, HR 112, anxious
  • Patient B: New admission, stable vitals, needs assessment
  • Patient C: Scheduled dressing change, wound healing well
  • Patient D: Requesting pain medication, rated 6/10

Answer: Patient A first. BP 92/58 + HR 112 = possible hypovolemic shock. This is a circulation problem (ABCDE: C). Even though the patient is "post-op day 1" and might seem routine, these vitals are abnormal and trending toward shock.

Scenario 2: Med-Surg Floor

  • Patient A: COPD, O2 sat 89% on 2L nasal cannula
  • Patient B: Diabetic, blood glucose 58 mg/dL, alert
  • Patient C: Post-op, requesting PRN pain med
  • Patient D: Needs discharge teaching

Answer: Patient B first. BG 58 is critically low. Patient is alert now but can deteriorate quickly into seizure or unconsciousness. Patient A has COPD with expected lower O2 sats — titrate O2, but not immediately life-threatening.

Scenario 3: ER Triage

  • Patient A: "I have a headache." Vitals stable, alert.
  • Patient B: Chest pain, diaphoretic, BP 180/110.
  • Patient C: Twisted ankle, swelling, no deformity.
  • Patient D: Abdominal pain, tender RLQ, fever 101.2°F.

Answer: Patient B first. Chest pain + diaphoresis + HTN = possible MI. This is ABC territory. Patient D might be appendicitis (concerning, but stable). Patient A and C are lowest priority.

The "Who Do You See First" Decision Tree

  1. Is anyone unstable? (ABCDE abnormal) → See them FIRST
  2. Is anyone in pain >7/10? → See them SECOND
  3. Is anyone with new/worsening symptoms? → See them THIRD
  4. Scheduled/routine tasks → Do AFTER urgent needs
