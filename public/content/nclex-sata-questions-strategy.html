NCLEX SATA Questions: The Strategy That Actually Works

Why SATA is the #1 feared question type — and the 4-step systematic approach that eliminates the fear.

If you're preparing for the Next Generation NCLEX, you've probably already encountered SATA questions — Select All That Apply. And if you're like most nursing students, they terrify you.

Here's the truth: SATA questions aren't harder. They're just different. And once you understand the systematic approach, they become your highest-scoring question type.

Why SATA Feels Impossible

The NCLEX SATA format gives you 4-6 options and asks you to select ALL that apply. No partial credit. Every correct option must be selected, and every incorrect option must be left unselected.

The psychological trap:

  • You see Option A and think "maybe..."
  • You see Option B and think "that could be right..."
  • By Option C, you're second-guessing everything
  • You end up selecting 5 out of 6 options "just to be safe"

This is exactly what the NCLEX wants you to do. SATA questions test your ability to discriminate — to know with certainty what's correct and what's not.

The 4-Step SATA Strategy

Step 1: Read the Question Twice

Not the options. The question stem. What is the question REALLY asking?

  • Is it asking for priority actions? (What do you do FIRST?)
  • Is it asking for appropriate interventions? (What is correct for this situation?)
  • Is it asking you to identify something? (Which findings are expected?)

The question stem determines how you evaluate each option. Don't skip this step.

Step 2: Evaluate Each Option INDEPENDENTLY

This is the key. Treat each option as a True/False question.

Don't look at Option B while evaluating Option A. Don't think "if A is right, then B must be too."

Each option stands alone.

Step 3: Ask "Is This ALWAYS True?"

For each option, ask: "In the context of this question, is this statement always correct?"

If there's ANY scenario where the option would be wrong, don't select it.

Step 4: Verify Your Selections

Before submitting, review each selected option one more time. Ask:

  • "Am I 100% confident this is correct?"
  • "Did I select this because I KNOW it's right, or because I'm afraid to leave it out?"

5 Practice SATA Questions

Question 1

A nurse is caring for a client with heart failure. Which findings indicate the client is experiencing fluid volume overload? Select all that apply.

  • A. Weight gain of 2 kg in 24 hours
  • B. Decreased urine output
  • C. Crackles in lung bases
  • D. Flat neck veins
  • E. Peripheral edema
  • F. Bounding pulse
Correct: A, B, C, E
A: Weight gain of 2kg (4.4 lbs) in 24h = fluid retention. Correct.
B: Decreased UOP = kidneys compensating for overload. Correct.
C: Crackles = fluid in alveoli. Correct.
D: Flat neck veins = hypovolemia, not overload. Incorrect.
E: Peripheral edema = classic sign. Correct.
F: Bounding pulse is possible but not specific to overload (could be fever, anxiety). Not ALWAYS true.

Question 2

A nurse is preparing a client for discharge after thyroidectomy. Which instructions should the nurse include? Select all that apply.

  • A. Report tingling in fingers immediately
  • B. Take calcium supplements as prescribed
  • C. Avoid strenuous activity for 1 week
  • D. Use a soft toothbrush
  • E. Monitor for signs of infection at incision site
Correct: A, B, C, E
A: Tingling = hypocalcemia from parathyroid damage. Emergency. Correct.
B: Calcium prevents hypocalcemia. Correct.
C: Activity restriction prevents bleeding. Correct.
D: Soft toothbrush is for thrombocytopenia/neutropenia, not standard post-thyroidectomy. Incorrect.
E: Infection monitoring = standard discharge instruction. Correct.

Question 3

Which actions by a nurse demonstrate appropriate delegation to a UAP? Select all that apply.

  • A. Asking the UAP to obtain a blood glucose reading
  • B. Instructing the UAP to ambulate a post-op client
  • C. Having the UAP document intake and output
  • D. Directing the UAP to administer a PRN acetaminophen
  • E. Requesting the UAP to obtain vital signs on a stable client
Correct: A, B, C, E
A: Point-of-care glucose = UAP scope. Correct.
B: Ambulation = UAP scope (RN assesses first). Correct.
C: I&O documentation = UAP scope. Correct.
D: Medication administration = NEVER delegated to UAP. Incorrect.
E: Vital signs on stable client = UAP scope. Correct.

Question 4

A client with COPD is receiving oxygen at 2 L/min via nasal cannula. Which findings indicate the need for immediate intervention? Select all that apply.

  • A. Oxygen saturation of 88%
  • B. Respiratory rate of 24
  • C. Client reports increased shortness of breath
  • D. Client is drowsy and difficult to arouse
  • E. Clubbing of fingers noted
Correct: A, C, D
A: SpO2 88% on O2 = worsening hypoxemia. Immediate. Correct.
B: RR 24 in COPD = baseline elevated, not emergent alone. Incorrect.
C: Increased SOB = acute deterioration. Correct.
D: Drowsy/difficult to arouse = CO2 narcosis (respiratory failure). EMERGENCY. Correct.
E: Clubbing = chronic finding, not acute intervention. Incorrect.

Question 5

Which nursing interventions are appropriate for a client with a nasogastric tube? Select all that apply.

  • A. Check tube placement before each feeding
  • B. Elevate the head of bed to 30 degrees during feeding
  • C. Flush the tube with 30 mL water every 4 hours
  • D. Assess for gastric residual every 8 hours
  • E. Secure the tube to the client's gown
Correct: A, C, D
A: Placement verification = safety priority. Correct.
B: HOB should be 30-45°, not just 30°. "30 degrees" is the minimum but not ideal. Borderline — NCLEX wants 30-45°. Incorrect.
C: Flushing maintains patency. Correct.
D: Gastric residual assessment = standard. Correct.
E: NG tube should be secured to NOSE/CHEEK, not gown (prevents tension/displacement). Incorrect.

The Mindset Shift

SATA questions aren't testing your memorization. They're testing your clinical judgment — your ability to discriminate between what's always true, sometimes true, and never true in a given context.
