💊 Digoxin (Lanoxin)

Therapeutic: 0.5-2.0 ng/mL | Toxic: >2.5 ng/mL

🔥 Toxicity Signs — Mnemonic: DIG Toxicity

  • D — Diarrhea, Disturbed color vision (yellow/green halos)
  • I — Irritability, Insomnia
  • G — GI upset (nausea, vomiting, anorexia)

Cardiac Manifestations

  • Any bradyarrhythmia or tachyarrhythmia
  • AV blocks, PVCs, bigeminy
  • "Regularization" of AF (warning sign!)

⚠️ Before Giving Digoxin:

  1. Check apical pulse for 1 full minute
  2. Hold if HR <60 (adult) or <70 (child) or <90 (infant)
  3. Check K⁺ — hypokalemia potentiates toxicity
  4. Check renal function (CrCl)

Antidote

Digoxin immune Fab (Digibind): Binds digoxin, removes from circulation. Dose based on ingested amount or serum level.

🧠 Lithium (Eskalith)

Therapeutic: 0.6-1.2 mEq/L | Toxic: >1.5 mEq/L

🔥 Toxicity Progression

Level Symptoms
1.5-2.0 (Mild) Tremor, nausea, diarrhea, polyuria, thirst
2.0-2.5 (Moderate) Confusion, ataxia, drowsiness, severe tremor
>2.5 (Severe) Seizures, coma, renal failure, death

💡 Mnemonic: LITHIUM

  • L — Lethargy, Loss of coordination
  • I — Increased urination (nephrogenic DI)
  • T — Tremor (fine hand tremor is early sign)
  • H — Hypothyroidism (chronic use)
  • I — Intoxication (toxic levels)
  • U — Urine output changes
  • M — Mental changes

Nursing Priorities

  • Fluid balance: Monitor I&O, daily weights
  • Na⁺ balance: Low sodium → lithium retention → toxicity
  • Never diurese without monitoring lithium levels
  • Ensure adequate hydration (2.5-3L/day)

⚡ Anticonvulsants

Phenytoin (Dilantin)

Therapeutic: 10-20 mcg/mL | Toxic: >30 mcg/mL

  • Subtherapeutic: Increased seizure frequency
  • Toxic: Nystagmus (early sign), ataxia, slurred speech, confusion
  • Chronic: Gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, folate deficiency

⚠️ IV Administration: Give SLOWLY (max 50 mg/min). Cardiac monitoring required. Can cause hypotension, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest if pushed fast.

Valproic Acid (Depakote)

Therapeutic: 50-100 mcg/mL | Toxic: >120 mcg/mL

  • Toxicity: Sedation, tremor, thrombocytopenia, hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis
  • Monitor: LFTs, platelets, ammonia level
  • Black box: Hepatotoxicity, fetal neural tube defects

Carbamazepine (Tegretol)

Therapeutic: 4-12 mcg/mL | Toxic: >15 mcg/mL

  • Toxicity: Diplopia, dizziness, nausea, ataxia, hyponatremia (SIADH)
  • Serious: Agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, SJS/TEN
  • Monitor: CBC, LFTs, Na⁺, skin reactions

🦠 Antibiotics: Peak & Trough

Vancomycin

Trough: 15-20 mcg/mL (serious infections) | Toxic trough: >30

  • "Red man syndrome" — histamine release from rapid infusion (NOT allergy!)
  • Nephrotoxicity — monitor creatinine, urine output
  • Ototoxicity — monitor hearing, tinnitus
  • Infuse over ≥1 hour (1g over 60 min minimum)

Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin, Tobramycin)

Gentamicin Tobramycin
Peak 5-10 mcg/mL 5-10 mcg/mL
Trough <2 mcg/mL <2 mcg/mL
  • Nephrotoxic — monitor Cr, I&O
  • Ototoxic — irreversible! Monitor hearing
  • Neuromuscular blockade — avoid with other NMB agents

🩸 Anticoagulants

Warfarin (Coumadin) — INR

Indication Target INR
DVT/PE prophylaxis 2.0-3.0
Mechanical heart valve 2.5-3.5
Atrial fibrillation 2.0-3.0

⚠️ INR >4.0 = BLEEDING RISK

  • Hold warfarin
  • Vitamin K (phytonadione) PO/SC/IV if actively bleeding
  • FFP if major bleeding

Vitamin K Food Interaction

foods DECREASE warfarin effect (need consistent intake, not avoidance):

  • Leafy greens (kale, spinach, collards)
  • Broccoli, Brussels sprouts
  • Green tea
  • Liver

Heparin — PTT

Therapeutic PTT: 1.5-2.5 × control (usually 60-80 seconds)

  • Subtherapeutic: <1.5 × control → increased clot risk
  • Supratherapeutic: >2.5 × control → bleeding risk
  • Antidote: Protamine sulfate (1 mg per 100 units heparin)

⚠️ Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT): Platelets drop >50% from baseline 5-10 days after starting. STOP heparin immediately. Use argatroban or fondaparinux.

🩺 NGN Case Study: Drug Toxicity

Patient Presentation

Mr. Rodriguez, 72 y/o, is admitted with confusion and nausea. He has AFib and takes digoxin 0.25 mg daily. He started HCTZ 3 days ago for edema.

  • VS: BP 110/70, HR 48 (irregular), RR 16, Temp 37.1°C
  • Digoxin level: 3.2 ng/mL
  • K⁺: 3.1 mEq/L | Cr: 1.8 mg/dL
  • EKG: AFib with frequent PVCs, bigeminy

What is the nurse's priority action?

✓ Correct: C

Rationale: Mr. Rodriguez has digoxin toxicity (level 3.2, therapeutic is 0.5-2.0). The classic triad is present: GI symptoms (nausea), cardiac manifestations (bradycardia, PVCs, bigeminy), and neuro changes (confusion). The new HCTZ caused hypokalemia, which potentiates digoxin toxicity. The nurse must hold the digoxin and notify the provider immediately. Potassium may be needed but only after provider order.
