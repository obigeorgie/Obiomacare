Toddler (1-2 y) 80-130 20-30 90-110 / 55-75 36.5-37.5
Preschool (3-5 y) 80-120 20-25 95-110 / 60-75 36.5-37.5
School age (6-12 y) 70-110 18-25 100-120 / 60-75 36.5-37.5
Adolescent (13+ y) 60-100 12-20 <120 / <80 36.5-37.5
Adult 60-100 12-20 <120 / <80 36.5-37.5
Older Adult (>65) 60-100 12-20 <130 / <80 36.0-37.0 (lower)

Temperature

Normal: 36.5-37.5°C (97.7-99.5°F)

Classification Celsius Fahrenheit
Hypothermia <35.0°C <95.0°F
Normal 36.5-37.5°C 97.7-99.5°F
Low-grade fever 37.5-38.3°C 99.5-100.9°F
Fever 38.3-39.0°C 100.9-102.2°F
High fever 39.0-40.0°C 102.2-104.0°F
Hyperpyrexia >40.0°C >104.0°F

Sites & Normal Variations

  • Oral: 0.3-0.5°C lower than core
  • Rectal: 0.3-0.5°C higher than oral (most accurate for infants)
  • Tympanic: Reflects core temperature
  • Axillary: 0.5-1.0°C lower than oral (least accurate)
  • Temporal: Close to core, convenient

Remember: Rectal is highest, Axillary is lowest. Think "Rise up, Fall down."

Pulse & Heart Rate

Tachycardia (>100 bpm in adults)

Causes — Mnemonic: TACHYCARDIA

  • T — Temperature (fever), Thyrotoxicosis
  • A — Anxiety, Anemia
  • C — Caffeine, Cocaine
  • H — Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, Hypotension
  • Y — Young (children normally higher)
  • C — Cardiac (heart failure, MI)
  • A — Activity, Acute pain
  • R — Respiratory (PE, pneumonia)
  • D — Dehydration, Drugs (atropine, epinephrine)
  • I — Infection, sepsis
  • A — Arrhythmias

Bradycardia (<60 bpm in adults)

Causes — Mnemonic: BRADY

  • B — Beta blockers, Bedrest
  • R — Rest, Relaxation (athletes)
  • A — Arrhythmias (heart block, sinus node dysfunction)
  • D — Digoxin toxicity
  • Y — Yellow (jaundice/↑ ICP — Cushing's triad)

Critical: Bradycardia + hypertension + irregular respirations = Cushing's Triad (↑ intracranial pressure). Emergency!

Pulse Quality

Quality Description Clinical Significance
Bounding Strong, forceful Hypertension, fever, anemia, aortic regurgitation
Thready Weak, difficult to feel Shock, hemorrhage, heart failure
Irregular Variable rhythm Arrhythmia (AFib, PVCs, heart block)

Respiration & SpO₂

Normal: 12-20 breaths/min (adult)

Respiratory Patterns

Pattern Description Cause
Bradypnea <12/min Opioids, ↑ ICP, metabolic alkalosis
Tachypnea >20/min Hypoxia, anxiety, metabolic acidosis, PE
Cheyne-Stokes Crescendo-decrescendo with apnea Heart failure, stroke, ↑ ICP, end-of-life
Kussmaul Deep, rapid Metabolic acidosis (DKA, renal failure)
Biot's Irregular with random apnea ↑ ICP, meningitis (ominous sign)
Apneustic Prolonged inspiration Brainstem lesion

Pulse Oximetry (SpO₂)

SpO₂ Interpretation Action
95-100% Normal Continue monitoring
90-94% Mild hypoxemia Assess, consider O₂
85-89% Moderate hypoxemia O₂ required, notify provider
<85% Severe hypoxemia Emergency! High-flow O₂, call Rapid Response

Important: SpO₂ can be falsely elevated in carbon monoxide poisoning (pulse ox cannot distinguish O₂ from CO). If CO poisoning suspected, use co-oximetry (ABG).

Blood Pressure

Hypertension Categories (JNC 8)

Category Systolic Diastolic
Normal <120 <80
Elevated 120-129 <80
Stage 1 HTN 130-139 80-89
Stage 2 HTN ≥140 ≥90
Hypertensive Crisis >180 >120

Hypotension

Generally <90/60 mmHg, but interpret in context:

  • Baseline BP 150/90 → 110/70 is relatively hypotensive
  • Accompanied by symptoms (dizziness, confusion, cool skin) = shock until proven otherwise

Pulse Pressure

Systolic - Diastolic = Pulse Pressure

  • Narrow (<30): Shock, hemorrhage, cardiac tamponade, aortic stenosis
  • Wide (>60): Aortic regurgitation, arteriosclerosis, thyrotoxicosis

Orthostatic (Postural) Vitals

How to Measure

  1. Measure BP and HR after 5 minutes supine
  2. Measure again within 1-3 minutes of standing
  3. Positive orthostatics = EITHER:
  • ↓ Systolic ≥20 mmHg
  • ↓ Diastolic ≥10 mmHg
  • ↑ HR ≥20 bpm

Causes

  • Hypovolemia (dehydration, hemorrhage)
  • Prolonged bedrest
  • Medications (diuretics, antihypertensives, vasodilators)
  • Autonomic dysfunction (diabetes, Parkinson's)

Safety: If orthostatic hypotension suspected, do NOT let patient stand alone. Dizziness → fall risk. Have them sit first, then stand with assistance.

(in pain) Pain Assessment

Pain is the 5th vital sign. Always assess:

  • P — Provocation/Palliation (what makes it better/worse?)
  • Q — Quality (sharp, dull, burning, crushing)
  • R — Region/Radiation (where does it go?)
  • S — Severity (0-10 scale)
  • T — Timing (onset, duration, frequency)

Special Populations

  • Pediatrics: FLACC scale (Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability)
  • Dementia/Non-verbal: PAINAD scale
  • Infants: CRIES scale

NGN Case Study: Vital Signs Emergency

Patient Presentation

Mr. Johnson, 78 y/o, is recovering from pneumonia. The nurse notes the following change from baseline:

Baseline Current
Temp 37.0°C 38.9°C
HR 76 112
RR 18 28
BP 134/82 96/58
SpO₂ 94% on 2L 89% on 2L

Additional: Confused, skin cool and clammy. WBC 16,500.

What is the nurse's priority action?

Correct: C

Rationale: Mr. Johnson meets criteria for septic shock:

  • Suspected infection (pneumonia, WBC 16,500)
  • Fever >38°C
  • Tachycardia (112)
  • Tachypnea (28)
  • Hypotension (96/58)
  • Altered mental status (confusion)

This is a medical emergency requiring immediate provider notification and sepsis protocol (fluids, blood cultures, antibiotics within 1 hour).
