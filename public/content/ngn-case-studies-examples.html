NGN Case Studies: Real NCLEX Examples with Walkthroughs

See exactly how Next Generation NCLEX case studies work. Walk through real patient scenarios with detailed rationales for every question.

By Nnamdi Okorafor, RN · 15-year ER nurse · Updated July 2026 · Obioma

What makes NGN case studies different: Instead of standalone questions, you get a patient scenario followed by 4-6 questions that test different cognitive skills. The questions build on each other. Your answer to question 1 affects how you think about question 2. This mirrors real nursing — you don't get one isolated problem, you get a patient who changes over time.

Case Study 1: Acute Myocardial Infarction

🫀 Patient Scenario

J.R., 67-year-old male, presents to the ER with crushing chest pain radiating to his left arm. He is diaphoretic and anxious.

Vital Signs:
BP: 88/52 mmHg | HR: 118 bpm (irregular)
RR: 24/min | SpO2: 89% on room air
Temp: 98.6°F (37°C) | Pain: 9/10

History: Hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoker (1 pack/day x 40 years). Father died of MI at age 62.

Medications: Lisinopril 10mg daily, Atorvastatin 20mg daily

Lab Results: Troponin I: 2.8 ng/mL (normal < 0.04), CK-MB: 12 IU/L (normal < 6)

Question 1 (Recognize Cues): Which findings are consistent with an acute myocardial infarction? (Select all that apply.)
Answer & Rationale:
✓ Chest pain radiating to left arm — Classic MI presentation
✓ Diaphoresis — Sympathetic response to cardiac ischemia
✓ BP 88/52 — Cardiogenic shock from decreased cardiac output
✓ HR 118 (irregular) — Possible ventricular dysrhythmia
✓ SpO2 89% — Hypoxemia from pulmonary edema
✓ Troponin 2.8 — Elevated cardiac marker confirms myocardial damage
✗ Temp 98.6 — Normal, not relevant to MI diagnosis
Question 2 (Prioritize Hypotheses): What is the nurse's priority action?
Answer: Apply supplemental oxygen and prepare for STEMI protocol.

Why: While all options may be appropriate, the ABC rule applies. SpO2 89% indicates hypoxemia. Oxygen FIRST, then morphine, then nitroglycerin (after checking BP), then aspirin. The order matters.
Question 3 (Take Action): Which medication should the nurse administer FIRST?
Answer: Aspirin 325 mg chewable.

Why: In STEMI protocol, aspirin is given first (if not already taken) because it reduces mortality. It's also an independent nursing action — no provider order needed in most protocols. Nitroglycerin requires a provider order and BP must be > 90 systolic. Morphine is for pain relief but doesn't reduce mortality.

Case Study 2: COPD Exacerbation

🫁 Patient Scenario

M.S., 72-year-old female, arrives via ambulance with severe dyspnea. She has a history of COPD (GOLD Stage 3) and is on home oxygen 2L NC.

Vital Signs:
BP: 162/94 mmHg | HR: 110 bpm
RR: 32/min | SpO2: 82% on 2L NC
Temp: 99.8°F (37.7°C) | Alert but anxious

Physical Assessment: Barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing, tripod position. Diminished breath sounds bilaterally with prolonged expiratory phase. Scattered wheezes. JVD +2.

Current Meds: Albuterol inhaler PRN, Fluticasone/Salmeterol BID, home O2 2L

Question 1 (Analyze Cues): What is the significance of the patient's SpO2 of 82% on her usual home oxygen?
Answer: This indicates acute respiratory failure. Her baseline on 2L NC is likely 88-90%. A drop to 82% on the same oxygen flow means her condition has worsened significantly. This is not just "her COPD" — this is an acute exacerbation requiring immediate intervention.
Question 2 (Generate Solutions): Which actions should the nurse take? (Select all that apply.)
Answer:
✓ Increase O2 to 4-6L NC and titrate to SpO2 88-92%
✓ Prepare for possible BiPAP or intubation
✓ Administer albuterol nebulizer
✓ Draw ABG
✗ Sedate the patient to reduce anxiety — WRONG. Sedation depresses respiratory drive in COPD patients. Use reassurance and positioning instead.

How to Practice NGN Case Studies

  1. Read the entire scenario first. Don't jump to questions. Understand the patient's baseline, current status, and context.
  2. Identify the CJMM skill being tested. Is it Recognize, Analyze, Prioritize, Generate, Take Action, or Evaluate?
  3. Answer based on the scenario, not memorization. The correct answer depends on THIS patient's specific data.
  4. Read every rationale. Even for questions you get right. Understanding why wrong answers are wrong is as important as knowing the right answer.

From the ER: These cases aren't just exam prep — they're how real patients present. The 67-year-old with chest pain? I've seen him a hundred times. The COPD exacerbation? Probably twice this week. The NCLEX isn't testing textbook knowledge. It's testing whether you can think like a nurse. That's what case studies train.
