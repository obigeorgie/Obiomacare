SATA Questions: The Strategy That Actually Works

Published July 24, 2026 · 4 min read · Test Strategies

SATA questions aren't trying to trick you. You're just reading them wrong.

Select All That Apply (SATA) questions are the most feared format on NCLEX. But here's the truth: they're actually the easiest points once you know the method.

The #1 Mistake

Students try to find ALL the right answers at once. They look for patterns. They think "these 3 look related, so I'll pick them."

Wrong approach. This is how you fail SATA.

The True/False Method

Step 1: Treat EACH Option as True/False

Don't look at all options together. Look at A alone. Is it true or false? Then B alone. Then C.

Each option is a separate true/false question. Period.

Step 2: If You're 50/50, Pick the SAFEST

NCLEX is a safety exam. When you can't decide, ask:

  • "Which option keeps my patient alive?"
  • "Which is the least harmful if I'm wrong?"

Step 3: Don't Overthink

Spending 5 minutes on one SATA question means borrowing time from 4 other questions.

Rule: If you can't decide in 90 seconds, pick your best guess and move on.

Real Example

SATA Question

A nurse is caring for a patient with heart failure. Select all that apply:

Which nursing interventions are appropriate?

  • A. Weigh the patient daily
  • B. Restrict fluid intake
  • C. Administer digoxin without checking pulse
  • D. Elevate head of bed to 30 degrees
  • E. Monitor potassium levels

Using the True/False Method:

  • A: True — daily weights track fluid retention in CHF
  • B: True — fluid restriction reduces cardiac workload
  • C: FALSE — NEVER give digoxin without checking apical pulse. Safety violation.
  • D: True — elevating HOB improves breathing
  • E: True — diuretics cause potassium loss, must monitor

Answer: A, B, D, E

Notice how C is obviously wrong once you evaluate it alone. The "pattern matching" approach might have made you second-guess.

When Students Get It Wrong

"I was treating SATA like a puzzle. I'd try to find the 'right combination.' Once I started treating each option as true/false, my SATA score went from 40% to 85%."
— Maria K., NCLEX-PN

The Safety Rule

When you're truly stuck on one option, remember:

NCLEX prioritizes patient safety above all else.

If one option is "call the physician" and another is "assess the patient first" — assess first. Unless the patient is actively dying, assessment comes before action.

Practice This Until It's Automatic

Do 20 SATA questions using this method. Force yourself to say "true" or "false" for each option out loud.

After 50 questions, you won't even think about it. It becomes muscle memory.
