🫁 NCLEX ABG Interpretation Quick Reference

4-step method + common disorders + practice cues

🎯 The 4-Step Method

1Look at the pH

Normal: 7.35-7.45

<7.35 = Acidosis | >7.45 = Alkalosis

2Look at PaCO₂ (respiratory component)

Normal: 35-45 mmHg

>45 = Respiratory acidosis (retaining CO₂)

<35 = Respiratory alkalosis (blowing off CO₂)

3Look at HCO₃⁻ (metabolic component)

Normal: 22-26 mEq/L

<22 = Metabolic acidosis (losing bicarb)

>26 = Metabolic alkalosis (retaining bicarb)

4Is there compensation?

Uncompensated: One value abnormal, pH abnormal

Partially compensated: Both values abnormal, pH still abnormal

Fully compensated: Both values abnormal, pH normal (7.35-7.45)

Trap: The body never overcompensates. If pH is acidotic, the PRIMARY problem is acidosis. The compensating system moves in the SAME direction as the primary problem.

⚡ Rapid Fire Questions

Q: ABG: pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 55, HCO₃⁻ 24. What's the disorder?
A: Respiratory acidosis (uncompensated). pH acidotic, PaCO₂ high (respiratory cause), HCO₃⁻ normal.
Q: ABG: pH 7.50, PaCO₂ 30, HCO₃⁻ 24. What's the disorder?
A: Respiratory alkalosis (uncompensated). pH alkalotic, PaCO₂ low (respiratory cause), HCO₃⁻ normal.
Q: ABG: pH 7.32, PaCO₂ 38, HCO₃⁻ 18. What's the disorder?
A: Metabolic acidosis (uncompensated). pH acidotic, HCO₃⁻ low (metabolic cause), PaCO₂ normal.
Q: ABG: pH 7.40, PaCO₂ 52, HCO₃⁻ 32. What's the disorder?
A: Respiratory acidosis with metabolic compensation (fully compensated). pH normal but on acidotic side, both values abnormal in same direction.
