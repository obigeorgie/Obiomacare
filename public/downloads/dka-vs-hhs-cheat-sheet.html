🔥 DKA vs HHS Quick Reference

Obioma Care | NCLEX Study Guide | obiomacare.com

🚨 DKA — Diabetic Ketoacidosis

  • Usually Type 1 DM
  • Onset: Hours to days
  • Glucose: >250 mg/dL
  • pH: <7.30
  • HCO₃⁻: <18 mEq/L
  • Anion gap: Elevated (>12)
  • Ketones: Positive (serum/urine)
  • Osmolality: <320 mOsm/kg
  • Mental status: Alert → stupor
  • Kussmaul respirations: Present
  • "Fruity" breath: Present

💧 HHS — Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State

  • Usually Type 2 DM
  • Onset: Days to weeks
  • Glucose: >600 mg/dL
  • pH: >7.30 (normal)
  • HCO₃⁻: >18 mEq/L
  • Anion gap: Normal
  • Ketones: Minimal/absent
  • Osmolality: >320 mOsm/kg
  • Mental status: Altered (hallmark)
  • Kussmaul respirations: Absent
  • "Fruity" breath: Absent

⚡ Emergency Management — BOTH

1. Fluids0.9% NS 1L/hr → switch to 0.45% when glucose ~2500.9% NS 1L/hr → continue until glucose 250-300

4. DextroseAdd D5 when glucose 200-250 (continue insulin!)Add D5 when glucose 250-300

⚠️ CRITICAL: Fluids BEFORE Insulin
Insulin before fluids can cause cardiovascular collapse in hypovolemic patients. Always resuscitate first!
💡 Memory Trick: DKA = ACID, HHS = OSMOLALITY
DKA = Acidosis (ketones, low pH) | HHS = Hyperosmolar (very high glucose, altered mental status, no acidosis)

📋 Resolution Criteria

  • DKA resolved: Glucose <200, pH >7.30, HCO₃⁻ >18, anion gap closed
  • HHS resolved: Osmolality <315, glucose 250-300, mental status improved
  • Transition to SC insulin ONLY when patient is eating and labs normalized
