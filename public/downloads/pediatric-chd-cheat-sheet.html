👶 Pediatric CHD: Cyanotic vs Acyanotic Defects

Obioma | NCLEX Study Guide | obiomacare.com
🔵 Cyanosis = ≥5 g/dL deoxygenated hemoglobin
Cyanotic lesions = Right-to-left shunt or decreased pulmonary blood flow

🔴 Cyanotic Defects (5 T's)

DefectKey Features
Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)Most common cyanotic CHD. VSD + pulmonary stenosis + overriding aorta + RVH. Squatting relieves cyanosis. Single S2.
Transposition of Great Vessels (TGV)Aorta from RV, PA from LV. Parallel circuits. NEED PDA/ASD to survive. CXR = "egg on a string."
Truncus ArteriosusSingle great vessel from heart. Always VSD present. Early CHF, not just cyanosis.
Tricuspid AtresiaNo tricuspid valve. RA cannot empty into RV. Needs ASD + VSD or PDA to survive.
Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Return (TAPVR)Pulmonary veins drain to RA (not LA). Obstructed type = emergency. CXR = "snowman" (supracardiac).

🟢 Acyanotic Defects (Left-to-Right Shunts)

DefectKey Features
ASD (Atrial Septal Defect)Fixed split S2. Usually asymptomatic in childhood. Murmur at LUSB. May close spontaneously.
VSD (Ventricular Septal Defect)Most common CHD. Harsh holosystolic murmur at LLSB. Small VSD may close; large causes CHF.
PDA (Patent Ductus Arteriosus)Continuous "machinery" murmur at LUSB. Wide pulse pressure. Prematurity = risk factor. Indomethacin to close.

🔧 Ductal-Dependent Lesions — PGE1 Required

Dependent on PDA for...Defects
Pulmonary blood flowPulmonary atresia, Tetralogy of Fallot, Tricuspid atresia, Critical pulmonary stenosis
Systemic blood flowHypoplastic left heart, Coarctation of aorta, Interrupted aortic arch, Critical aortic stenosis

PGE1 (Alprostadil): 0.01-0.1 mcg/kg/min. Keeps PDA open. Side effects: Apnea, hypotension, fever, flushing. Have intubation equipment ready!

💡 NCLEX Memory Trick: "5 T's = Cyanotic"
Tetralogy, Transposition, Truncus, Tricuspid atresia, TAPVR

"Please Don't Be Late" = Left-to-Right (acyanotic)
PDA, Ductal (not a shunt but related), ASD, VSD — all cause L→R shunt initially

🚨 Post-Op Care After Cardiac Surgery

  • • Monitor for cardiac tamponade (Beck's triad: hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds)
  • • Watch for chylothorax (milky chest tube drainage after coarctation repair)
  • • Endocarditis prophylaxis for dental/surgical procedures (6 months post-op or lifelong if residual defect)
  • • Monitor for arrhythmias — especially after ASD/VSD closure
  • • Pain control — morphine/fentanyl. Avoid NSAIDs (bleeding risk, renal effects)
🍼 Feeding in CHD: Infants with CHD tire easily → small, frequent, high-calorie feeds. May need NG supplementation. Monitor weight gain closely.
