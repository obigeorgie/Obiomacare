🤰 Preeclampsia Severe Features + Magnesium Sulfate

Obioma Care | NCLEX Study Guide | obiomacare.com
⚠️ Preeclampsia = New-onset HTN + Proteinuria after 20 weeks (or HTN + end-organ dysfunction)
Severe features = delivery indicated regardless of gestational age

🔴 Severe Features — Any ONE = Severe

  • • SBP ≥160 or DBP ≥110 (×2, 4 hours apart)
  • • Platelets <100,000
  • • Creatinine >1.1 or doubling
  • • AST/ALT >2× normal
  • • Pulmonary edema
  • • New cerebral/visual disturbances
  • • Epigastric/RUQ pain

🟡 Mild Preeclampsia

  • • BP ≥140/90 (×2, 4-6 hours apart)
  • • Proteinuria ≥1+ on dipstick or ≥300 mg/24h
  • • After 20 weeks gestation
  • • No severe features
  • • Management: Monitor, antihypertensives, deliver at 37 weeks

📋 HELLP Syndrome (Severe Variant)

H — Hemolysis
Schistocytes on smear, LDH ↑, indirect bilirubin ↑, low haptoglobin
EL — Elevated Liver enzymes
AST/ALT >2× upper limit of normal
LP — Low Platelets
<100,000. Can occur without HTN or proteinuria!
🚨 Eclampsia = Seizure in preeclamptic patient NOT explained by other causes
Magnesium sulfate = anticonvulsant of choice. Reduces seizure risk by ~50%.

💉 Magnesium Sulfate Protocol

PhaseDoseDuration
Loading4-6 g IV over 20-30 minOne-time
Maintenance1-2 g/hr continuous IV infusionDuring labor + 24 hours postpartum

Alternative (IM): 10 g IM (5 g in each buttock) + 5 g IM q4h. IV preferred — less painful, more reliable levels.

✅ Magnesium Monitoring — "R-U-M"

R — Reflexes: Patellar reflexes must be present (1+ or 2+)
STOP if reflexes absent (sign of impending toxicity)
U — Urine output: Must be ≥30 mL/hr
Magnesium is renally excreted. Low UOP = accumulation risk. STOP if <30 mL/hr.
M — Respirations: Must be ≥12/min
STOP if RR <12 (sign of respiratory depression from Mg toxicity)

🔬 Magnesium Toxicity Levels & Antidote

Serum Mg LevelEffects
4-7 mEq/LTherapeutic (loss of DTRs at upper end)
8-10 mEq/LFlushing, warmth, nausea, headache, DTRs absent
10-12 mEq/LRespiratory depression, sedation
>12 mEq/LRespiratory arrest, cardiac arrest
💊 ANTIDOTE: Calcium gluconate 1 g IV (10 mL of 10% solution) over 3 min
Keep at bedside during MgSO₄ infusion. Reverses Mg-induced respiratory/cardiac depression.

🩺 Antihypertensives in Preeclampsia

DrugDoseNotes
Labetalol20 mg IV, then 20-80 mg q10 min (max 300 mg)First-line. Avoid in asthma (beta-blocker).
Hydralazine5-10 mg IV q15-20 minSecond-line. Tachycardia, headache common.
Nifedipine10 mg PO, repeat in 20 min if neededPO option. Avoid sublingual (rapid BP drop).

Goal BP: 140-150/90-100 (avoid hypotension → uteroplacental hypoperfusion)

💡 NCLEX Memory Trick: "DELIVER = Definitive Treatment"
For severe preeclampsia → deliver regardless of gestational age
For mild preeclampsia → deliver at 37 weeks
For eclampsia → stabilize with MgSO₄, control BP, then deliver

📊 Postpartum Monitoring (Critical!)

Preeclampsia can worsen up to 6 weeks postpartum
Continue BP monitoring, magnesium if severe features, seizure precautions.
Discharge teaching: Red flags
Severe headache, visual changes, RUQ pain, sudden weight gain, decreased fetal movement, SOB/swelling.
